The [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]] is a chart that ranks the best-performing songs of the United States. Its data, published by ''[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]'' magazine and compiled by [[Nielsen SoundScan]], is based collectively on each single's weekly [[CD single|physical]] and [[Digital distribution|digital]] sales, as well as [[Heavy rotation|airplay]]. In 2011, 14 singles claimed the top spot in 53 issues of the magazine, one of which, singer [[Katy Perry]]'s "[[Firework (song)|Firework]]" started its peak position in late 2010.<ref name="Pietroluongo">{{Cite magazine|url=http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/949557/katy-perrys-firework-shines-over-hot-100|title=Katy Perry's 'Firework' Shines Over Hot 100|magazine=Billboard|publisher=Prometheus Global Media|first=Silvio|last=Pietroluongo|date=2010-12-08|access-date=2010-12-08|archive-date=2013-02-12|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130212162311/http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/949557/katy-perrys-firework-shines-over-hot-100|url-status=live}}</ref>

In 2011, nine acts achieved their first US number-one single, either as a lead artist or a featured guest: [[Wiz Khalifa]], [[Adele]], [[Pitbull (entertainer)|Pitbull]], [[Afrojack]], [[Nayer]], [[LMFAO (group)|LMFAO]], [[Lauren Bennett]], [[GoonRock]] and [[Calvin Harris]]. Six collaboration singles topped the chart. Pop singers Adele, [[Britney Spears]], Katy Perry and [[Rihanna]] each earned two number-one songs during the year.{{ref|Bill|[a]}} 
One of Adele's songs, "[[Rolling in the Deep]]", was the best-performing single of 2011, topping the [[Billboard Year-End Hot 100 singles of 2011|''Billboard'' Year-End Hot 100]].<ref name="adele">{{Cite magazine|url=http://www.billboard.com/charts/year-end/2011/hot-100-songs|title=Hot 100 Songs Music Chart|magazine=Billboard|publisher=Prometheus Global Media|access-date=2012-02-03|archive-date=2014-02-11|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140211095431/http://www.billboard.com/charts/year-end/2011/hot-100-songs|url-status=live}}</ref> She became the fourth solo female to have multiple songs spend at least five weeks each at number one in one calendar year.<ref name="adele1"/>

Rihanna's "[[We Found Love]]" was the longest-running single of the year, having topped the chart for eight consecutive weeks in 2011 and two additional weeks in 2012.<ref name="We Found Love"/> It became only the eleventh single by a female solo artist to have spent at least ten weeks at number-one in the chart's history.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/511191/rihannas-love-scores-a-perfect-10-atop-hot-100|title=Rihanna's 'Love' Scores A Perfect 10 Atop Hot 100|magazine=Billboard|publisher=Prometheus Global Media|last=Trust|first=Gary|date=2012-01-18|access-date=2012-02-03|archive-date=2020-09-15|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200915013903/https://www.billboard.com/articles/news/511191/rihannas-love-scores-a-perfect-10-atop-hot-100|url-status=live}}</ref> Adele's "Rolling In the Deep" stayed at number one for seven consecutive weeks, while Lady Gaga's "[[Born This Way (song)|Born This Way]]"—the 1,000th number-one single of the Hot 100—and LMFAO's "[[Party Rock Anthem]]" topped the chart for six consecutive weeks.<ref name="We Found Love"/><ref name="1000th number one"/> Perry's "[[E.T. (song)|E.T.]]" and Adele's "[[Someone Like You (Adele song)|Someone Like You]]" each spent five weeks at the number-one spot.<ref name="We Found Love"/>
