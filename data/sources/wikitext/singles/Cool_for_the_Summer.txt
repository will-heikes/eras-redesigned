"'''Cool for the Summer'''" is a song by American singer [[Demi Lovato]]. It was released as the lead single from her fifth studio album ''[[Confident (album)|Confident]]'' (2015) on July 1, 2015, by [[Hollywood Records]] and [[Island Records]], and premiered on radio on the same date via [[Republic Records]]. Lovato co-wrote "Cool for the Summer" with [[Alexander Kronlund|Alexander Erik Kronlund]], [[Savan Kotecha]], and its producers [[Max Martin]] and [[Ali Payami]]. It is an [[electropop]] and [[pop rock]] song with an [[electronic music|electronic]] instrumental arrangement, a synthesized beat and an electric-guitar riff during the chorus.

"Cool for the Summer" was included in several year-end lists and received nominations for [[MTV Video Music Awards]] and [[2015 Teen Choice Awards]] in the category Song of the Summer. It also received an award for being one of the most-performed songs of 2015 at the [[ASCAP Pop Music Awards]]. Commercially, "Cool for the Summer" reached number-one in Greece and Israel and reached the top ten in the Czech Republic, Lebanon, New Zealand, Scotland, and the United Kingdom. The song also peaked within the top 20 in Australia, Canada, Ireland, Slovakia, and the United States.

A music video for the song was directed by [[Hannah Lux Davis]] and was released on [[Vevo]] on July 23, 2015. The video was positively received; publications praised Lovato's sultriness and artistic growth. To promote the song, Lovato performed it at pool parties a few days after its release, and on several television programs such as ''[[The Voice (Australian TV series)|The Voice Australia]]'', ''[[Sunrise (Australian TV program)|Sunrise]]'', the [[2015 MTV Video Music Awards]] and ''[[Jimmy Kimmel Live]]''. The singer also performed the song in a medley with "[[Confident (Demi Lovato song)|Confident]]" on ''[[Saturday Night Live]]'' during the show's [[Saturday Night Live (season 41)|forty-first season]]. Wearing a T-shirt featuring an inclusive bathroom symbol, Lovato showed her support for the [[LGBTQ community]] and demonstrated against [[Bathroom bill#North Carolina|North Carolina]]'s anti-LGBT bathroom bill during her performance of "Cool for the Summer" at the [[2016 Billboard Music Awards|2016 ''Billboard'' Music Awards]] on May 22, 2016. She also included the song on the set lists for the [[Future Now Tour]] (2016), [[Tell Me You Love Me World Tour]] (2018), and [[Holy Fvck Tour]] (2022).

Due to a resurgence of the song on TikTok in March 2022, the song re-peaked in several countries worldwide, reaching number-one on the global Shazam chart and debuting on the [[Billboard Global 200|''Billboard'' Global 200]] chart. In response to the song going viral, Lovato released a sped-up version of the song on April 1, 2022. A re-recorded rock version of the song was released on May 25, 2023, and was included on her first remix album ''[[Revamped]]'' (2023).

==Chart performance==
===North America===
In the United States, "Cool for the Summer" debuted at number 36 on the [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]] dated July 18, 2015, with opening sales of 80,000 copies.<ref name="billboard debut">{{cite magazine|last1=Trust|first1=Gary|title=Hot 100 Chart Moves: Rihanna Roars to New High After 'Money' Video Release|url=https://www.billboard.com/pro/hot-100-chart-moves-rihanna-bbhmm-video/|access-date=24 August 2015|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|publisher=[[Prometheus Global Media]]|date=July 10, 2015}}</ref> In its second week on the chart, the song rose to number 28, becoming Lovato's first top-thirty hit since "[[Really Don't Care]]" (2014). In the same week, the song was also ranked at number 15 on [[Digital Songs]] with sales of 108,000; and at number 40 on [[Radio Songs (chart)|Radio Songs]], prompted by a 29-percent increase in the radio audience that week to 36 million. The release of its [[lyric video]] on July 7, 2015, also prompted a gain of 185 percent in US streams to 2.4 million, the second-largest advance on the chart that week behind [[Fetty Wap]]'s song "[[679 (song)|679]]".<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Trust |first=Gary |date=July 16, 2015 |title=Hot 100 Chart Moves: Demi Lovato's 'Summer' Heats Up |url=https://www.billboard.com/pro/hot-100-chart-moves-demi-lovato-cool-for-summer/ |magazine=Billboard |access-date=February 22, 2017}}</ref> On the August 1, 2015, chart, the song slipped from its number 28 high to number 31 on the Hot 100 but continued to gain radio audience and streams. It climbed from number 40 to number 25 on Radio Songs—45 million, up 25 percent—and debuted on [[Streaming Songs]] at number 41 with 3.3 million US streams, up 41 percent.<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Trust |first=Gary |date=July 22, 2015 |title=Hot 100 Chart Moves: Fifth Harmony Ups Its 'Worth,' Selena Gomez & Demi Lovato Gain |url=https://www.billboard.com/pro/fifth-harmony-selena-gomez-demi-lovato-hot-100/ |magazine=Billboard |access-date=February 22, 2017}}</ref> After the released of the music video on July 23, 2015, "Cool For the Summer" rose from number 31 to number 24 on August 8, 2015, becoming Lovato's highest charting position since "[[Heart Attack (Demi Lovato song)|Heart Attack]]" (2013).<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Petroluongo |first=Silvio |date=July 29, 2015 |title=Hot 100 Chart Moves: Demi Lovato's 'Cool' Cruises, X Ambassadors Climb |url=https://www.billboard.com/pro/hot-100-chart-moves-demi-lovatos-cool-cruises-x-ambassadors/ |magazine=Billboard |access-date=February 22, 2017}}</ref>

The song climbed from 24 to number 19 on the Hot 100 chart issued on August 15, 2015, and from number 18 to number 12 on the Digital Songs chart with sales of 63,000 downloads (up 17 percent) in the week ending July 30, according to [[Nielsen SoundScan]]. It climbed from number 31 to number 18 on Streaming Songs—6.6 million US streams, up 49 percent—and rose from number 22 to number 18 on Radio Songs with 56 million audience impressions, a rise of nine percent.<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Trust |first=Gary |date=August 3, 2015 |title=OMI's 'Cheerleader' No. 1 on Hot 100 for Fourth Week |url=https://www.billboard.com/pro/omi-cheerleader-number-1-hot-100-fourth-week/ |magazine=Billboard |access-date=February 22, 2017}}</ref><ref>{{cite magazine |last=Trust |first=Gary |date=August 6, 2015 |title=Hot 100 Chart Moves: Charlie Puth & Meghan Trainor's 'Marvin Gaye' Hits Top 40 |url=https://www.billboard.com/pro/charlie-puth-meghan-trainor-marvin-gaye-hot-100-top-40/ |magazine=Billboard |access-date=February 22, 2017}}</ref> "Cool for the Summer" later peaked at number 11 on the Hot 100.<ref name=BBHot100/>

On the [[Dance Club Songs]] chart, "Cool for the Summer" reached number one in its fifth week; its climb from number five to number one was the quickest on that chart since [[Lady Gaga]]'s song "[[Applause (Lady Gaga song)|Applause]]", which in October 2013 also reached number one in five weeks.<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Murray |first=Gordon |date=September 1, 2015 |title=Demi Lovato's 'Cool for the Summer' Is Fastest No. 1 on Dance Club Songs in Two Years |url=https://www.billboard.com/pro/demi-lovato-cool-for-summer-dance-club-songs-chart/ |magazine=Billboard |access-date=February 22, 2017}}</ref> On September 23, 2016, "Cool for the Summer" was certified Double Platinum by the [[Recording Industry Association of America]] (RIAA), denoting sales and streams of two million copies in the US.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.riaa.com/gold-platinum/?tab_active=default-award&ar=DEMI+LOVATO&ti=COOL+FOR+THE+SUMMER|title=Cool for the Summer by Demi Lovato - ==Remixes==
[[Safehouse Records]] released several remixes of the song; the first remixes were done by [[Todd Terry]], VARA, [[Dave Audé]], [[Cahill]], Plastic Plates, and Mike Cruz. These were released on a [[compact disc single|CD single]] and digital download titled ''Cool for the Summer– The Remixes'' on August 7, 2015.<ref name="TheRemixes">{{cite web|url=https://music.apple.com/us/album/cool-for-summer-remixes/1023476435|title=iTunes Music&nbsp;— Cool for the Summer: The Remixes&nbsp;— EP by Demi Lovato|date=January 2015 |publisher=iTunes Store|access-date=August 15, 2015|archive-date=November 5, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181105070623/https://itunes.apple.com/us/album/cool-for-summer-remixes/id1023476435|url-status=live}}</ref> Lindsay Harvey of ''Billboard'' wrote that the remix by Plastic Plates "slows things down a bit" and features a "steadily paced bass line" with "lighter rhythms".<ref name="Billboard4">{{cite magazine |last=Havens |first=Lyndsey |date=October 7, 2015 |title=Demi Lovato Songs Remixed: Listen to 7 of the Best |url=http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/dance/7533423/demi-lovato-songs-best-remixes-list |magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]] |access-date=February 23, 2017}}</ref> American DJ Todd Terry told ''Billboard'':

<blockquote>I really liked the freedom I got from Demi and her team. She told me to go make a version that I would play out in one of my sets versus focusing on a radio-friendly mix. That was refreshing and I think that being able to fully approach it with doing what was best for the track for club play really made this remix rock.<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Caulfield |first=Keith |date=July 20, 2015 |title=Demi Lovato's 'Cool For the Summer' Remix by Todd Terry: Exclusive Premiere |url=https://www.billboard.com/music/pop/demi-lovato-cool-for-the-summer-remix-todd-terry-exclusive-6634731/ |magazine=Billboard |access-date=February 23, 2017}}</ref></blockquote>

"Cool for the Summer" also was remixed by [[electronic dance music]] DJ duo [[Jump Smokers]] and Suraci; ''Billboard''{{'}}s Harvey described the first version as a "high-energy remix" and praised it, calling it relentless "from beginning to end, save for a momentary breath of air in the middle that exists merely as a much needed break".<ref name="Billboard4"/> Both remixes were included in the deluxe edition of ''[[Confident (album)|Confident]]''.<ref>{{cite web|url= https://music.apple.com/us/album/confident-deluxe-edition/1033757133|title= Confident (Deluxe Edition)|work= iTunes Store (US)|date= January 2015|access-date= February 23, 2017|archive-date= October 8, 2015|archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20151008123640/https://itunes.apple.com/us/album/confident-deluxe-edition/id1033757133|url-status= live}}</ref> Following the resurgence of the song in March 2022, Lovato announced another remix of the song titled "Sped Up" by Speed Radio, which was released on April 1, 2022.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/music/pop/demi-lovato-cool-for-the-summer-remix-on-the-heels-of-tiktok-success-1235052601/|title=Demi Lovato Announces 'Sped Up Remix' of Newly Viral Hit 'Cool for the Summer'|magazine=Billboard|last=Aniftos|first=Rania|date=March 30, 2022|access-date=April 9, 2022}}</ref>

In January 2022, Lovato held a "funeral" for her pop music ahead of the release of her eighth studio album ''[[Holy Fvck]]'' in August of that year.<ref>{{Cite magazine|last=Bowenbank|first=Starr|date=January 21, 2022|title=Demi Lovato Hosts 'Funeral' Mourning Their Pop Music, Teases New Rock Song|url=https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/demi-lovato-funeral-pop-music-teases-rock-song-1235021727/|access-date=May 14, 2023|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|language=en-US|archive-date=June 7, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220607120002/https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/demi-lovato-funeral-pop-music-teases-rock-song-1235021727/|url-status=live}}</ref> The album, which embraced a heavier rock sound that departed from the pop sound of Lovato's previous releases, was supported by the [[Holy Fvck Tour]]. The setlist included several rock versions of her older pop songs, including "Cool for the Summer".<ref>{{cite web|first=Jared|last=Stossel|url=https://consequence.net/2022/09/demi-lovato-concert-review-2022-tour-setlist/|title=Demi Lovato Rages at "HOLY FVCK" Tour Stop in Sacramento: Review, Photos and Setlist|work=[[Consequence (publication)|Consequence]]|date=September 23, 2022|access-date=May 14, 2023|archive-date=March 23, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230323231022/https://consequence.net/2022/09/demi-lovato-concert-review-2022-tour-setlist/|url-status=live}}</ref>

On March 24, 2023, Lovato released a [[Heart Attack (Demi Lovato song)#"Heart Attack (Rock Version)"|rock version]] of her pop song "[[Heart Attack (Demi Lovato song)|Heart Attack]]" to mark the song's 10th anniversary.<ref>{{cite web|first=Rebekah|last=Gonzalez|url=https://www.iheart.com/content/2023-03-24-demi-lovato-re-records-heart-attack-for-10-year-anniversary/|title=Demi Lovato Re-Records 'Heart Attack' For 10-Year Anniversary|work=[[iHeart Radio]]|date=March 24, 2023|access-date=May 14, 2023|archive-date=May 14, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230514225512/https://www.iheart.com/content/2023-03-24-demi-lovato-re-records-heart-attack-for-10-year-anniversary/|url-status=live}}</ref> On May 18, she announced via social media that she would release a rock version of "Cool for the Summer".<ref name="BillboardRockVersion">{{Cite magazine|first=Hannah|last=Dalley|url=https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/demi-lovato-cool-for-the-summer-rock-remix-sneak-peek-1235332636/|title=Demi Lovato Is Giving 'Cool for the Summer' a Rock Remix: Check Out the Sneak Peek|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|date=May 19, 2023|access-date=May 19, 2023}}</ref> It was released on May 25, 2023.<ref name="RollingStoneRockVersion">{{Cite magazine|first=Daniel|last=Kreps|url=https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/demi-lovato-rock-version-cool-for-the-summer-1234741937/|title=Demi Lovato Drops a Rock Version of Her Hit 'Cool for the Summer'|magazine=[[Rolling Stone]]|date=May 25, 2023|access-date=May 30, 2023}}</ref>

===Production===
Lovato initially teased a rock version of "Cool for the Summer" in an August 2022 video for ''[[Elle (magazine)|Elle]]'', saying that "if I had to re-record one of my songs for this album [''Holy Fvck''], it'd be 'Cool for the Summer'".<ref>{{Cite magazine|first=Samantha|last=Olson|url=https://www.seventeen.com/celebrity/music/a40978965/demi-lovato-rock-re-recording-cool-for-the-summer/|title=Demi Lovato Teases Rock Re-Recording of "Cool for the Summer"|magazine=[[Seventeen (American magazine)|Seventeen]]|date=August 24, 2022|access-date=May 30, 2023}}</ref>

"Cool for the Summer (Rock Version)" was produced by Warren "Oak" Felder, Keith "Ten4" Sorrells, and Alex Nice, all of whom contributed to ''Holy Fvck''.<ref name="RollingStoneRockVersion"/> Hannah Dalley of ''[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]'' described the snippet shared by Lovato to announce the rock version's release as consisting of "pounding drums and razor-edged electric guitar".<ref name="BillboardRockVersion"/> Upon the full song's release, Starr Bowenbank wrote for the same publication that the rock version "swaps the original's poppy synths and uptempo instrumentation for a darker sound backed by electric guitars and grittier lyric delivery from Lovato".<ref>{{Cite magazine|first=Starr|last=Bowenbank|url=https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/demi-lovato-cool-for-the-summer-rock-version-1235340294/|title=Demi Lovato Updates 'Cool For the Summer' With New Rock Version: Listen|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|date=May 25, 2023|access-date=May 30, 2023}}</ref> "Cool for the Summer (Rock Version)" contains a lyric change: the line "don't tell your mother" in the pre-chorus was replaced by "go tell your mother".<ref name="BillboardRockVersion"/>
