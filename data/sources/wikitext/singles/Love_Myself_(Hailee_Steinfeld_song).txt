"'''Love Myself'''" is the debut single by American actress and singer [[Hailee Steinfeld]]. It was released on August 7, 2015, through [[Republic Records]] and [[Universal Music Group]], as the lead single from her debut [[extended play]] (EP), ''[[Haiz (EP)|Haiz]]'' (2015).<ref name="iTunes Store (US)">{{cite web|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151001173355/https://itunes.apple.com/us/album/love-myself-single/id1024233199 |url=https://itunes.apple.com/us/album/love-myself-single/id1024233199 |title=Love Myself - Single by Hailee Steinfeld on iTunes |archive-date=October 1, 2015 |access-date=September 9, 2016 |work=[[iTunes Store]] (US) |url-status=dead }}</ref> The song was written by Mattias Larsson, Robin Fredriksson, [[Oscar Holter]], [[Julia Michaels]], and [[Justin Tranter]], with the production being handled by Holter, with Larsson and Fredriksson under their stage name Mattman & Robin.<ref>{{cite web|url = http://www.allmusic.com/album/love-myself-mw0002868900|title = Love Myself - Hailee Steinfeld - Songs, Reviews, Credits - AllMusic|access-date = September 9, 2016|work = [[AllMusic]]}}</ref><ref>{{cite magazine|url = https://www.billboard.com/music/pop/hailee-steinfeld-bad-blood-exclusive-taylor-swift-record-deal-pitch-perfect-6590062/|title = 'Pitch Perfect' Star Hailee Steinfeld on Her Debut Album and Starring in 'Bad Blood' - Billboard|last1 = Hampp|first1 = Andrew|access-date = September 9, 2016|date = June 6, 2015|magazine = [[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|publisher = [[Prometheus Global Media]]}}</ref>

A [[dance-pop]] and [[electropop]] record, the track lyrically centres around self-confidence and has been referred to as an "ode to [[masturbation]]" in the media. The song received positive reviews from contemporary music critics who praised Steinfeld's voice and style, comparing positively to [[Taylor Swift]] and [[Selena Gomez]]. The song also found commercial success, peaking at number 30 on the [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]], number six on the [[US]] [[Dance Club Songs]] chart and number 15 on the ''Billboard'' [[Mainstream Top 40|Pop Songs]] chart.

==Background and release==
Steinfeld gained recognition as a singer after performing a cover of [[Jessie J]]'s "[[Flashlight (Jessie J song)|Flashlight]]" in ''[[Pitch Perfect 2]]'' (2015) which she also starred in.<ref>{{Cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/hailee-steinfeld-single-love-myself-debut-pitch-perfect-2-6656667/|title=Hailee Steinfeld Debuts 'Love Myself,' Self-Empowering First Single|accessdate=December 16, 2021|magazine=Billboard}}</ref> Named "Flashlight (Sweet Life Mix)", the cover was released on the special edition of the movie's [[Pitch Perfect 2 (soundtrack)|soundtrack]], appearing alongside the original version, and was certified Gold by [[Recorded Music New Zealand]] (RMNZ). She would soon sign with [[Republic Records]] in the beginning of 2015.<ref name="peoplelmhs">{{Cite web|url=https://people.com/celebrity/hailee-steinfeld-releases-love-myself-her-first-single/|title=Listen to Hailee Steinfeld's Super-Catchy First Single, 'Love Myself'|accessdate=December 16, 2021|website=People}}</ref> "Love Myself" was released as Steinfeld's debut single on August 7, 2015.<ref name="peoplelmhs"/> ''[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]'' noted that:<blockquote>
"The 18-year-old, who showed the world her chops as the standout newcomer in ''Pitch Perfect 2'', brings all the self-confidence a girl could need to the empowering anthem."</blockquote>

==Music and lyrics==
The song was described as a "sugary, feel-good pop jam".<ref name="peoplelmhs"/> The lyrics to the song include "I'm gonna put my body first/And love me so hard 'til it hurts" and "I'm gonna touch the pain away/I know how to scream my own name"; the music video, released one week later, features Steinfeld wearing a leotard emblazoned with "self service".<ref name=LoveMyself>{{cite magazine|last=Weatherby|first=Taylor|url=http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/6656667/hailee-steinfeld-single-love-myself-debut-pitch-perfect-2|title=Hailee Steinfeld Debuts "Love Myself", Self-Empowering First Single|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|date=August 7, 2015|accessdate=August 16, 2015}}{{indent|3}}{{cite web|last=Russell|first=Erica|url=http://popcrush.com/hailee-steinfeld-love-myself-video/|title=Hailee Steinfeld Spreads the Love In New Video for "Love Myself"|work=POPCRUSH|date=August 15, 2015|accessdate=August 16, 2015}}</ref> This prompted many fans and media outlets to dub the song an "ode to [[masturbation]]".<ref>{{cite news|url=https://nypost.com/2015/08/13/hailee-steinfeld-sings-an-ode-to-masturbation/|title=Hailee Steinfeld sings an ode to masturbation|work=New York Daily News|author=Hardeep Phull|date=August 13, 2015|accessdate=September 12, 2015}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|url=http://hollywoodlife.com/2015/08/14/hailee-steinfeld-masturbating-love-myself-meaning-fans-reaction-twitter/|title=Hailee Steinfeld's 'Love Myself' About Masturbating? ==Themes and analysis==
''[[Vulture (magazine)|Vulture]]'' writer Dee Lockett noted that Steinfeld "made her ode to masturbation just subliminal enough to fly over a less-involved parent's head, and its sugary [[TeenNick]]-appropriate production probably will not raise any suspicions either".<ref name="vlm1"/> She also pointed out a feminist lens of the song saying that if "Love Myself" means the music industry "might finally let young female pop-stars stop pretending that men are the only way they can get off, then Hailee Steinfeld might've just thrown her name into the conversation about pop's new feminist generation".<ref name="vlm1"/>

Steinfeld herself has stated that the meaning of the song is intended to be interpreted by the listener, "but for me it's an empowerment record and it’s ultimately about taking care of yourself and indulging yourself, whether that be emotionally or physically or with material things."<ref name=Popjustice>{{cite web|last=Robinson|first=Peter|author-link=Peter Robinson (journalist)|url=http://www.popjustice.com/articles/hailee-steinfeld-interview-i-wasnt-prepared-for-it-to-happen-this-quickly/137953/|title=Hailee Steinfeld interview: "I wasn't prepared for it to happen this quickly"|work=[[Popjustice]]|date=August 25, 2015|accessdate=August 28, 2015}}</ref> Steinfeld noted in an interview with [[MTV]] that "there is that sense of fearlessness, which I think kind of came from that word exactly: just being fearless and being able to kind of put myself out there".<ref name="mtv123"/>

==Critical reception==
[[File:Hailee Steinfeld 2 (42287125684 ).jpg|thumb|130px|Steinfeld performing the song on the [[Witness: The Tour|Witness Tour]] in 2017]]
Upon release, the track received positive reviews. ''[[The Young Folks]]'' writer Brooke Pawling Stennet wrote that she "shows great potential through her debut track" calling it a "solid debut for a young Steinfeld now eighteen years old and ready to make a name for herself".<ref name="tyf1lm">{{Cite web|url=https://www.theyoungfolks.com/review/61639/hailee-steinfeld-releases-debut-track-love-myself/|title=Hailee Steinfeld Releases Debut Track, "Love Myself"|accessdate=January 7, 2022|website=The Young Folks|date=11 August 2015 }}</ref> She did feel that the 2015-2016 trend for being "body positive" is not captivated in the song but noted that it is still a "fun song that lies in the same vein as the new style that artists like [[Taylor Swift]], [[Selena Gomez]], and [[One Direction]] are all pushing towards as they break free of their own invisible chains of adolescent and innocent teeny bop status".<ref name="tyf1lm"/> ''[[The Independent]]'' also praised the track saying that it "leaves any listener feeling empowered and happy with themselves".<ref name="tilm1">{{Cite web|url=https://www.indiependent.co.uk/track-review-love-hailee-steinfeld/|title=Track Review: Love Myself // Hailee Steinfeld|accessdate=January 7, 2022|website=The Independent|date=31 August 2015 }}</ref> They also called the song "pop bliss" praising Hailee's unique voice and comparing it to [[Tove Lo]] and [[Ariana Grande]].<ref name="tilm1"/> They went on to sat that "if this is what [Steinfeld] has to offer on her debut, then she is an artist to look out for".<ref name="tilm1"/>

''[[Vulture (magazine)|Vulture]]'' writer Dee Lockett also praise the song noting "a glaze to Steinfeld’s vocal style on the track that's unmistakably [[Taylor Swift|Swift]]-influenced.<ref name="vlm1">{{Cite web|url=https://www.vulture.com/2015/08/listen-hailee-steinfeld-love-myself.html|title=Hailee Steinfeld Releases 'Love Myself'; It Means Just What You Think It Means|accessdate=January 7, 2022|website=Vulture|date=7 August 2015 }}</ref> She also compared the song's "down to the shout-sung 'I love me!' hook" to [[Kendrick Lamar]].<ref name="vlm1"/> She also compared the song to Selena Gomez's "[[Good for You (Selena Gomez song)|Good for You]]" noting that Steinfeld also "celebrates a sexual awakening".<ref name="vlm1"/> ''[[MTV]]'' writer Madeline Roth called Steinfeld a "fearless pop star" noting that she "came roaring out the gate with a self-empowerment anthem" which sets "her apart from the pack of pop stars who came before her".<ref name="mtv123">{{Cite web|url=http://www.mtv.com/news/2693414/hailee-steinfeld-fearless-pop-star/|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151224044658/http://www.mtv.com/news/2693414/hailee-steinfeld-fearless-pop-star/|url-status=dead|archive-date=December 24, 2015|title=HAILEE STEINFELD IS THE FEARLESS POP STAR WE NEED IN 2016|accessdate=January 20, 2022|website=MTV}}</ref> She also compared "Love Myself" to the debut singles of [[Britney Spears]], [[Christina Aguilera]], [[Mandy Moore]], [[Jessica Simpson]], Selena Gomez and Taylor Swift ("[[...Baby One More Time (song)|...Baby One More Time]], "[[Genie In a Bottle]]", "[[Candy (Mandy Moore song)|Candy]]", "[[I Wanna Love You Forever]]", "[[Come & Get It (Selena Gomez song)|Come & Get It]]" and "[[Tim McGraw (song)|Tim McGraw]]" respectively).<ref name="mtv123"/> Roth added that the song is "a strong statement for a 19-year-old to make, and in a way, it's one that only someone as young and optimistic as her could make".<ref name="mtv123"/>

==Commercial performance==

=== United States of America ===
Upon its release, the song was commercially successful for a new artist. On release, the single peaked at number 30 on the [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]], alongside charting at number 36 on the [[Adult Pop Airplay]] chart, number six on the [[US]] [[Dance Club Songs]] chart, number 30 on the [[Dance/Mix Show Airplay]] chart, and number 15 on the ''Billboard'' [[Mainstream Top 40|Pop Songs]] chart. On the latter, the song debuted at number 27, marking the [[List of Billboard Hot 100 chart achievements and milestones|highest debut for a solo female artist on the chart in 17 years]], since [[Natalie Imbruglia]]'s single "[[Torn (Ednaswap song)#Natalie Imbruglia version|Torn]]" entered at number 26 in 1998.<ref>{{cite magazine|last1=Anderson|first1=Trevor|title=Hailee Steinfeld Finds 'Love' on Billboard + Twitter Top Tracks|url=https://www.billboard.com/pro/hailee-steinfeld-love-myself-twitter-top-tracks/|access-date=28 November 2015|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|publisher=[[Prometheus Global Media]]|date=August 20, 2015}}</ref><ref>{{cite magazine|url={{BillboardURLbyName|artist=Hailee Steinfeld|chart=Pop Songs B}}|title=Hailee Steinfeld – Chart history (Pop Songs)|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|publisher=[[Prometheus Global Media]]|access-date=November 28, 2015}}</ref> By the end of 2016, the song was positioned at number 48 on the Dance Club Songs chart. The song was also certified 2× Platinum by the [[Recording Industry Association of America]] (RIAA) for equivalent sales of 2,000,000 units in the [[United States of America]].

=== Canada ===
In [[Canada]], the single charted higher than in the United States of America, peaking at number 15 on the [[Canadian Hot 100]]. It also charted at number 37 on the [[Billboard charts|AC Airplay]] and number 16 on both the [[CHR/Top 40]] chart and the [[Hot AC]] chart. The single was also certified 2× Platinum by [[Music Canada]] (MC) for equivalent sales of 160,000 units in the country. By the end of 2015, the single was positioned at number 79 on the Canadian Hot 100.

=== Europe ===
In [[Belgium]], the single charted at number 4 on the [[Ultratip Bubbling Under]] chart, in [[Flanders]], its highest position on any chart, and number 29 on the Ultratip Bubbling Under chart in [[Wallonia]]. In the [[Czech Republic]], the single charted at number 43 on the [[Rádio – Top 100 (Czech Republic)|Rádio – Top 100]] chart and peaked at number 12 on the [[International Federation of the Phonographic Industry|Singles Digital Top 100]] chart. In [[Denmark]], the single charted at number 36 on the [[Tracklisten]] chart and was certified Platinum by [[IFPI Danmark]] (IFPI DEN) for equivalent sales of 90,000 units in the country. In [[Ireland]], the single charted at number 51 on the [[Irish Singles Chart]]. In [[Italy]], the single peaked at number 50 on the [[Italian Singles Chart]] and was certified Platinum by [[Federazione Industria Musicale Italiana]] (FIMI) for equivalent sales of 50,000 units in the country. In [[Netherlands]], the single charted at number 26 on the [[Dutch Top 40]] and number 37 on the [[Dutch Single Top 100]]. By the end of 2015, the single was positioned at number 99 on the [[NPO 3FM]] chart.

In [[Norway]], the single peaked at number 20 on the [[VG-lista]] charts. In [[Slovakia]], the single charted at number 72 on the [[Rádio – Top 100 (Slovakia)|Rádio – Top 100]] and peaked at number 12 on the Singles Digital Top 100. In [[Spain]], although the single did not chart, it was certified Gold by [[Productores de Música de España]] (PROMUSICAE) for equivalent sales of 30,000 units in the country. In [[Sweden]], the single charted at number 35 on the [[Sverigetopplistan]] chart and was certified Platinum by [[Grammofonleverantörernas förening]] (GLF) for equivalent sales of 40,000 units in the country. In the [[United Kingdom]], the single peaked at number 180 on the [[UK singles chart]], its lowest position on any chart, and was certified Platinum by the [[British Phonographic Industry]] (BPI) for equivalent sales of 600,000 units in the country.

=== Other countries ===
In [[Australia]], the single peaked at number 92 on the [[ARIA Charts]]. In [[Brazil]], although the single did not chart, it was still certified 2× Platinum by [[Pro-Música Brasil]] (PMB) for equivalent sales of 120,000 units in the country. In [[Israel]], the single peaked at number 5 on the [[Media Forest]] TV Airplay chart. In [[Japan]], the single charted at number 35 on the [[Japan Hot 100]]. In [[New Zealand]], the song charted at number 19 on the [[Official Aotearoa Music Charts]] and was certified 2× Platinum by [[Recorded Music New Zealand]] (RMNZ) for equivalent sales of 60,000 units in the country. In [[Switzerland]], the single charted at number 64 on the [[Schweizer Hitparade]] chart.

==Usage in media==
The song has been featured in multiple soundtracks, films and television shows. In 2015, the song was included on the ''[[Jem and the Holograms (film)|Jem and the Holograms]]'' soundtrack.<ref>{{cite web|url = http://popcrush.com/hilary-duff-joins-jem-and-the-holograms-youngblood/|title = Hilary Duff Joins Jem And The Holograms On Film Soundtrack|last1 = Russell|first1 = Erica|access-date = September 9, 2016|date = October 17, 2015|work = [[PopCrush]]}}</ref> The song was also featured in ''[[Blockers (film)|Blockers]]'' (2018).<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://tvovermind.com/the-best-uses-of-hailee-steinfeld-songs-in-movies-or-tv/|title=The Best Uses of Hailee Steinfeld Songs in Movies or TV|accessdate=January 20, 2022|website=TV Over Mind|date=21 December 2018 }}</ref> In a review for the film, ''[[Insider (website)|Insider]]'' writer Kim Renfro commented that "the anthem carried throughout the movie" and that the song "drives the message home".<ref>{{Cite news|url=http://www.thisisinsider.com/blockers-movie-review-john-cena-2018-4|title=REVIEW: 'Blockers' is a raunchy, must-see comedy with a rare feminist message|work=Insider|last=Renfro|first=Kim|date=April 4, 2018|access-date=April 8, 2018|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180408141459/http://www.thisisinsider.com/blockers-movie-review-john-cena-2018-4|archive-date=April 8, 2018}}</ref> The song has also been played in ''[[Love Island (franchise)|Love Island]]'', ''[[Stargirl (TV series)|DC's Stargirl]]'', ''[[How to Be Single]]'' and at [[World of Dance]] events.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.tunefind.com/artist/hailee-steinfeld|title=Hailee Steinfeld music featured in movies, TV shows and video games|accessdate=January 20, 2022|website=TuneFind}}</ref>
