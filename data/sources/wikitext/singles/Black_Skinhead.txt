"'''Black Skinhead'''" (also stylized as "'''BLKKK SKKKN HEAD'''") is a song by American rapper [[Kanye West]], from his sixth studio album ''[[Yeezus]]'' (2013). It was [[hip hop production|produced]] by West and [[Daft Punk]].<!--with additional production by  [[Gesaffelstein]], Brodinski, [[Mike Dean (record producer)|Mike Dean]], [[Lupe Fiasco]], [[Salem (Michigan band)|Jack Donoghue]], and Noah Goldstein. [Excessive for lead clearly not that important since they are mentioned only in the Credits section]]--> The song's lyrics center on racial tensions and the crumbling mental state of the character West portrays on the album. <!--A lyrical reference to West's track "[[Good Morning (Kanye West song)|Good Morning]]" helps envelop the character. The industrial production lies among the opening stretch of gnarled [[Electronic music|electro]] and pounding [[Industrial hip hop|industrial rap]] on ''Yeezus''. [Not sure what either sentence means]--> The song premiered on ''[[Saturday Night Live]]'' in May 2013, with West performing it in front of a projected backdrop. He has since performed the song live on various occasions, including at the [[Glastonbury Festival]] and the [[Billboard Music Awards|''Billboard'' Music Awards]] in 2015. It was universally praised by music critics and ranked by numerous sites, including ''[[Rolling Stone]]'' and ''[[NME]]'', as one of the best tracks of 2013. The song's accompanying [[music video]] was directed by [[Nick Knight (photographer)|Nick Knight]] and features [[computer-generated imagery]] of West, with interactive options including "screen grabbing" and adjusting the speed of his vocals. The video was released in July 2013 and has received positive reviews from critics.

Although West initially said ''Yeezus'' would have no singles, "Black Skinhead" became the first single released from the album. It was serviced to [[contemporary hit radio]] stations in the [[United Kingdom]] one day after the album's release. On July 2, 2013, the single was sent to [[urban contemporary]] radio stations in the United States. The song charted in the United States, the United Kingdom, Ireland, France, Canada, Belgium and Australia in 2013 and 2014. It has since been [[music recording sales certification|certified]] platinum in the United States, the United Kingdom and Denmark. American singer [[Jack White]] covered the song in June 2014. A remix featuring [[Miley Cyrus]] and [[Travis Scott]], recorded shortly after the [[2013 MTV Video Music Awards]], leaked online in January 2016.

The song's title uses "[[skinhead]]", which originated as a description of a 1960s [[Social class in the United Kingdom#Working class|British working-class]] male subculture whose members often had closely cropped hair. It revolved around fashion and music and went on to inspire the [[punk rock]] scene.<ref name="daily"/> Despite attracting young males across the political spectrum, the term had come to be popularly associated with [[Neo-Nazism|neo-Nazis]] at the time of single's release.<ref name="daily"/> [[Travis Scott]] said of his initial reaction to the song: "I jumped off the stairs onto the couch. I was going HAM. That was when I heard the 'na na na na' part for the first time, I lost my fucking mind. That's some soccer anthem-type shit."<ref name="sessions"/> According to co-producer [[Mike Dean (record producer)|Mike Dean]], the song was almost left off ''Yeezus'' for sounding "too much like a soccer song."<ref name="sessions">{{cite web | url=https://pitchfork.com/features/article/9157-the-yeezus-sessions/ | title=The Yeezus Sessions | work=[[Pitchfork (website)|Pitchfork]] | date=June 24, 2013 | access-date=March 30, 2019 | author=Dombal, Ryan | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181106144327/https://pitchfork.com/features/article/9157-the-yeezus-sessions/ | archive-date=November 6, 2018 | url-status=live }}</ref> In November 2013, co-writer [[Lupe Fiasco]] still felt unsure what the song was supposed to be about. He described it as "a lot of emotional, a lot of this, a lot of cliché, a lot of kinda stuff balled in together, and it's just supposed to be presented as a package and you digest it."<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.rap-up.com/2013/11/17/lupe-fiasco-sounds-off-on-yeezus-new-album-control-response/ | title=Lupe Fiasco Sounds Off on 'Yeezus,' New Album, & 'Control' Response | work=[[Rap-Up]] | date=November 17, 2013 | access-date=April 6, 2019 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150703031700/http://www.rap-up.com/2013/11/17/lupe-fiasco-sounds-off-on-yeezus-new-album-control-response/ | archive-date=July 3, 2015 | url-status=dead }}</ref>

"Black Skinhead" has an industrial-sounding beat, and is part of the Yeezus character's opening segment of gnarled [[Electronic music|electro]] and pounding [[Industrial hip hop|industrial rap]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.avclub.com/kanye-west-yeezus-1798177119|title=Kanye West: Yeezus|work=[[The A.V. Club]]|publisher=[[Onion, Inc.]]|first=Evan|last=Rytlewski|date=June 17, 2013|access-date=July 9, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130709111934/http://www.avclub.com/articles/kanye-west-yeezus%2C99105/|archive-date=July 9, 2013|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.npr.org/blogs/therecord/2013/05/21/185781341/kanye-west-stands-alone|title=Kanye West Stands Alone|work=[[NPR]]|publisher=The Record|first=Frannie|last=Kelley|date=May 21, 2013|access-date=July 9, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130711041251/http://www.npr.org/blogs/therecord/2013/05/21/185781341/kanye-west-stands-alone|archive-date=July 11, 2013|url-status=live|df=mdy-all}}</ref> West begins the song with the lines: "For my theme song/My leather black jeans on/My by-any-means on" a reference to "[[Good Morning (Kanye West song)|Good Morning]]", the opening track of his third studio album ''[[Graduation (album)|Graduation]]''.<ref name="daily"/> These lines use a "simple aesthetic touch"—leather black jeans—to envelop Yeezus, the character West portrays, in black, rugged gear. This reflects his awareness, expressed on his 2005 single "[[Diamonds from Sierra Leone]]", that money and possessions are often equated with self-worth by young black men.<ref name="daily">{{cite web | url=https://www.dailydot.com/upstream/kanye-west-black-skinhead/ | title='Black Skinhead': The Politics of New Kanye West on 'Yeezus' | work=[[Daily Dot]] | date=November 12, 2017 | access-date=April 6, 2019 | author=Lambert, Chris | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171112222310/https://www.dailydot.com/upstream/kanye-west-black-skinhead/ | archive-date=November 12, 2017 | url-status=live}}</ref> The old West is gone. "Black Skinhead" is "now his theme music. This grimy, grungy tone that doesn't require just any jeans, but <em>leather black</em> jeans."<ref name="daily"/> The song's lyrics do not mention Malcolm X or the Civil Rights Movement. "But when we make the connections between 'by any means necessary' (Malcolm's famous phrase) and the jeans call-back to 'Good Morning,' and the reference to a cultural movement associated with rebellion, the ghost of Malcolm X and the Civil Rights Movement of the '50s and '60s comes alive."<ref name="daily"/> Yeezus views himself as not only a political leader for the Black community, but a superhero.<ref name="daily"/> West raps: "Pardon, I'm getting my scream on" in the fourth line and screams in frustration throughout the song.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://mashable.com/archive/kanye-west-blkkk-skkkn-head | title=Kanye West Debuts Interactive Video for 'Black Skinhead' | work=[[Mashable]] | date=July 22, 2013 | access-date=May 23, 2019 | author=Fitz-Gerald, Sean | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181211132509/https://mashable.com/2013/07/22/kanye-west-blkkk-skkkn-head/ | archive-date=December 11, 2018 | url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.vibe.com/amp/2013/05/kanye-west-gets-his-scream-rapper-shares-lyrics-black-skinhead-twitter | title=Kanye West Gets His Scream On: Rapper Shares Lyrics to "Black Skinhead" Via Twitter | work=[[Vibe (magazine)|Vibe]] | date=May 19, 2013 | access-date=May 23, 2019 | author=Rogulewski, Charley | archive-date=December 23, 2020 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201223170022/https://www.vibe.com/amp/2013/05/kanye-west-gets-his-scream-rapper-shares-lyrics-black-skinhead-twitter | url-status=dead }}</ref> The rest of song sees Yeezus discussing "racial tensions and his own crumbling mental state."<ref name="daily"/> The "manic repetition" of "Black" shows there is "so much more at stake" for him.<ref name="daily"/> West ends the song by repeating "God!" in an exasperated way. Yeezus is a "leader frustrated by the inaction of his people: 'These niggas ain't doin' shit'." He "mutates the external idea of God into an internal one", which leads into the next track "[[I Am a God]]" where he "hypes himself up."<ref name="daily"/>

==Critical reception==
"Black Skinhead" received universal acclaim from music critics. Ray Rahman of ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'' cited it as one of the album's best songs, describing it as "a galloping punk-rap manifesto".<ref name="Rahman">{{cite magazine | url=https://ew.com/article/2013/07/09/yeezus-album/ | title=Yeezus (2013): Kanye West | magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]] | location=New York | date=June 25, 2013 | access-date=June 29, 2013 | ''Popdust'' rated the song five out of five, describing it as what "would've sounded at home on Top 40 in the late-'00s, at least if you stripped away all the growling bass and the background yelps and turned the drums down in the mix considerably".<ref>{{cite journal | url=https://www.popdust.com/kanye-wests-yeezus-reviewed-black-skinhead-1889715665.html | title=Kanye West's "Yeezus" Reviewed: "Black Skinhead" | journal=Popdust | date=June 17, 2013 | access-date=April 10, 2019 | }}</ref> ''[[The Guardian]]''{{'}}s  [[Alexis Petridis]] pointed out "the battering bovver-glam drum and sampled screaming of 'Black Skinhead'" is an example on the album where, "West appears to be operating under the influence of industrial music".<ref>{{cite journal | url=https://www.theguardian.com/music/2013/jun/17/kanye-west-yeezus-review | title=Kanye West: Yeezus – review {{!}} Music | "[[Hold My Liquor]]" were classified by Phil Witmer of ''[[Vice (magazine)|Noisey]]'' as "rock anthems from the 25th century" not rap.
