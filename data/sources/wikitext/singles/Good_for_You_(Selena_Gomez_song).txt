"'''Good for You'''" is the lead single from American singer [[Selena Gomez]]'s second solo studio album, ''[[Revival (Selena Gomez album)|Revival]]'' (2015). It features vocals by American rapper [[ASAP Rocky]]. The song was written by Gomez and Rocky themselves, alongside fellow American singers [[Julia Michaels]] and [[Justin Tranter]], Hector Delgado, and the song's producers [[Nick Monson]] and [[Sir Nolan|Nolan Lambroza]], the latter producing as "Sir Nolan". The song was recorded in January 2015, at both Interscope Studios, based in [[Santa Monica, California]], and Downtown Studios, based in [[New York City, New York]].

Originally, the track was conceived by the writers in 45 minutes, during a session booked by the latter. Gomez then received the song through the trio's [[A&R]] and was entirely involved in its creative session, helping to develop it as a representation of her process of self-realization, her confidence as a young woman, and her feelings of vulnerability. After it was reworked by Lambroza, she sent the song to Rocky, who added new instruments and co-produced it with his frequent collaborator Hector Delgado, who was in charge of Rocky's vocal production.

After anticipation by Gomez, "Good for You" was [[Music download|digitally]] released on June 22, 2015 by [[Interscope Records|Interscope]] and [[Polydor Records]], serving as ''Revival''{{'}}s [[lead single]] as well as the first song by Gomez to be released through those labels. A [[torch song]], it is a [[Pop music|pop]] and [[Electro (music)|electro]]-[[Contemporary R&B|R&B]] [[slow jam]] with [[Hip hop music|hip hop]] elements that comprises a mellow, low-key snap-drop hip hop beat reminiscent of modern, pitched-down [[Southern hip hop]] productions, minor sound bursts, [[percussion]] instrumentation, [[drum machine]]s, airy and transparent [[synthesizer]], and a minimalistic [[Bass (instrument)|bass]]. Gomez sings atypically with [[Breathy voice|breathy vocals]] and a plaintive [[timbre]] in the track, which was noted for being her first collaboration with a rapper. Its come-hither lyrics have intensity, psychodrama, self-confidence, and female empowerment as main themes, dealing with pleasing her lover and being the perfect complement for him. Rocky raps his verse at the end of the song, which is written from a male, player perspective and features braggadocio, profanity, and sexual innuendos.

"Good for You" received acclaim from music critics, who praised its minimalistic production, sex appeal and Gomez's vocals, as well as her maturity and artistic growth; some of them, however, had mixed reactions towards Rocky's contribution. Reviewers also compared her vocals to those of [[Lana Del Rey]], [[Imogen Heap]] and [[Lorde]], while noting similarities to the works of Del Rey; it was later included in many year-end lists of best songs. Commercially, the song reached the top twenty of countries as Australia, Austria, Canada, Denmark, France, New Zealand, and Spain, as well as Czech Republic and Slovakia's digital charts. In the United States, it debuted at number nine on the [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]] and later peaked at number five, becoming her highest-charting single until 2019 when "[[Lose You to Love Me]]" topped the chart, as well reaching [[Pop Songs]]' summit, where it became her first song to do so.

Its accompanying music video was directed by [[Sophie Muller]] and premiered on [[Times Square]] screens on June 26, 2015. The project uses a new and slower version of the song, which omits Rocky's verse and features [[string instrument]]ation. Highlighted for its provocative imagery, it follows a low-key premise with Gomez wallowing on several vignettes wearing a number of casual outfits. A second version was later released using the explicit edition of the track and features Rocky rapping his verse with Gomez's scenes and dynamic visuals in the background; new shots also are present in this version. Both videos received positive feedback from critics, who praised Gomez's sensuality and the growth in her artistic imagery, describing the first as her "most intimate" to date and comparing it to Del Rey's works. To promote "Good for You", Gomez made interviews for radios and a few live performances in shows as ''[[Today (U.S. TV program)|Today]]'' and [[Taylor Swift]]'s [[The 1989 World Tour]] (2015), where she made its first live performance and served as a special guest.

==Writing and production==
[[File:A$AP Rocky UIT 2013 (cropped).jpg|thumb|left|upright|Besides featuring on "Good for You", rapper ASAP Rocky co-produced it with frequent collaborator Hector Delgado using the same production process from his albums.<ref name="MTV ASAP" />]]
[[Nick Monson]] booked a two-hour session with songwriters [[Julia Michaels]] and [[Justin Tranter]] to adjust one of their compositions. Finishing the adjustment in 45 minutes, they decided to use the remaining time to write another song so that they would not waste the money for the studio time they paid for. "Good for You" was subsequently written in 45 minutes.<ref name="Spin 3">{{cite web|last=Carley|first=Brennan|date=November 6, 2015|title=Justin Tranter's Written All of Your Favorite Pop Songs This Year|url=https://www.spin.com/2015/11/justin-tranter-interview-justin-bieber-gwen-stefani-selena-gomez-britney-spears/|work=[[Spin (magazine)|Spin]]|access-date=December 25, 2015}}</ref> It began with Monson playing its [[bassline]] and putting a snap on it before Michaels [[freestyle rap|freestyled]] the opening lyric, "I'm on my 14 carats".<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.songwriteruniverse.com/nick-monson-songwriter-2015.htm|title=Nick Monson Interview – Hit Songwriter & Producer|last=Kawashima|first=Dale|date=November 29, 2015|publisher=Songwriters Universe|access-date=December 25, 2015}}</ref> Her lyrics were influenced by her boyfriend who told her, "Julia, you never write any happy songs for me. It's kind of sad," to which she responded: "Fine, I'll write a good song about you, shut up!".<ref>{{cite web|last=Wood Rudulph|first=Heather|date=November 2, 2015|title=Get That Life: How I Became a Hit Songwriter by Age 21|url=https://www.cosmopolitan.com/career/interviews/a48541/get-that-life-julia-michaels-songwriter/|work=[[Cosmopolitan (magazine)|Cosmopolitan]]|access-date=December 25, 2015}}</ref> Tranter's lyrics were inspired by their ex-boyfriend who preferred them to dress femme.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.newnownext.com/singer-songwriter-justin-tranter-talks-about-the-queer-meaning-behind-selena-gomezs-good-for-you/12/2015/|title=Songwriter Justin Tranter Talks Injecting His Queer Point Of View Into Selena Gomez's 'Good For You'|last=Katz|first=Evan Ross|date=December 8, 2015|publisher=[[NewNowNext Awards]]|access-date=December 25, 2015}}</ref> Upon hearing "Good for You", the trio's [[A&R]] told them that the song should be recorded by Selena Gomez, despite Tranter initially thinking the song was "too indie" for her.<ref name="Spin 3" /> The A&R sent the track to Gomez who "freaked out", deeming it the artistic direction she had envisioned for her second studio album, ''[[Revival (Selena Gomez album)|Revival]]'' (2015).<ref name="Spin 3" /> She described the song as "a piece that was just everything I feel a woman should embody, but not in the obvious way"<ref name="MTV News 2">{{cite web|author=Garibaldi, Christina|url=http://www.mtv.com/news/2193213/selena-gomez-good-for-you-recording/|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150623220439/http://www.mtv.com/news/2193213/selena-gomez-good-for-you-recording/|url-status=dead|archive-date=June 23, 2015|title=You Won't Believe How Long It Took Selena Gomez To Record 'Good For You'|publisher=[[MTV News]]|date=June 23, 2015|access-date=June 26, 2015}}</ref> and explained to [[iHeartRadio]]: "I think it just sets the tone perfect for the record just because it's sexy, but not trying too hard. ==Commercial performance==
{{quote box|align=left|width=24%|quote="As an artist, you never know how people will respond to something you've created and that is very personal to you. I cannot thank enough everyone at all the radio stations and my fans for their support and joining me in this next chapter.|source=—Gomez talking about the rise of "Good for You" to Pop Songs chart's summit<ref name="Popsongs" />}}
In the United States, "Good for You" entered the [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]] issued for July 11, 2015, at number nine, marking Gomez's highest career debut and third top-ten single, following "[[Come & Get It (Selena Gomez song)|Come & Get It]]" (2013), "[[The Heart Wants What It Wants]]" (2014), and Rocky's second following "[[Fuckin' Problems]]" (2012). It became the second song to debut within the chart's top ten in 2015, after [[Maroon 5]]'s "[[Sugar (Maroon 5 song)|Sugar]]"; and was the first top-ten debut by a female artist since [[Taylor Swift]]'s "[[Shake It Off (Taylor Swift song)|Shake It Off]]" (2014). "Good for You" also debuted at number one on the [[Digital Songs]] chart with first-week sales of 179,000 copies—the best sales week in Gomez's career for a single and both hers and Rocky's first number one single on the chart. It was the chart's first number-one debut since Swift's "[[Blank Space]]" (2014).<ref name="Trust">{{cite magazine|author=Trust, Gary|url=https://www.billboard.com/pro/wiz-khalifa-no-1-hot-100-selena-gomez-debuts/|title=Wiz Khalifa No. 1 on Hot 100 'Again', Selena Gomez Debuts at No. 9|magazine=Billboard|date=July 1, 2015|access-date=July 2, 2015}}</ref> Weeks after its debut on the Hot 100, "Good for You" then reached number six.<ref>{{Cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/pro/omi-no-1-hot-100-sixth-week/|magazine=Billboard|title=OMI No. 1 on Hot 100 for Sixth Week|first=Gary|last=Trust|date=August 24, 2015|access-date=August 24, 2015}}</ref> After slipping one position, it rebounded to number five, its best position on the chart. It became the first top-five single on the Hot 100 for both Gomez and Rocky.<ref>{{Cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/pro/the-weeknd-replaces-himself-hot-100-the-hills-no-1/|magazine=Billboard|title=The Weeknd Replaces Himself Atop Hot 100 as 'The Hills' Hits No. 1|first=Gary|last=Trust|date=September 21, 2015|access-date=September 21, 2015}}</ref><ref name="hot100" /> For Gomez, "[[Same Old Love]]" matched its number-five peak in January 2016, and both remained her highest-charting singles until "[[Lose You to Love Me]]" reached number one in November 2019. For Rocky, "Good For You" remained his highest-peaking single to date until "[[No Limit (G-Eazy song)|No Limit]]" rose one position higher to number four in January 2018. "Good for You" entered the [[Mainstream Top 40]] chart at number twenty-one as the chart's highest debut and greatest gainer for the week ending July 11,<ref>{{cite magazine|author=Trust, Gary|url=https://www.billboard.com/pro/chart-highlights-taylor-swift-adds-another-no-1-with-style/|title=Chart Highlights: Taylor Swift Adds Another No. 1, With 'Style'|magazine=Billboard|date=June 29, 2015|access-date=July 4, 2015}}</ref> also debuting at 27 and 21 positions of [[Streaming Songs]] and [[Radio Songs (chart)|Radio Songs]] charts, respectively.<ref name="Trust" /> Later, it peaked at number four on Radio Songs and at number one on the Pop Songs chart  becoming her first song to reach the summit.<ref name="Popsongs" /><ref>{{cite magazine|url=http://www.billboard.com/artist/278617/selena-gomez/chart?f=350|title=Selena Gomez – Chart History – Radio Songs|magazine=Billboard|access-date=May 12, 2016|archive-date=March 11, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150311042951/http://www.billboard.com/artist/278617/selena-gomez/chart?f=350|url-status=dead}}</ref> Gomez had previously peaked as high as number two on Pop Songs with "Come & Get It" in June 2013.<ref name="Popsongs">{{cite magazine|author=Trust, Gary|url=https://www.billboard.com/pro/selena-gomez-scores-first-pop-songs-no-1-with-good-for-you/|title=Selena Gomez Scores First Pop Songs No. 1 With 'Good for You'|magazine=Billboard|date=September 14, 2015|access-date=September 14, 2015}}</ref> It was certified triple platinum by the [[Recording Industry Association of America]] (RIAA),<ref name="RIAA" /> selling 1.6 million copies in the United States as of May 2017.<ref name="sales">{{cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/pro/ask-billboard-selena-gomezs-career-album-song-sales/|title=Ask Billboard: Selena Gomez's Career Album & Song Sales|last=Trust|first=Gary|magazine=Billboard|publisher=Billboard|date=May 14, 2017|access-date=May 15, 2017}}</ref>

==Music videos==
===Background and release===
{{Quote box|width=30%|align=right|quote="There's no love interest in the video, it's all me. The reason why I wanted it to feel that way is because it's about truly feeling good about yourself. I love the sensuality. It doesn't try too hard. It's not in everybody's face. It's like when you have that feeling with someone, when you just want to feel pretty and you want to feel good. Whatever it is, whether it's in jeans and a t-shirt, or someone else's shirt. Just something that makes you feel beautiful and sexy."|source=—Gomez on the music video's concept<ref name="KAMP-FM" />}}
The accompanying music video for "Good for You" was directed by [[Sophie Muller]].<ref name="Entertainment Weekly">{{cite magazine|author=Goodman, Jessica|url=https://www.ew.com/article/2015/06/26/selena-gomez-good-you-music-video|title=Selena Gomez's Good For You video doesn't feature A$AP Rocky|magazine=Entertainment Weekly|date=June 26, 2015|access-date=June 29, 2015}}</ref> In the video, Gomez wanted to represent her journey to womanhood and to promote [[Girl power|female empowerment]] — which she felt strongly about and wanted to depict in a raw and vulnerable way.<ref name="iHeart" /><ref>{{cite news|author=Van De Wall, Virginia|url=http://www.j-14.com/posts/selena-gomez-releases-good-for-you-music-video-preview-61395|title=Selena Gomez Releases 'Good For You' Music Video Preview|work=[[J-14 (magazine)|J-14]]|date=June 22, 2015|access-date=June 29, 2015}}</ref> With the clip, she wanted to prove that young women should not be self-conscious of their sex appeal or be obliged to wear lingerie to feel attractive. "I feel like people here, especially guys are like, 'Ah, lingerie and sexy.' I think being sexy as a young woman or girl, is not caring what other people think, wearing like casual things, feeling sexy".<ref name="iHeart" /> Gomez felt it was important to collaborate with a female director as a means of female empowerment and wanted the visual to be raw and vulnerable. ===Synopsis===
[[File:Good for You music video.jpeg|thumb|left|alt=A portrait of a young brunette woman taking a shower.|A screenshot from the music video, noted for its provocative imagery and manifestation of Gomez's artistic maturity.]]
The music video for "Good for You" follows a restrained,<ref name="Idolator Video" /> low-key,<ref name="CBS Corporation">{{cite web|author=Carlin, Shannon|url=http://radio.com/2015/06/26/selena-gomez-good-for-you-video/|title=Selena Gomez Strips Down For 'Good For You' Video|publisher=CBS Corporation|date=June 26, 2015|access-date=June 28, 2015}}</ref> straightforward premise;<ref name="Billboard" /> it was shot on a basic set<ref name="Teen Vogue" /> and focuses solely on Gomez.<ref name="Entertainment Weekly" /><ref name="Cosmopolitan" /> Emilee Lindner of MTV News summarized the clip as "a full three minutes of Selena's face".<ref name="MTV Newsmv">{{cite web|author=Lindner, Emilee|url=http://www.mtv.com/news/2197217/selena-gomez-good-for-you-video/|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150627163039/http://www.mtv.com/news/2197217/selena-gomez-good-for-you-video/|url-status=dead|archive-date=June 27, 2015|title=Watch Selena Gomez's Steamy 'Good For You' Music Video|publisher=MTV News|date=June 26, 2015|access-date=June 27, 2015}}</ref> It has a minimalist theme of water.<ref name="People" /><ref name="Nylon">{{cite news|author=Gharnit, Yasmeen|url=http://www.nylon.com/articles/selena-gomez-good-for-you|title=Watch Selena Gomez's 'Good For You' Video|work=[[Nylon (magazine)|Nylon]]|date=June 26, 2015|access-date=June 28, 2015}}</ref> It also uses sexual imagery,<ref name="Us Weekly" /> contains several sexual innuendos,<ref name="Nylon" /> and features Gomez striking a number of provocative poses.<ref name="Yahoo!">{{cite web|author=Geller, Wendy|url=https://www.yahoo.com/music/selena-gomez-shows-a-little-suggests-a-lot-in-122507499221.html|title=Selena Gomez Shows a Little, Suggests a Lot in New 'Good for You' Video|publisher=[[Yahoo!]]|date=June 26, 2015|access-date=June 28, 2015}}</ref> Wendy Geller of [[Yahoo!]] said the poses were "PG-13, but still have a remarkably raunchy feel for not overtly showing anything".<ref name="Yahoo!" /> Although the song includes the lyric "hair real nice", Gomez chose to use a natural look throughout; she is apparently bra-less,<ref name="Yahoo!" /> has wavy hair, and wears minimal make-up.<ref name="E! Online" /> She changes her clothes several times during the clip; Casey Lewis of ''[[Teen Vogue]]'' said her outfits were "each more low-key than last".<ref name="Teen Vogue" />

The video begins with Gomez lying seductively on a bright, lime-green, velvet couch;<ref name="Cosmopolitan 2" /><ref name="InStyle" /><ref name="Los Angeles Times" /> wearing a loosely tied, robe-like fuchsia dress,<ref name="Cosmopolitan 2" /><ref name="The Huffington Post" /><ref name="Yahoo!" />  her eyes look sad and she is seemingly awaiting the arrival of her partner.<ref name="Billboard" /><ref name="The Fader" /> Gomez wears a  floral, pink and lavender dress.<ref name="InStyle" /> The video then alternates between scenes of Gomez writhing around on a hardwood floor in ripped denim and a white T-shirt.<ref name="Us Weekly" /><ref name="Rap-Up Video" /><ref name="MTV Newsmv" /><ref name="The Huffington Post" /> In the next scene, Gomez  undresses and takes a shower in [[slow-motion]]; though not explicitly nude,<ref name="E! Online" /><ref name="Rap-Up Video" /><ref name="MTV Newsmv" /> the scene focuses on her head and shoulders.<ref name="Yahoo!" /> Gomez is later depicted towering over a camera in a warehouse, before returning to scenes of her writhing around and expressing her inner longing.<ref name="Billboard" /> Gomez is also pictured with wet hair,<ref name="Daily News" /> sitting on a bar stool against a bare wall, wearing a wet white T-shirt;<ref name="Us Weekly" /> imagery reminiscent of single's cover art.<ref name="E! Online" /> It then alternates between a scene in which she wallows on an old-fashioned leather couch wearing a silk dress.<ref name="Us Weekly" /><ref name="Los Angeles Times" /><ref name="MTV Newsmv" /> Throughout the video, Gomez is pictured singing for the camera, often making direct eye contact with the viewer and seemingly lost in deep thought.<ref name="The Huffington Post" />

===Reception===

The music video garnered over 17 million views on [[YouTube]] within a week of its release,<ref>{{cite web|author=Malkin, Marc|url=https://uk.eonline.com/news/672867/selena-gomez-s-good-for-you-comes-out-on-top|title=Selena Gomez's Good For You Comes Out On Top|publisher=E!|date=July 2, 2015|access-date=July 4, 2015}}</ref> receiving 70 million views in the site within less than a month.<ref name="Teen Vogue Video 2" /><ref name="Billboard Video 2" /> According to Gary Trust of ''Billboard'', the release of the music video helped "Good for You" to debut at number 27 on the [[Streaming Songs]] chart, contributing to its first-week stream tally of 4.5 million in the United States.<ref name="Trust" /> Some critics compared the music video to the works of Lana Del Rey.<ref name="Out" /><ref name="New York" /> Writing in ''New York'', Dee Lockett said Gomez was "doing her best Lana Del Rey bombshell impression".<ref name="New York" />

A number of critics said the clip manifested growth in Gomez's artistic imagery.<ref name="E! Online" /><ref name="Us Weekly" /><ref name="Yahoo!" /> Wendy Geller of Yahoo! wrote, "If ever there were any doubts that Selena Gomez has moved on from her Disney roots, [the music video] should quickly dispel them".<ref name="Yahoo!" /> Similarly, Allison Corneau of ''[[Us Weekly]]'' said Gomez showed "a decidedly more adult side" and proved she was "far from the Disney princess we used to know".<ref name="Us Weekly" /> Dennis Hinzmann of ''[[Out (magazine)|Out]]'' said the visual showcased a continuation of Gomez redefining herself as an artist.<ref name="Out" /> Jeff Benjamin of [[Fuse (TV channel)|Fuse]] said the video "shed any Disney princess connection [Gomez] may have had with a steamy shower scene".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.fuse.tv/2015/08/fall-albums-2015-upcoming-music#25|title=35 Most Anticipated Fall 2015 Albums|last=Benjamin|first=Jeff|date=August 26, 2015|publisher=[[Fuse (TV channel)|Fuse]]|access-date=December 27, 2015}}</ref> Other critics said the video silenced then-recent [[hate speech]] Gomez had received, noting an underlying message in its premise.<ref name="Us Weekly" /><ref name="Cosmopolitan" /><ref name="UPI" /> Cydney Eckert of ''Cosmopolitan'' wrote, "While the video reveals a sexier side of the singer we haven't really seen since ''Spring Breakers'', it also showcases that same confidence that allows Selena to keep standing up to body-shamers and haters".<ref name="Cosmopolitan" /> This view was shared by Corneau, who said the clip was "no doubt part of [Gomez's] plan to silence the haters and live life on her own terms".<ref name="Us Weekly" />

Gomez's appearance and portrayal were highlighted by some reviewers. ''Rap-Up'' called it Gomez's most intimate video yet,<ref name="Rap-Up Video" /> while Idolator's Carl Williott called it "understated".<ref name="Idolator Video" /> Alyssa Bailey of ''[[Elle (magazine)|Elle]]'' said the clip was "basically an instruction on how to be hot" and proved "power in vulnerability".<ref>{{cite news|author=Bailey, Alyssa|url=https://www.elle.com/culture/music/news/a29060/selena-gomez-good-for-you-video/|title=Selena Gomez Is a Seriously Sexy Shower Singer in 'Good for You'|work=[[Elle (magazine)|Elle]]|date=June 26, 2015|access-date=June 26, 2015}}</ref> ''Teen Vogue'' writer Casey Lewis said, "But if she wants to look good for us, she certainly succeeds&nbsp;...&nbsp;Actually ''good'' is an understatement".<ref name="Teen Vogue" /> According to Geller, Gomez was as involved in the clip's overall intimacy as Muller, and her portrayal was "well-played".<ref name="Yahoo!" /> Geller lauded the visual as "a masterpiece of suggestion", saying that although Gomez's shower scene was "PG-13", it was "still sexy enough to make any respectable fan's tongue hang out".<ref name="Yahoo!" /> Francesca Bacardi of E! said Gomez should permanently choose her "absolutely gorgeous" natural look sported in the clip.<ref name="E! Online" />

The music video's representation of the song, and its exclusion of Rocky were also well received. Morgan Peterson of ''[[Harper's Bazaar]]'' said the visual saw Gomez "syncing up to her sultry new sound",<ref name="Harpers Bazaar" /> while [[MTV UK]] wrote that it "keeps true to the intense theme of the song".<ref name="MTV UK" /> Lockett said it reinstated the song's "titillating striptease" theme, and that Rocky's exclusion was the clip's biggest statement, "so that all eyes and ears are on Selena. 'Make you never wanna leave, indeed.'{{sp}}"<ref name="New York" /> Lockett's commendation of Rocky's exclusion was echoed by Jason Lipshutz of ''Billboard'' and Marjua Esteves of ''[[Vibe (magazine)|Vibe]]'', who both said his absence assisted the premise conceptually.<ref name="Billboard" /><ref>{{cite news|author=Esteves, Marjua|url=https://www.vibe.com/2015/06/selena-gomez-good-for-you-video/|title=Selena Gomez Takes A Sultry Shower In 'Good For You Video|work=[[Vibe (magazine)|Vibe]]|date=June 29, 2015|access-date=July 1, 2015}}</ref> In a less enthusiastic review, Yasmeen Gharnit of ''[[Nylon (magazine)|Nylon]]'' wrote, "While there's no exact formula for how Disney stars transition into full-fledged adults, it seems like it always involves a water and sexual innuendo-filled video".<ref name="Nylon" /> Gharnit said the visual "seems in equal parts too try-hard and unimaginative", and that it distracted from its intended stripped-down representation of the song.<ref name="Nylon" />
