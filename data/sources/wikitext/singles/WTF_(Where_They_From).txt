"'''WTF (Where They From)'''" is a song by American rapper [[Missy Elliott]] featuring American musician [[Pharrell Williams]]. Produced by Williams, it was released in 2015 as Elliott's first single and first music video, since 2008's "[[Shake Your Pom Pom]]", although she had been a featured artist on others' singles and videos and had released promotional singles, during the intervening years. The song received significant praise from critics, and was sometimes referred to as her "comeback".<ref>{{cite web|last=Boursicot|first=Jimmy|title=WTF (Where They From), the comeback of Missy Elliott|url=https://translate.google.com/translate?hl=en&sl=fr&u=http://www.radio-monaco.com/music-tour/item/8316-wtf-where-they-from-le-come-back-de-missy-elliott-avec-pharrell-williams&prev=search|work=Radio Monaco: Music & News|publisher=Radio Monaco|date=12 November 2015|access-date=15 November 2015}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|last=Rivieccio|first=Genna|title=The Merciful Return of Missy Elliott|url=http://www.culledculture.com/the-merciful-return-of-missy-elliott/|work=Culled Culture|date=14 November 2015|access-date=15 November 2014}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|title=Missy Elliott Creates a Frenzy with New Video|url=http://www.thejvcoolfiles.com/#!Missy-Elliott-Creates-a-Frenzy-with-New-Video/fv3li/564514f20cf2708e001825dc|publisher=TheJVcoolFiles.com|date=12 November 2015|access-date=15 November 2015|archive-date=17 November 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151117015245/http://www.thejvcoolfiles.com/#!Missy-Elliott-Creates-a-Frenzy-with-New-Video/fv3li/564514f20cf2708e001825dc|url-status=dead}}</ref>

==Background==
Pick for a lead single of Elliott's seventh album stretches back as early 2008, when Elliott coined the soundtrack single "[[Ching-a-Ling]]" as a potential selection.<ref>{{cite web|title=Missy Elliot Invites Fans To Name New Album|url=http://thehypefactor.com/missy-elliot-invites-fans-to-name-new-album/|work=The Hype Factory|date=6 February 2008|access-date=12 November 2015}}</ref> However, by summer of that same year, Elliott decided against the idea and released the buzz record "[[Best, Best]]" instead.<ref>{{cite web|title=New Missy Elliott - "Best, Best"|url=http://www.stereogum.com/10417/new_missy_elliot_-_best_best/mp3s/|work=Stereogum|date=13 June 2008|access-date=12 November 2015}}</ref> With "Best" servicing to minimal reaction nationwide, Elliott then entered recording sessions with recurring collaborator [[Pharrell Williams]] throughout the first quarter of 2009, as effort to produce a stronger radio single.<ref name="yt"/> The result was the [[Teyana Taylor]]-featured "Put It on Ya", which like its predecessor failed to gain commercial appeal; but managed to cite limited promo attention in European regions.<ref name="yt">{{cite web|title=Missy Elliott feat. Pharrell & Teyana - Put it on you High Quality <nowiki>[NEW SONG]</nowiki>|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=waVC-c0a_Ac|publisher=[[YouTube]]|date=19 May 2009|access-date=13 November 2015}}</ref>

In 2012, in between both battling [[Graves' disease]] and contributing production for protégées [[Jazmine Sullivan]] and [[Sharaya J]], Elliott returned to the making of her seventh album, and recruited childhood friend [[Timbaland]] to materialize the dual-promotional efforts "[[9th Inning]]" and "[[Triple Threat (Missy Elliott song)|Triple Threat]]".<ref>{{cite web|last=Kelly|first=Chris|title=Lucky Sevens: Missy Elliott and Timbaland's greatest collaborations|url=http://www.factmag.com/2012/09/21/lucky-sevens-missy-elliott-and-timbalands-greatest-collaborations/|work=FACT Magazine|date=21 September 2012|access-date=13 November 2015}}</ref> While the songs met with lukewarm success on the digital charts, the releases failed to amass the proper impact needed to revive the "energy" of Elliott's seventh studio set. Elliott additionally had plans to release the [[Lil' Kim]]- and [[Eve (rapper)|Eve]]-assisted "Murder She Wrote" as a follow-up single, however plans were ultimately shelved.<ref>{{cite web|title=Eve Talks Missy Elliott, Lil' Kim Collaboration|url=http://www.rap-up.com/2013/02/23/eve-talks-missy-elliott-lil-kim-collaboration/|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130501220051/http://www.rap-up.com/2013/02/23/eve-talks-missy-elliott-lil-kim-collaboration/|url-status=dead|archive-date=May 1, 2013|work=[[Rap-Up (magazine)|Rap-Up]]|date=23 February 2013|access-date=13 November 2015}}</ref>

By late April 2014, reports surfaced of Elliott returning to the studio with Pharrell, in preparation of a potential collaboration.<ref>{{cite web|title=Are Pharrell & Missy Elliott Getting Their Musical Freak On?|url=http://perezhilton.com/2014-04-28-pharrell-and-missy-elliott-tease-collaboration-on-twitter/?from=post#.VkV51fmrTIU|publisher=[[Perez Hilton]]|date=28 April 2014|access-date=12 November 2015|archive-date=17 November 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151117021827/http://perezhilton.com/2014-04-28-pharrell-and-missy-elliott-tease-collaboration-on-twitter/?from=post#.VkV51fmrTIU|url-status=dead}}</ref> Further news circulated of the pair's work coming to light, when in April 2015, Pharrell alluded to [[Jimmy Fallon]] that new Elliott material was on the way and concluded that he "was so proud of her."<ref>{{cite web|last=Daly|first=Rhian|title=Pharrell confirms he is working with Missy Elliott|url=http://www.nme.com/news/pharrell-williams/84229|work=[[New Musical Express]]|publisher=NME.com|date=5 April 2015|access-date=12 November 2015}}</ref>

On October 26, 2015, Missy Elliott debuted a snippet of the Pharrell-produced cut "WTF" during an airing of ''[[Monday Night Football]]''.<ref name="radio">{{cite web|last=Cornell|first=Jeff|title=Missy Elliott Debuts Snippet of "WTF" during Monday Night Football|url=http://radio.com/2015/10/26/missy-elliott-debuts-wtf/|publisher=[[Radio.com]]|date=26 October 2015|access-date=13 November 2015}}</ref> Originally set for a release on November 13,<ref name="radio"/> "WTF" was released to digital outlets on November 12 at 9 a.m. EST, alongside the simultaneous release of its music video.<ref>{{cite web|last=Jones|first=Jaleesa M.|title=Missy Elliott drops new video, shocks world|url=https://www.usatoday.com/story/life/entertainthis/2015/11/12/missy-elliott-new-video-wtf-where-they-from/75641270/|work=[[USA Today]]|date=13 November 2015|access-date=13 November 2015}}</ref>

==Composition==
"WTF (Where They From)" is a futuristic up-tempo number that incorporates a "drum line eseque" pattern and a [[Trap music (hip hop)|trap]] backdrop.<ref>{{cite web|last=Hopf|first=Andrew|title='No exaggeration,' Missy 'Misdemeanor' Elliott is back|url=http://alleghenycampus.com/12566/features/no-exaggeration-missy-misdemeanor-elliott-is-back/|work=The Campus|date=12 November 2015|access-date=15 November 2015}}</ref> The song's entirety also features a female voice muttering the phrase "in person," which derives from [[Trayvon Martin]]'s friend Rachel Jeantel, who uttered the wording in a 2013 interview with Huffpost Live.

==Critical reception==
"WTF (Where They From)" received widespread praise from music critics. Maeve McDermott form ''[[USA Today]]'' named it "Song of the Week" and wrote that "after becoming a household name with a decade of classic records before stepping back for the next ten-year stretch, Missy sounds triumphant on "WTF", serving her classic sing-song braggadocio and figure-praising lyrics alongside a guest appearance from Pharrell."<ref>{{cite web|last=McDermotttitle|first=Maeve|title=Song of the week: Missy Elliott's back with 'WTF'|url=https://www.usatoday.com/story/life/music/2015/11/13/song-of-the-week-missy-elliott-wtf-where-they-from/75643640/|work=[[USA Today]]|date=13 November 2015|access-date=13 November 2015}}</ref> ''[[The Guardian]]'' writer Lanre Bakare summed: "What it is a welcome bit of weird, fun pop that exists in its own pristine bubble. [[Get Ur Freak On]]."<ref name="guardian">{{cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/music/2015/nov/12/missy-elliott-wtf-where-they-from-released|title=Missy Elliott: She's back (maybe) and still like no one else |first=Lanre|last=Bakare|work=[[The Guardian]]|date=November 12, 2013|access-date=November 12, 2013}}</ref> In his review for the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'', Gerrick D. Kennedy wrote that "the new jam is Missy at her best. The beat is as off-kilter and futuristically funky as could be expected from a track with her name on it, and the lyrics are a barrage of tongue-twisty, nonsensical punches".<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.latimes.com/entertainment/music/posts/la-et-ms-missy-elliott-returns-to-form-with-wtf-where-they-from-20151112-story.html|title=Missy Elliott returns to form with 'WTF (Where They From)' |first=Gerrick D.|last=Kennedy |work=[[Los Angeles Times]]|date=November 12, 2013|access-date=November 12, 2013}}</ref> Spencer Kornhaber from ''[[The Atlantic]]'' found that "no one is as reliably able and willing to start a party as Missy [...] She’s not buying melodies from Swedish pop geniuses in attempts to conquer multiple radio formats; she’s not lugging around a Narrative. She’s pure rhythm + attitude, an equation for joy. So it is on “WTF,” with its earthquaking low end and catchiness-through-elongated-syllables. Pharrell, another rap-master of body music, shows up for a verse that fits nicely into the nonsensical tapestry."<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.latimes.com/entertainment/music/posts/la-et-ms-missy-elliott-returns-to-form-with-wtf-where-they-from-20151112-story.html|title=Joy to the World, Missy Elliott Is Here |first=Spencer |last=Kornhaber |work=[[The Atlantic]]|date=November 12, 2013|access-date=November 12, 2013}}</ref> ''[[Pitchfork Media]]'' awarded the song with their "Best New Track" tag, praising Elliott's comeback, the track's production and lyrical criticism of [[cultural appropriation]],<ref>{{cite web|url=http://pitchfork.com/reviews/tracks/17821-missy-elliott-wtf-where-they-from-ft-pharrell|title=Missy Elliott "WTF (Where They From)" [ft. Pharrell]|website=Pitchfork|date=November 13, 2015|access-date=May 23, 2016}}</ref> and eventually naming the track the 30th best of 2015.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://pitchfork.com/features/lists-and-guides/9765-the-100-best-tracks-of-2015/?page=8|title=The 100 Best Tracks of 2015|work=Pitchfork|date=December 14, 2015|access-date=October 8, 2016}}</ref>

Alexa Camp, author for ''[[Slant (magazine)|Slant]]'' magazine, wrote that the song "pairs Pharrell's paint-can beats with deep, sinuous 808s, and if the track lacks an obvious hook, it makes up for it with sheer swagger. Of course, Missy doesn't miss a beat, spitting rhymes like it's 2005."<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.slantmagazine.com/house/article/review-missy-elliott-drops-new-single-music-video-wtf-where-they-from|title=Review: Missy Elliott Drops New Single & Music Video "WTF (Where They From)"|first=Alexa |last=Camp |work=[[Slant (magazine)|Slant]]|date=November 12, 2013|access-date=November 12, 2013}}</ref> Similarly, ''[[MTV News]]''{{'}}s Amanda Bell found that "the new Missy track is slick and stylish and all the things that made Missy slay back in the day (and now too, yay!)."<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.mtv.com/news/2426820/missy-elliott-wtf-miley-cyrus/|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151113192022/http://www.mtv.com/news/2426820/missy-elliott-wtf-miley-cyrus/|url-status=dead|archive-date=November 13, 2015|title=Here's Why People Think Missy Elliott's 'WTF' Is Calling Out Miley Cyrus|first=Amanda |last=Bell|work=[[MTV News]]|date=November 12, 2013|access-date=November 12, 2013}}</ref> Jon Blistein from ''[[Rolling Stone]]'' felt that "Elliott and Pharrell's [provide] infectious flows over the track's intergalactic production."<ref name="rs">{{cite magazine|first=Jon|last=Blistein|title=Missy Elliott, Pharrell Spin Heads in Wild, Futuristic 'WTF' Video|magazine=[[Rolling Stone]]|url=https://www.rollingstone.com/music/news/missy-elliott-pharrell-spin-heads-in-wild-futuristic-wtf-video-20151112|date=November 12, 2013|access-date=November 12, 2013}}</ref> The prestigious ''[[Village Voice]]'' named "WTF (Where They From)" the sixth-best single released in 2015 on their annual year-end critics' poll, [[Pazz & Jop]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://furia.com/pjs/table_song_2015_total.html|title=Pazz & Jop Statistics|publisher=The Village Voice|access-date=January 13, 2016}}</ref>

==Music video==
===Filming background===
[[Image:Neon art inside the Pershing Square Metro subway station in downtown Los Angeles, California LCCN2013631610.tif|thumb|Parts of the music video for "WTF (Where They From)" were filmed at the [[Pershing Square (Los Angeles Metro station)|Pershing Square]].<ref name="yt2"/>]]
News of the video's formation first broke the air by [[TMZ]] in October 2015.<ref>{{cite web|title=WTF<nowiki>?</nowiki> Missy Elliott New Music Video|url=http://www.tmz.com/2015/10/08/missy-elliott-new-music-video/|publisher=[[TMZ]]|date=8 October 2015|access-date=13 November 2015}}</ref> The critically acclaimed "futuristic" visual features extensive directing control by Elliott's frequent collaborator [[Dave Meyers (director)|Dave Meyers]], alongside hyper-paced choreography by longtime friend [[Hi-Hat (choreographer)|Hi-Hat]]. Shooting locations included [[Downtown Los Angeles]], [[Westlake, Los Angeles]], [[Pershing Square (Los Angeles Metro station)]], and an "under-entrance" near [[Sixth Street Viaduct]].

===Synopsis===
The [[Dave Meyers (director)|Dave Meyers]]-directed piece opens with [[Downtown Los Angeles]] residents singing along to Elliott's "WTF" while going about their usual routines. The video then takes the viewers' attention to a glitzy-disco-cloaked Elliott dancing along an abandoned alleyway, before transitioning to rapid-paced choreography by Elliott and her background dancers. Elliott is then quickly shown rapping to the camera, where she begins to morph a cheetah-print tattoo from one side of her face to the other.

Moments later, a segment follows with Williams and Elliott as dancing [[marionettes]] rapping their verses. The remainder of the video then intercuts with Elliott and headlamp-sporting others dancing upon open boxes and moving hoverboards. Guest appearances throughout the visual's entirety comprises [[Les Twins]] and Missy Elliott's protégée [[Sharaya J]].<ref name="yt2" /> The marionettes were designed by the New York based Furry Puppet Studio.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2015-11-12 |title=Missy Elliott's Back - Furry Puppet Studio |url=https://www.furrypuppet.com/blog/missy-elliott-wtf-marionettes/ |access-date=2026-06-09 |language=en-US}}</ref> 

===Reception===
Upon its release, the video received general acclaim from critics. ''[[The Guardian]]'' writer Lanre Bakare felt that "her first music video in seven years demonstrates that pop has missed Missy’s unique mix of street culture, futuristic imagery and off-kilter production." He summed it as "instantly recognizable [and] full of daft choreography".<ref name="guardian"/> Additionally, less than 9 hours after its initial premiere, the video accumulated over 3 million views via YouTube.<ref name="yt2">{{cite web|title=Missy Elliott - WTF (Where They From) ft. Pharrell Williams <nowiki>[Official Video]</nowiki>|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KO_3Qgib6RQ|publisher=[[YouTube]]|date=12 November 2015|access-date=13 November 2015}}</ref>
