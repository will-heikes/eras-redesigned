"'''TKO'''" is a song by American singer [[Justin Timberlake]] from his fourth studio album, ''[[The 20/20 Experience – 2 of 2]]'' (2013). It was written and produced by Timberlake, [[Timothy "Timbaland" Mosley]] and [[Jerome "J-Roc" Harmon]], with additional writing from [[James Fauntleroy]] and a sample of [[Barry White]]'s "Somebody's Gonna Off the Man". A remix by rappers [[J. Cole]], [[ASAP Rocky]], and [[Pusha T]], subtitled the "Black Friday Remix", was also released. Lyrically, it uses boxing metaphors to describe the feeling of seeing a former lover with someone else.

[[RCA Records]] released "TKO" on September 20, 2013, as the second single from ''The 20/20 Experience – 2 of 2''. It received mixed reviews from music critics and entered the top 40 on the US [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]], where it reached number thirty-six. The song was eventually certified platinum by [[Pro-Música Brasil]] (PMB) and gold by [[Music Canada]] and [[Grammofonleverantörernas förening]] (GLF). The accompanying music video, directed by Ryan Reichenfeld, was released on October 29, 2013, and stars [[Elvis Presley]]'s granddaughter [[Riley Keough]] as Timberlake's love interest.

==Release and promotion==
According to [[RCA Records]], the label perceived much more hit potential in "[[Not a Bad Thing]]" (2014); however, they decided to release "[[Take Back the Night (song)|Take Back the Night]]" (2013) and "TKO" as pre-album singles, as they felt it best represented the album's R&B side. Released a year after the just-as-pop-heavy "[[Mirrors (Justin Timberlake song)|Mirrors]]" (2013), "Not A Bad Thing" became a top-ten hit.<ref>{{cite web|last=Trust|first=Gary|date=April 23, 2014|url=https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/pharrell-williams-still-atop-hot-100-justin-timberlake-chris-6062908/|title=Pharrell Williams Still Atop Hot 100; Justin Timberlake, Chris Brown Reach Top 10|work=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|access-date=July 10, 2026}}</ref>

==Reception==
Upon release, "TKO" garnered mixed reviews from critics. Supporters appreciated the presence of the "unique Timberlake charm" and Timbaland style of R&B production.<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Dixon |first=Josh |title=Single Review: Justin Timberlake – 'TKO' |url=https://renownedforsound.com/single-review-justin-timberlake-tko/ |access-date=December 18, 2025 |magazine=[[Renowned for Sound]]}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |last=Bakare |first=Lanre |date=September 26, 2013 |title=Justin Timberlake: The 20/20 Experience Part 2 – review |url=https://www.theguardian.com/music/2013/sep/26/justin-timberlake-the-20-20-experience-part-2 |access-date=July 10, 2026 |newspaper=[[The Guardian]]}}</ref> Underwhelmed and disappointed reviewers, such as [[Annie Zaleski]], criticized the monotony and a lack of evolution in Timbaland's production, and the usage of "tired" and "predictable" boxing cliches in the lyrics.<ref name="Cinquemani-2013">{{cite magazine |last=Cinquemani |first=Sal |date=September 20, 2013 |title=Single Review: Justin Timberlake, "TKO" |url=https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/single-review-justin-timberlake-tko/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140122053352/http://www.slantmagazine.com/house/2013/20/single-review-justin-timberlake-tko |archive-date=January 22, 2014 |access-date=September 23, 2013 |magazine=[[Slant Magazine]]}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |last=Zaleski |first=Annie |date=October 1, 2013 |title=Justin Timberlake: The 20/20 Experience—2 of 2 &#124; Music |url=https://www.avclub.com/justin-timberlake-the-20-20-experience-2-of-2-1798178140 |access-date=October 2, 2013 |newspaper=[[The A.V. In North America, "TKO" peaked at number thirty-six on the US [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]]<ref name="Billboardhot100" /> and number nine on the US [[Hot R&B/Hip-Hop Songs]] chart.<ref name="Billboardrandbhiphop" /> In terms of airplay, it peaked at number thirty-six on [[Adult Pop Airplay]],<ref name="Billboardadultpopsongs" /> number twelve on [[Pop Airplay]],<ref name="Billboardpopsongs" /> and number ten on [[Rhythmic Airplay]].<ref name="Billboardrhythmic" /> It peaked at number twenty-eight on the [[Canadian Hot 100]].<ref name="Canada" /> On its component airplay charts, it peaked at number ten on [[Canada CHR/Top 40]]<ref name="Billboardcanadachrtop40" /> and number thirty-four on [[Canada Hot AC]].<ref name="Billboardcanadahotac" /> It was certified platinum by [[Music Canada]].<ref name="MC" /> In South America, the song was certified platinum by [[Pro-Música Brasil]] (PMB).<ref name="PMB" />

In Europe, "TKO" peaked at numbers five and thirteen on the bubbling under charts in the Flanders and Wallonia regions of Belgium,<ref name="Flanders Tip" /><ref name="Wallonia Tip" /> but peaked at number twenty-three on the former's urban chart.<ref name="Flanders Urban" /> It also peaked at number four in Bulgaria,<ref name="Bulgaria" /> number one hundred sixty-three in France,<ref name="France" /> number seventy-one in Germany,<ref name="Germany" /> number fifty-one on the [[Irish Singles Chart]],<ref name="Ireland" /> number thirteen in the Netherlands,<ref name="Netherlands" /> number sixty-eight in Switzerland,<ref name="Switzerland" /> number fifty-eight on the [[UK Singles Chart]],<ref name="UK" /> and number fifteen on the [[UK Hip Hop and R&B Singles Chart]].<ref name="UKrandb" /> It was certified gold by [[Grammofonleverantörernas förening]] (GLF).<ref name="GLF" />

In Asia, "TKO" peaked at number thirteen in Lebanon<ref name="Lebanon" /> and numbers one hundred fifty-five and five on [[Gaon Chart]]'s digital and international charts in South Korea.<ref name="Gaon Digital Chart" /><ref name="Gaon International Chart" /> In Oceania, the song peaked at number twenty-four on the urban chart in Australia.<ref name="Australiaurban" />
