"'''Sledgehammer'''" is a song recorded by American girl group [[Fifth Harmony]] for their first full-length studio album, ''[[Reflection (Fifth Harmony album)|Reflection]]'' (2015). The song was released on October 28, 2014 by [[Epic Records]] and [[Syco Music]] as the record's second [[single (music)|single]]. It was written by [[Jonas Jeberg]], [[Meghan Trainor]] and [[Sean Douglas (songwriter)|Sean Douglas]], and produced by Jeberg and [[Harvey Mason Jr.]] Musically, "Sledgehammer" is a [[synthpop]] song with influences of [[electronic dance music]], [[New wave music|new wave]] and 1980s pop music. Its lyrics utilize a [[sledgehammer]] metaphor to describe the effects of being [[Infatuation|infatuated]].

[[Music journalism|Music critics]] generally gave positive reviews of the song, praising its production. Commercially, "Sledgehammer" peaked at number 40 on the [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]], making it Fifth Harmony's first top 40 entry in the United States. It was eventually certified [[Platinum (music sales)|Platinum]] by the [[Recording Industry Association of America]] (RIAA) for sales of over one million copies. The song also reached the top 40 in New Zealand, The Czech Republic and Slovakia.

An accompanying [[music video]] for the track was choreographed and directed by [[Fatima Robinson]]. It features silhouette visuals and the members swinging on a scaffold, among other scenes. Critics noted the video's simple aesthetics as a growth in the group's visuals. Fifth Harmony performed the song live at the [[2014 MTV Europe Music Awards]], ''[[Good Morning America]]'', ''[[The Talk (talk show)|The Talk]]'', ''[[Today (American TV program)|Today Show]]'' and ''[[The Ellen DeGeneres Show]]''.

"Sledgehammer" is a heavy [[synthpop]] production backed by an [[electronic dance music]] (EDM) beat, taking influence from [[New wave music|new wave]] and 1980s music.<ref name="PreReleaseMTV"/><ref name="Credits1" /><ref name="musictimes"/><ref name="BillboardReview"/> Lyrically, the song expresses the physical sensations of being infatuated, comparing it to a sledgehammer motif: "If you take my pulse right now, it would feel just like a sledgehammer."<ref name=spin>{{cite magazine|url=http://www.spin.com/2015/02/fifth-harmony-reflection/|title=Review: Fifth Harmony Harness Pop's Gooey, Feel-Good Core on 'Reflection'|magazine= [[Spin (magazine)|Spin]]|author= Brennan Carley|date= February 3, 2015|access-date= June 15, 2017|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20180302214313/https://www.spin.com/2015/02/fifth-harmony-reflection/|archivedate= March 2, 2018}}</ref> Jessica Hyndman of [[MTV]] suggested that the song is about a kind of love that just "can't be contained".<ref name=PreReleaseMTV /> According to music sheet published at Musicnotes.com by [[BMG Rights Management]], "Sledgehammer" is written in the key of [[G major]] and is set in a {{music|time|4|4}} [[common time]] at 100 [[Tempo|bpm]] with an upbeat [[pop music|pop]] melody. The quintet's vocals span from G<sub>3</sub> to F<sub>5</sub> and follow a [[chord progression]] of C–G–D–C–E<sub>m</sub>–D.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.musicnotes.com/sheetmusic/mtd.asp?ppn=MN0146506|title= Sledgehammer by Fifth Harmony - Music Sheet|publisher= Musicnotes.com|author= BMG Rights Management|author-link= BMG Rights Management|date= October 28, 2014|accessdate= March 27, 2017}}</ref>

==Reception==
===Critical===
"Sledgehammer" received generally positive reviews from [[Music journalism|music critics]]. Jason Lipshutz of ''[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]'' praised the song's "EDM-meets-new-wave beat" and its "immediate hook", believing that "Sledgehammer" is "the best pop track about the physical effects of lovesickness since [[Demi Lovato]]'s "[[Heart Attack (Demi Lovato song)|Heart Attack]]" (2013)". It was tied as the best ranked song of the week, sharing the honor with "[[Don't It]]" (2014) by [[Country music|country]] singer [[Billy Currington]].<ref name=BillboardReview>{{cite magazine|url=http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/6312076/the-best-and-worst-singles-of-the-week-from-fifth-harmony-to-spandau-ballet|title=The Best and Worst Singles of the Week - From Fifth Harmony to Spandau Ballet|magazine=Billboard|author= Jason Lipshutz|date=November 7, 2014|accessdate=December 4, 2014|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20180302215344/https://www.billboard.com/articles/news/6312076/the-best-and-worst-singles-of-the-week-from-fifth-harmony-to-spandau-ballet|archivedate= March 2, 2018}}</ref> Mike Wass of [[Idolator (website)|Idolator]] called it "a sugary sweet synth-pop anthem with serious radio appeal".<ref name=Credits1 /> Jessica Hyndman from MTV noted that "'Sledgehammer' takes on a fun pop vibe" that lacked in the group's previous single, "Boss".<ref name=PreReleaseMTV /> A reviewer from ''[[Teen Vogue]]'' deemed the track as "catchy",<ref>{{cite magazine|url=http://www.teenvogue.com/story/fifth-harmony-sledehammer-single-reflection|title= Oh Hi, Fun New Fifth Harmony Song! "Sledgehammer" Might Just Be Their Best and Buzziest Track Yet|magazine= [[Teen Vogue]]|author= Tehrene Firman|date= October 30, 2014|access-date= June 15, 2017}}</ref> while Carolyn Menyes from Music Times gave "Sledgehammer" a mixed review. She praised the group's performance, commending that their distinctive voices sound "perfectly", but expressed that the single fell "completely into average territory."<ref name="musictimes">{{cite journal|last1=Menyes|first1=Carolyn|title=Fifth Harmony "Sledgehammer" Review: Girl Group Goes '80s on New 'Reflection' Single|url=http://www.musictimes.com/articles/13810/20141029/fifth-harmony-sledgehammer-review-listen-reflection.htm|journal=Music Times|date=October 29, 2014|accessdate=December 4, 2014|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20180302215655/https://www.teenvogue.com/story/fifth-harmony-sledehammer-single-reflection|archivedate= March 2, 2018}}</ref>

In a 2017 article published on Idolator, Mike Neid covered the song in the website's "Should Have Been Bigger" feature, stating that the track was one of the group's most "underrated" songs. Neid further commented on how their visuals were not as "sexy" as the music video for their 2016 single "[[Work from Home (song)|Work from Home]]", but served as a "joyful and chic" reminder of their "youthful beginnings."<ref>{{cite web|last1=Neid|first1=Mike|title=Should Have Been Bigger: Fifth Harmony's "Sledgehammer"|url=https://www.idolator.com/7665113/flashback-fifth-harmony-sledgehammer|publisher=Idolator|date= June 20, 2017|accessdate=June 25, 2017|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20180302220117/https://www.idolator.com/7665113/flashback-fifth-harmony-sledgehammer?andro=1|archivedate= March 2, 2018}}</ref> ''Billboard'' ranked the song at number 57 on their 100 Greatest Girl Group Songs of All Time list.<ref name="Billboard Critics'list">{{cite magazine|title=100 Greatest Girl Group Songs of All Time: Critics' Picks|url=https://www.billboard.com/media/lists/100-greatest-girl-group-songs-7857816/|magazine=Billboard|author= Jason Lipshutz|date= July 1, 2017|access-date=July 11, 2017|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180302220631/https://www.billboard.com/articles/columns/pop/7857816/100-greatest-girl-group-songs|archive-date= March 2, 2018}}</ref> Lipshutz stated that though one of the lines is "more than a bit confusing", the group manage to "sell their tale of unsaid infatuation with dedicated [[melisma]]s and gooey production".<ref name="Billboard Critics'list"/>

In 2017, ''[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]'' ranked "Sledgehammer" at number 57 on its list of 100 Greatest Girl Group Songs of All Time: Critics' Picks.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/media/lists/100-greatest-girl-group-songs-7857816/|title=100 Greatest Girl Group Songs of All Time: Critics' Picks|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|date=July 10, 2017|access-date=December 25, 2019}}</ref>

===Commercial performance===
Upon its release, "Sledgehammer" debuted on ''Billboard''{{'s}} [[Mainstream Top 40]] chart at number 28. It peaked at number 21 in December 2014, becoming their highest position–surpassing their debut single "[[Miss Movin' On]]" (2013) at the time.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=http://www.billboard.com/artist/5665538/fifth-harmony/chart?f=381|title=Fifth Harmony - Chart history: Pop Songs|magazine=Billboard|publisher=Prometheus Global Media|accessdate=December 25, 2014}}</ref> The song went on to spend three non-consecutive weeks at its peak position.<ref>{{cite magazine|title=Pop Music: Top Pop Songs Chart|url=https://www.billboard.com/charts/pop-songs/2015-02-07|magazine=Billboard|date= February 7, 2015|access-date= November 12, 2018}}</ref> For the week dated December 4, 2014, "Sledgehammer" debuted at number 93 on the [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]].<ref>{{cite magazine|title=Top 100 Songs-Billboard Hot 100 Charts|url=https://www.billboard.com/charts/hot-100/2014-12-13|magazine=Billboard|date=December 13, 2014|access-date= November 12, 2018}}</ref> It eventually rose to a peak of 40 in its fifth week on the chart, with sales of 85,000–aided by a price reduction on digital download services, which reported a 277 percent spike in sales. This marked Fifth Harmony's best week sales, as well as their highest-charting single and first top 40 hit;<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/pro/hot-100-chart-moves-fifth-harmony-notches-first-top-40-hit-with/|title=Hot 100 Chart Moves: Fifth Harmony Notches First Top 40 Hit With 'Sledgehammer'|last1=Trust|first1=Gary|magazine=Billboard|publisher=Prometheus Global Media|date=January 2, 2015|access-date=January 3, 2015}}</ref> It has since been surpassed by "[[Worth It (Fifth Harmony song)|Worth It]]" (2015) and "Work from Home" (2016).<ref>{{cite magazine|url=http://www.billboard.com/artist/5665538/Fifth+Harmony/chart?f=379|title=Fifth Harmony Album & Song Chart History|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|accessdate=27 May 2015}}</ref> On June 26, 2015, "Sledgehammer" was certified Platinum by the [[Recording Industry Association of America]] (RIAA) in the United States for sales of one million units.{{Certification Cite Ref|region=United States|title=Sledgehammer|artist=Fifth Harmony}} It also received a Gold certification in both Canada.<ref name="CanadaCert"/>

==Music video==
An accompanying music video for "Sledgehammer" was uploaded onto Fifth Harmony's official [[Vevo]] account on November 25, 2014. It was directed and choreographed by [[Fatima Robinson]], who also directed the music video for "Boss". Scenes in the visual include a swing set, a unicorn sculpture, and a silhouetted man swinging a sledgehammer while the group is dressed in colorful dresses and silky hair.<ref name="Billbvideo">{{cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/fifth-harmony-sledgehammer-music-video-6327724/|title=Watch Fifth Harmony Hit Like a 'Sledgehammer' In New Video|last1=Lipshutz|first1=Jason|magazine=Billboard|date=November 25, 2014|access-date=November 25, 2014}}</ref> MTV released an exclusive behind-the-scenes video of "Sledgehammer" on November 21, 2014.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.mtv.com/news/2005820/fifth-harmony-sledgehammer-behind-the-scenes/|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141125054956/http://www.mtv.com/news/2005820/fifth-harmony-sledgehammer-behind-the-scenes/|url-status=dead|archive-date=November 25, 2014|title=Go Behind The Scenes Of Fifth Harmony's 'Sledgehammer' Music Video Before It Even Drops|last=Wilson|first=Gaby|publisher=MTV|date=November 21, 2014|accessdate=February 5, 2015}}</ref>
[[File:Fifth Harmony - Sledgehammer Music Video.png|thumb|right|alt=|In the music video, the members of Fifth Harmony portray themselves as [[silhouette]]s dancing in a background of blue and white shades.]]
The video begins with the members of Fifth Harmony standing in a background of white and blue shades, where [[Camila Cabello]] takes her pulse, the band starts dancing and the screen goes dark. The group is subsequently standing in a pyramid stair, with Cabello moving her hands in rhythm of the song before switching back to the prior scene. Following this, the screen goes dark several times, switching from a shot of a shirtless man moving a sledgehammer and the members in the white and blue shade background.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=http://www.seventeen.com/celebrity/music/reviews/a28409/fifth-harmony-sledgehammer-music-video-premiere/|title=Watch Fifth Harmony's Badass New Music Video For "Sledgehammer"|magazine= [[Seventeen (American magazine)|Seventeen]]|date=November 25, 2014|access-date=June 15, 2017|first= Noelle|last= Devoe}}</ref> The next scene turns into color, revealing the faces of the group for the first time, with each pounding their heart with their hands as the lyric "If you could take my pulse right now, it would feel like a sledgehammer" is sung. Choreographed dance moves such as the members throwing their fists in the air and the shot between the shirtless man moving the sledgehammer are alternated. Another seen has the girls on a swing set/scaffolding area barefoot and wearing different dresses. [[Normani]] is in a light green dress, [[Ally Brooke]] is in a red dress, Camila is in an orange dress, [[Dinah Jane]] is in a purple dress, and [[Lauren Jauregui]] is in a blue dress.

The screen then shows blue and orange colors, in a special effect mimicking lights moving around them. Final scenes shows the members individually singing, all wearing a different dress from the first shot. The video continues with repeating several scenes with the [[Negative (photography)|negative special effects]] becoming more present. In a different shot, the silhouette one of the group is seen with a fan blowing air towards her, waving her hair and dress simultaneously. The video rewinds the moment the shirtless man breaks pieces of brick apart to make it seem as if though the pieces are reforming, similar to when one is going through heartbreak. Writing for ''Billboard'', Lipshutz said the video's [[Monochrome|monochromatic]] setting allows the group to "demonstrate their maturity". He also states that the video is "more of a relax fest" and called the "giddiness radiation" of the girls "pretty infectious".<ref name="Billbvideo"/> As of July 2020, the video has surpassed over 170 million views.
