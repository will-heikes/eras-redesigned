"'''Ultraviolence'''" is a song recorded by American singer and songwriter [[Lana Del Rey]] for her third studio album [[Ultraviolence (album)|of the same name]] (2014). It was co-written by Del Rey and Daniel Heath, and produced by [[Dan Auerbach]]. The song was released on June 4, 2014, by [[Polydor]] and [[Interscope Records]], as the third single from ''Ultraviolence''. A music video, directed by [[Francesco Carrozzini]], was released on July 30, 2014.

According to Brenna Ehrlich of [[MTV News]], "Ultraviolence" tells the story of a "typical Lana Del Rey romantic relationship: broken, failed and painful".<ref>[https://web.archive.org/web/20140606203929/http://www.mtv.com/news/1838435/lana-del-rey-ultraviolence-single/ Lana Del Rey's 'Ultraviolence' Will Give You Goosebumps – MTV<!-- Bot generated title -->]</ref> In the line "I can hear sirens sirens, he hit me and it felt like a kiss," Del Rey references the 1962 [[The Crystals]] song "[[He Hit Me (and It Felt like a Kiss)]]", and according to Harriet Gibson of ''[[The Guardian]]'', "appear[s] to romanticise brutality".<ref>{{cite web|last1=Gibsone|first1=Harriet|title=Listen to Lana Del Rey's Ultraviolence|url=https://www.theguardian.com/music/2014/jun/05/listen-lana-del-rey-ultraviolence|work=[[The Guardian]]|access-date=June 5, 2014|date=June 5, 2014}}</ref> Molly Lambert of ''[[Grantland]]'' noted that the song is about a "difficult man" named Jim. Lambert hypothesized that "Jim" might be a reference to cult leader [[Jim Jones]] and the [[Jonestown|Jonestown massacre]], due to the line "you're my cult leader."<ref>{{cite web |url=https://grantland.com/hollywood-prospectus/songs-of-the-week-lana-del-rey-gets-culty-kanye-gets-bubblegummed-and-sheryl-crow-rides-shotgun/ |title=Songs of the Week: Lana Del Rey Gets Culty, Kanye Gets Bubblegummed, and Sheryl Crow Rides Shotgun |author=Molly Lambert |work=[[Grantland]] |publisher=[[ESPN]] |date=June 6, 2014 |access-date=December 18, 2023 }}</ref> Aimee Ferrier of ''Far Out'' said fans often assume Jim "represents a figure akin to Jim Jones."<ref>{{cite web |url=https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/who-is-lana-del-reys-character-jimmy/ |title=Who is Lana Del Rey's character 'Jimmy'?
 |author=Aimee Ferrier |work=Far Out |date=March 16, 2023 |access-date=December 18, 2023 }}</ref>

Kevin Rutherford of Radio.com remarked that "Ultraviolence" maintained the theme of songs previously released from the album; it "ups the lilting, low-tempo, strings-heavy form" Del Rey had established in her earlier work.<ref>{{cite web|last1=Rutherford|first1=Kevin|title=Listen to Lana Del Rey's Solemn, Strings-Heavy New Song 'Ultraviolence'|url=http://radio.com/2014/06/04/lana-del-rey-new-song-ultraviolence-listen/|website=Radio.com|access-date=June 5, 2014|date=June 4, 2014}}</ref> Sal Cinquemani of ''[[Slant Magazine]]'' described "Ultraviolence" as a "laconic, string-laden [[torch song]]".<ref>{{cite magazine|last1=Cinquemani|first1=Sal|title=Track Review: Lana Del Rey, "Ultraviolence"|url=https://www.slantmagazine.com/house/2014/04/track-review-lana-del-rey-ultraviolence|magazine=[[Slant Magazine]]|access-date=June 5, 2014|date=June 4, 2014}}</ref> A writer for the Music Times commented that the "violins, lightly thumping drums and Del Rey's angelic singing" gave the song a "[[Church music|church-y]]" feel, pointing out that the track presented her "title sound".<ref>{{cite web|title=REVIEW: Lana Del Rey Drops 'Ultraviolence' Title Track|url=https://www.musictimes.com/articles/6573/20140604/review-lana-del-rey-drops-ultraviolence-title-track.htm|website=Music Times|date=4 June 2014 |access-date=June 4, 2014}}</ref>

== Critical reception ==
{{Quote box|quote=She exalts a violent man on the title track, referencing [[the Crystals]]' 'He Hit Me (And It Felt Like a Kiss)'. It's harder still to listen knowing that Del Rey claims these situations are all true-to-life. 85% of domestic violence victims return to their abusers, submission is an ancient peccadillo, and there's no accounting for taste. But she conjures them with surface-level sadness worn like a shade of eyeshadow, which swiftly wears thin if you have no time for one-dimensional cads.|source=– ''[[NME]]'' on ''Ultraviolence''<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.nme.com/reviews/lana-del-rey/15401|title=NME Reviews – Lana Del Rey – 'Ultraviolence'|work=[[NME]]|first=Laura|last=Snapes|date=June 16, 2014|access-date=February 10, 2015}}</ref>|align=right|width=20em|salign=yes}}
Nolan Feeney of ''[[Time (magazine)|Time]]'' criticized the song for its glorification of [[domestic violence]], mentioning [[Lorde]]'s comment on Del Rey's music: "This sort of shirt-tugging, desperate, don't leave me stuff. That's not a good thing for young girls, even young people, to hear." However, Feeney also stated that Del Rey would "likely" not endorse the "screwed-up tales of vice and luxury" her character, Lana Del Rey, sings about.<ref>{{cite magazine|last1=Feeney|first1=Nolan|title=Does Lana Del Rey's New Song Glorify Domestic Violence?|url=https://time.com/2823016/lana-del-rey-ultraviolence-title-track/|magazine=[[Time (magazine)|Time]]|access-date=June 5, 2014|date=June 4, 2014}}</ref> While noting that Del Rey did not offer a positive or negative opinion on domestic violence, Harley Brown of ''[[Spin (magazine)|Spin]]'' said that the lyrics to the song could generate controversy, especially since Del Rey dismissed [[feminism]] in an interview with ''[[The Fader]]'', saying, "For me, the issue of feminism is just not an interesting concept. I'm more interested in, you know, [[SpaceX]] and [[Tesla Science Center at Wardenclyffe|Tesla]], what's going to happen with our intergalactic possibilities. Whenever people bring up feminism, I'm like, god. I'm just not really that interested."<ref>{{cite news|last1=Brown|first1=Harley|title=Listen to Lana Del Rey's Sweeping, Cinematic 'Ultraviolence'|url=https://www.spin.com/2014/06/lana-del-rey-ultraviolence-stream-title-track/|work=[[Spin (magazine)|Spin]]|date=4 June 2014 |access-date=June 5, 2014}}</ref><ref name="FaderCoverStory">{{cite web|last1=Cooper|first1=Duncan|title=Cover Story: Lana Del Rey Is Anyone She Wants to Be|url=https://www.thefader.com/2014/06/04/lana-del-rey-cover-interview/|work=[[The Fader]]|access-date=June 5, 2014|date=June 4, 2014}}</ref> By the end of 2014, ''NME'' named "Ultraviolence" the 32nd best song of the year.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.nme.com/photos/nme-s-top-50-tracks-of-2014/364505|title=NME's Top 50 Tracks Of 2014|work=NME|access-date=December 20, 2014}}</ref>

== Music video ==
A music video, shot entirely on an [[iPhone]] on the 8mm Vintage Camera app, was directed by [[Francesco Carrozzini]] and filmed on July 3, 2014 in [[Portofino]], Italy. In the video, Del Rey can be seen wearing a white wedding dress with a veil and a bouquet of flowers in her hands, wandering around an outside setting and later entering a church (Cappella di San Sebastiano). The video was released on July 30 by Noisey (''[[Vice (magazine)|Vice]]'').<ref>{{cite magazine|title=Watch Lana Del Rey's New Official Music Video, "Ultraviolence"|url=https://www.vice.com/en/article/lana-del-rey-ultraviolence-video-official/|magazine=[[Vice (magazine)|Vice]]|access-date=July 30, 2014|date=July 30, 2014}}</ref><ref>{{cite magazine|last1=Grow|first1=Kory|title=Lana Del Rey Plays a Bride Without a Groom in 'Ultraviolence' Video|url=https://www.rollingstone.com/music/videos/lana-del-rey-plays-a-bride-without-a-groom-in-ultraviolence-video-20140730|magazine=[[Rolling Stone]]|access-date=July 30, 2014|date=July 30, 2014}}</ref>

== Live performances ==
Del Rey premiered "Ultraviolence" as part of her set at the [[PNE Forum]] in [[Vancouver]], British Columbia, Canada on May 25, 2014.<ref>{{cite web|last1=Frydenlund|first1=Zach|title=Watch Lana Del Rey Debut "Ultraviolence" at Her Show in Vancouver|url=https://www.complex.com/music/2014/05/lana-del-rey-debuts-ultraviolence|website=Complex|access-date=June 4, 2014|date=May 27, 2014}}</ref>
