"'''Venus'''" is a song recorded and produced by American singer [[Lady Gaga]] for her third [[studio album]], ''[[Artpop]]'' (2013). It was written by Gaga, [[DJ White Shadow|Paul "DJ White Shadow" Blair]], [[Madeon]], Dino Zisis, Nick Monson, and [[Sun Ra]]. The recording includes a sample from the French [[electropop]] duo [[Zombie Zombie (band)|Zombie Zombie]]'s cover of Sun Ra's song "Rocket Number 9", from his studio album, ''[[Interstellar Low Ways]]'' (1966); Sun Ra received a co-writing credit on the track. Originally intended to be the second single from the album, it was released as the first promotional single from ''Artpop'' on October 27, 2013, to the [[iTunes Store]], following the positive reception of "[[Do What U Want]]", which was planned to be a promotional single only.

"Venus" is a [[1980s in music|1980s]]-inspired [[synth-pop]], [[dance-pop]] and [[glam rock]] song with four [[Hook (music)|hooks]], and references [[Sandro Botticelli]]'s painting ''[[The Birth of Venus]]''. Gaga worked on the song with Madeon and was inspired by a number of things, chief among them were: [[Venus (mythology)|Venus]], the [[List of Roman deities|Roman goddess]] of love, the [[Venus|eponymous planet]], and sexual intercourse. Lyrically it also name-checks the planets in the [[Solar System]]. Three accompanying artworks were designed for the single by [[Steven Klein (photographer)|Steven Klein]], including one with a scorpion attached to Gaga's head, a picture of a dead bat, and a third showing her standing naked with an open clam shell superimposed above her shoulder and covering her lower face. A music video was initially conceived for the song, but never materialized. The track was later used during a sequence in the music video for ''Artpop''{{'}}s third single, "[[G.U.Y.]]" instead.

Upon its release, "Venus" received mixed reviews from [[Music journalism|music critics]]. They called the song catchy, but had mixed feelings towards the lyrics, and preferred the previously released "Do What U Want". It achieved moderate success on charts around the world, reaching the top of the charts in Hungary and Spain, and the top ten in seven additional countries. The song debuted and peaked at number 32 on the US [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]]. Gaga performed the track on the [[The X Factor (British series 10)|tenth series]] of [[The X Factor (British TV series)|British ''The X Factor'']], along with a suggestive performance of "Do What U Want", which prompted complaints to the broadcaster and British media regulator, [[Ofcom]]. Other notable performances occurred at ''[[The Graham Norton Show]]'', Gaga's [[ArtRave]] promotional event, and the [[ArtRave: The Artpop Ball]] tour in 2014.

==Background and development==
[[File:Madeon (color corrected).png|thumb|upright|left|[[Madeon]] co-wrote "Venus" with Gaga and the other songwriters.|alt=A close-up of Madeon with tousled brown hair, looking downward while wearing a shiny pale pink T-shirt.]]
Development of Gaga's third studio album, ''[[Artpop]]'', began shortly after the release of her second record, ''[[Born This Way (album)|Born This Way]]'' (2011), and by the following year, the album's concepts were "beginning to flourish" as Gaga collaborated with producers [[Fernando Garibay]] and [[DJ White Shadow]].<ref name=":1">{{cite magazine|last=Shewey|first=Don|date=July 30, 2013|url=https://www.rollingstone.com/music/news/eight-things-we-know-about-lady-gagas-artpop-20130730|title=Lady Gaga's 'ARTPOP': What We Know So Far|magazine=[[Rolling Stone]]|access-date=February 21, 2014|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131127124958/http://www.rollingstone.com/music/news/eight-things-we-know-about-lady-gagas-artpop-20130730|archive-date=November 27, 2013}}</ref><ref name="DJWS">{{cite web|url=http://www.mtv.com/news/1675019/lady-gaga-next-album-tour/|title=Lady Gaga 'Doing Prep' for Tour, Next Album|last=Vena|first=Jocelyn|date=November 28, 2011|access-date=January 17, 2012|publisher=[[MTV News]]|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141211093036/http://www.mtv.com/news/1675019/lady-gaga-next-album-tour/|archive-date=December 11, 2014}}</ref><ref name="albumname">{{cite web|url=http://www.mtv.com/news/1675458/lady-gaga-next-album/|title=Lady Gaga Says Next Album Is 'Beginning to Flourish'|last=Vena|first=Jocelyn|date=December 6, 2011|access-date=January 17, 2012|publisher=MTV News|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140507183044/http://www.mtv.com/news/1675458/lady-gaga-next-album/|archive-date=May 7, 2014}}</ref> In the meantime, the singer also worked with French disc jockey [[Madeon]]. This was his first experience collaborating with a vocalist face-to-face.<ref name="NRJ"/> On [[MTV News]] he clarified that he had "always wanted to work with pop artists and my #1 on my list was Lady Gaga. So when I had the opportunity to do that, I was really thrilled."<ref name="madeon">{{cite web|url=http://www.mtv.com/news/1717638/madeon-lady-gaga-artpop-producer/|title=After Lady Gaga, Madeon Wants Kanye West Next|last=Vena|first=Jocelyn|date=November 8, 2013|access-date=November 26, 2016|publisher=MTV News|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161129023540/http://www.mtv.com/news/1717638/madeon-lady-gaga-artpop-producer/|archive-date=November 29, 2016}}</ref> Having mutual respect for each other's work, Gaga praised Madeon's production skills, saying, "He is so amazing. He has such an understanding of music at such a young age. He reminds me of myself so much. He's obsessed, so obsessed with music."<ref>{{cite web|date=August 27, 2013|title=Lady Gaga Teases New Song 'Gypsy' With Madeon And Producer RedOne|url=https://www.capitalfm.com/artists/lady-gaga/news/new-song-gypsy-red-one/|website=[[Capital (radio network)|Capital FM]]|access-date=October 3, 2026|url-status=live|archive-url=https://megalodon.jp/2026-1003-0212-42/https://www.capitalfm.com:443/artists/lady-gaga/news/new-song-gypsy-red-one/|archive-date=October 3, 2026}}</ref>

Madeon was associated with co-writing and co-producing three songs on ''Artpop''—"Venus", "Mary Jane Holland" and "[[Gypsy (Lady Gaga song)|Gypsy]]".<ref name="madeon"/> During her interview with [[Sirius XM Holdings|SiriusXM]] radio, Gaga confirmed that "Venus" was the first song developed for ''Artpop'' and it set the mood and tone for the rest of the album to follow.<ref name="tbt">{{cite web|url=http://www.newnownext.com/lady-gaga-explains-artpop-track-by-track-listen/12/2013/|title=Lady Gaga Explains 'ARTPOP' Track-by-Track: Listen|last=Shorey|first=Eric|date=December 12, 2013|access-date=November 23, 2016|publisher=[[Logo TV]]|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131205111425/http://www.newnownext.com/lady-gaga-explains-artpop-track-by-track-listen/12/2013/|archive-date=December 5, 2013}}</ref> Gaga produced the track entirely by herself with some co-producing contributions from Madeon. Among the inspirations behind the track were: [[Venus (mythology)|Venus]], the [[List of Roman deities|Roman goddess]] of love, the [[Venus|eponymous planet]], and sexual intercourse.<ref name="tbt"/> A further explanation was provided by the singer:
<blockquote>
"So, this song is about faith, but it's also about finding faith in other places, in the beyond, and my experience with love being something that took me up a really long time to find. So it's really psychedelic and it really takes you on a journey [...] I kinda got this lot of different inspiration from this sort of futuristic [[Disco music|disco]] meets late 70's and [[Jazz music|jazz]] and this really really kinda gooey deep groove [...] This song is really about sex, but it's about sex on the most mythological way."<ref name="tbt"/>
</blockquote>

==Recording and composition==
[[File:SunRa in 1992 (cropped).jpg|thumb|"Venus" samples "Rocket Number 9", released by French duo [[Zombie Zombie (band)|Zombie Zombie]], which itself was a cover of [[jazz music|jazz]] artist [[Sun Ra]]'s ''(pictured)'' "Rocket Number 9 Take Off to the Planet Venus".|alt=Sun Ra seated in a wheelchair while playing an electronic keyboard, wearing a dark patterned jacket and a black beret decorated with small colorful pins, with a drum kit and other musical equipment visible behind him.]]

Madeon praised Gaga's work ethic during the [[Born This Way Ball]], recalling that she continued recording music after her three-hour concerts and brought the same level of energy to even the earliest [[Demo (music)|demo]] takes.<ref name="NRJ">{{cite web |title=Lady Gaga : elle impressione en studio ! |url=https://www.nrj.fr/artistes/lady-gaga/actus/lady-gaga-elle-impressione-en-studio-296932 |publisher=[[NRJ (France)|NRJ]] |language=fr |date=March 21, 2013 |access-date=October 15, 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230206021443/https://www.nrj.fr/artistes/lady-gaga/actus/lady-gaga-elle-impressione-en-studio-296932 |archive-date=February 6, 2023 |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite podcast |last=Moglia |first=Guillaume |title=MADEON raconte les coulisses d'ARTPOP avec LADY GAGA (Interview) |url=https://podfollow.com/popslay-le-podcast/episode/49743f7e9f05399c5929b8288d2d8990b/view |website=Popslay, Le Podcast |language=fr |date=July 2, 2026 |access-date=October 12, 2026}}</ref> Madeon's production on ''Artpop'' was different from his usual endeavours, and he helped take the mood of the track from being a [[sentimental ballad|ballad]] to being an anthem, but still retaining the sentimental and inspirational aspects of it.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.dancingastronaut.com/2013/11/editorial-what-every-electronic-music-fan-should-know-about-lady-gagas-artpop/|title=Editorial: What every electronic music fan should know about Lady Gaga's 'Artpop'|last=Farella|first=Dylan|date=November 6, 2013|access-date=November 28, 2016|work=Dancing Astronaut|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161129024343/http://www.dancingastronaut.com/2013/11/editorial-what-every-electronic-music-fan-should-know-about-lady-gagas-artpop/|archive-date=November 29, 2016}}</ref> "Venus" was written and produced by Gaga, with additional songwriting from DJ White Shadow, Nick Monson and Dino Zisis. The song also includes a sample from the French [[electropop]] duo [[Zombie Zombie (band)|Zombie Zombie]]'s cover of jazz musician [[Sun Ra]]'s song "Rocket Number 9", from his studio album, ''[[Interstellar Low Ways]]'' (1966). Sun Ra was thus given a co-writing credit on the track.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/art/features/arts-observations-lady-gagas-cosmic-sound-8926160.html|work=[[The Independent]]|title=Arts observations: Lady Gaga's cosmic sound|last=Mugan|first=Chris|date=November 9, 2013|access-date=November 5, 2013|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150808193915/http://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/art/features/arts-observations-lady-gagas-cosmic-sound-8926160.html|archive-date=August 8, 2015}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.thewire.co.uk/news/27907/sun-ra-given-writing-credit-on-lady-gaga_s_venus_|title=Sun Ra given writing credit on Lady Gaga's 'Venus'|access-date=November 30, 2016|work=[[The Wire (magazine)|The Wire]]|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161201020351/http://www.thewire.co.uk/news/27907/sun-ra-given-writing-credit-on-lady-gaga_s_venus_|archive-date=December 1, 2016}}</ref> The chorus' build-up, bridge and drums are heavily influenced by Madeon's unreleased track titled 'Ubelkeit'.

The song was [[sound recording and reproduction|recorded]] by Dave Russell, Bill Malina and Ghazi Hourani at the [[Record Plant]] Studios, with additional recording carried out by Benjamin 'Ben' Rice at [[Chicago Recording Company|CRC Studios]]. Andrew Robertson and Daniel Zaidenstadt assisted Rice in the recording sessions, while Steve Faye assisted Malina. Gaga did the [[bass (music)|bass]] [[arrangement]]s with Monson, who also worked on the synth arrangement with Madeon and on additional production. Tim Stewart was responsible for the guitars played on "Venus". It was [[audio mixing (recorded music)|mixed]] by Manny Marroquin at [[Larrabee Sound Studios|Larrabee North Studios]]; further mixing was done by Chris Galland and Delhert Bowers. Gene Grimaldi [[audio mastering|mastered]] the track at Oasis Mastering Studios, in Burbank, California.<ref name="liner"/>

"Venus" is a [[synth-pop]], [[dance-pop]] and [[glam rock]] song consisting of four hooks.<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Levine |first=Nick |title=Lady Gaga unveils second snippet of new track 'Venus' – listen |url=https://www.nme.com/news/music/lady-gaga-145-1237751 |magazine=[[NME]] |date=October 26, 2013 |access-date=October 2, 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190718235620/https://www.nme.com/news/music/lady-gaga-145-1237751|archive-date=July 18, 2019|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name=EntertainmentWeeklyReview/><ref>{{cite web|last=Slater|first=Tom|date=November 15, 2013|url=http://www.spiked-online.com/newsite/article/lady_gaga_civiliser_of_the_pop_masses/14297|title=Lady Gaga: civiliser of the pop masses|work=[[Spiked (magazine)|Spiked]]|access-date=May 18, 2018|archive-date=May 18, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180518200801/http://www.spiked-online.com/newsite/article/lady_gaga_civiliser_of_the_pop_masses/14297|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last1=Damshenas |first1=Sam |title=We ranked every single Lady Gaga album from worst to best |url=https://www.gaytimes.co.uk/culture/118750/we-ranked-every-single-lady-gaga-album-from-worst-to-best/ |website=[[Gay Times|GAYTIMES]] |access-date=April 26, 2020 |date=February 25, 2020 |quote=...to 80s glam-rock (Venus) [...] |archive-date=December 16, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191216165058/https://www.gaytimes.co.uk/culture/118750/we-ranked-every-single-lady-gaga-album-from-worst-to-best/ |url-status=live }}</ref> Gaga's vocal delivery is similar to that of  [[David Bowie]] in his persona [[The Rise and Fall of Ziggy Stardust and the Spiders from Mars|Ziggy Stardust]], while the song's structure is reminiscent of [[Bananarama]]'s 1986 single of [[Venus (Shocking Blue song)#Bananarama version|the same name]]. Along with the Bowie-esque verses, there's one hook where the beat drops, then another hook where it starts building up, and finally the [[refrain|chorus]] is reached. When Bradley Stern from ''MuuMuse'' reviewed the single, he described the chorus as "total pop euphoria" with Gaga belting the lyrics, "When you touch me I die just a little inside / I wonder if this could be love!"<ref name=muumuse/> The song is written in the key of [[F minor]] and composed in the [[time signature]] of [[common time]], with a tempo of 122 beats per minute. Gaga's vocals range from F<sub>3</sub> to E{{flat}}<sub>5</sub>, and the track follows a basic sequence of Fm–E{{flat}}–Fm–E{{flat}} in the first verse, A{{flat}}–E{{flat}}–Fm–E{{flat}} in the second hook, Fm–B{{flat}}–Fm–B{{flat}} in the third verse, and finally Fm–A{{flat}}–E{{flat}}–D{{flat}} in the chorus.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.musicnotes.com/sheetmusic/mtd.asp?ppn=MN0126813|title=Lady Gaga 'Venus' Sheet Music|publisher=Musicnotes.com|access-date=December 2, 2016|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161203000121/http://www.musicnotes.com/sheetmusic/mtd.asp?ppn=MN0126813|archive-date=December 3, 2016|date=November 5, 2013}}</ref>

According to [[Jon Pareles]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'', it is a "mutating, episodic dance-floor track, [in which] the singer presents herself as the goddess of love in the 'seashell bikini' painted by [[Sandro Botticelli|Botticelli]] [in ''[[The Birth of Venus]]'']".<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2013/11/10/arts/music/blurring-art-artifice-and-pop-culture.html|title=Blurring Art, Artifice and Pop Culture|last=Pareles|first=Jon|author-link=Jon Pareles|date=November 6, 2013|access-date=December 2, 2016|work=[[The New York Times]]|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161006114233/http://www.nytimes.com/2013/11/10/arts/music/blurring-art-artifice-and-pop-culture.html|archive-date=October 6, 2016}}</ref> The lyrics of "Venus" are space-themed, name-checking the planets in the [[Solar System]], with references to the planet's mythical counterpart, [[Aphrodite]], the Greek goddess of love.<ref name=EntertainmentWeeklyReview/><ref name="billboard"/> The extraterrestrial themes drew comparisons to [[Katy Perry]]'s "[[E.T. (song)|E.T.]]" (2011) in the ''Entertainment Weekly'' review of the track.<ref name=EntertainmentWeeklyReview/> John Walker from MTV News noted that "During Gaga's futuristic ballroom emcee moment, where she channels something straight out of ''[[Paris Is Burning (film)|Paris Is Burning]]'' she utters the line 'Uranus!/ Don't you know my ass is famous!?' That's like, the greatest version of 'Don't You Know Who I Am!?' that we've ever heard."<ref name=MTVReview/>
