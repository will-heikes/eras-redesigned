"'''Partition'''" is a song by American singer [[Beyoncé]] from her fifth studio album, [[Beyoncé (album)|''Beyoncé'']] (2013). Beyoncé wrote and produced the song with [[Justin Timberlake]], [[Timbaland]], [[Jerome "J-Roc" Harmon]], [[Key Wane|Dwane "Key Wane" Weir]], and [[Mike Dean (record producer)|Mike Dean]], with additional writing from [[The-Dream]] and additional production from [[Boots (musician)|Jordy "Boots" Asher]]. "Partition" was released to American [[urban contemporary]] radio on February 25, 2014, as the third [[Single (music)|single]] from ''Beyoncé.''

The song consists of two parts, individually titled "'''Yoncé'''" and "'''Partition'''". "Partition" has a sound that combines [[electro-R&B]] and [[hip-hop]]. Its production musically consists of a sparse [[synthesizer|synthesized]] arrangement, a thrusting [[bassline]] and a heavy [[Beat (music)|drumbeat]]. Exploring the deep, sexual nature of ''Beyoncé'', "Partition" depicts sexual intercourse in the back of a limousine. The closing minute of the song features a spoken word interpolation taken from the French dub of the 1998 film ''[[The Big Lebowski]]'', a speech originally spoken by [[Julianne Moore]].

"Partition" peaked at number 23 on the US [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]] chart, number nine on the US [[Hot R&B/Hip-Hop Songs]] chart and number one on the US [[Hot Dance Club Songs]] chart. Accompanying [[music video]]s for "Yoncé" and "Partition" were released on December 13, 2013, on ''Beyoncé''. The former clip was directed by Ricky Saiz and filmed on the streets of [[Brooklyn]], [[New York (state)|New York]], while the latter was directed by [[Jake Nava]] and shot at the [[Paris]]ian [[cabaret]] club [[Crazy Horse (cabaret)|Crazy Horse]]. Both "Yoncé" and "Partition" were added to the setlist and performed live during the second European leg of [[The Mrs. Carter Show World Tour]] (2013–14) and later during the [[On the Run Tour (Beyoncé and Jay-Z)|On the Run Tour]] (2014), [[The Formation World Tour]] (2016), and the [[Renaissance World Tour]] (2023).

==Conception and release==
[[File:Justin Timberlake Cannes 2013.jpg|thumb|upright|[[Justin Timberlake]] (''pictured'') co-wrote and co-produced the song.]]
"Partition" was conceived as a two-part song, the first part of which is called "Yoncé".<ref name="credits">{{cite web |date=December 14, 2013 |title=Beyonce Album Credits |url=http://www.beyonce.com/album/beyonce/?media_view=credits |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160530141716/http://www.beyonce.com/album/beyonce/?media_view=credits |archive-date=May 30, 2016 |access-date=December 22, 2013 |publisher=[[Beyoncé|Beyonce.com]]}}</ref> "Yoncé" was written by Beyoncé and [[The-Dream|Terius Nash]] with music by [[Timbaland|Timothy Mosley]], [[Justin Timberlake]], [[Key Wane|Dwane Weir]], [[J-Roc|Jerome Harmon]] and [[Mike Dean (music producer)|Mike Dean]]. "Partition" was written by Beyoncé with music by Dwane Weir and Mike Dean. The production was handled by Timberlake, Beyoncé, as well as Mosley, Harmon and Wane under their stage names Timbaland, J-Roc and Key Wane respectively, with additional production by Mike Dean and [[Boots (musician)|Boots]].<ref name="credits" /> Yoncé's original [[Beat (music)|drumbeat]] was created by Timberlake on a [[bucket]].<ref name="defender" /> Mike Dean then added the dropping 808's lead synths, claps and strings. Beyoncé described the sound that he created as "organic" and said, "It reminded me of being in middle school during the lunch breaks, when you just start free-styling."<ref name="defender" /> The beat was originally given to British rapper [[Dizzee Rascal]], but didn't use it because he "couldn't come up with nothing for it".<ref>{{Cite web |last=Williams |first=Kyann-Sian |date=2024-02-24 |title=Dizzee Rascal says he was originally given Beyoncé's 'Partition' beat |url=https://www.nme.com/news/music/dizzee-rascal-says-beyonce-partition-beat-was-originally-his-3591409 |access-date=2024-12-25 |website=NME |language=en-GB}}</ref> The trio decided to keep the buckets as Beyoncé wanted an organic production.<ref name="defender" /> Afterwards, The-Dream started writing some of the lyrics including, "Yoncé all on his mouth like liquor".<ref name="defender" /> Beyoncé found it awkward at the beginning but she soon loved the phrase, which led to the birth of her new [[alter ego]], Yoncé.<ref name="defender">{{cite news|url=http://chicagodefender.com/2013/12/23/who-is-yonce-beyonce-explains-the-birth-of-her-new-alter-ego-video/|title=Who Is Yoncé? Beyonce Explains The Birth Of Her New Alter Ego [Video]|date=December 23, 2013|work=[[Chicago Defender]]|access-date=January 19, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140202102428/http://chicagodefender.com/2013/12/23/who-is-yonce-beyonce-explains-the-birth-of-her-new-alter-ego-video/|archive-date=February 2, 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref> The singer revealed that she was "embarrassed" after she recorded "Partition" because of its overtly sexual lyrics.<ref name="people" /> Speaking about how the other part of the song materialized, Beyoncé said:

<blockquote>
It takes me back to being in my car as a teenager. It takes me back to when me and my husband first meet, and he tries to scoop me and he thinks I'm the hottest thing in the world. I kinda had this whole fantasy of being in the car, and this whole movie played in my head. I didn't have a pen and paper. I got to the mic, I'm like, 'Oh, press Record.'<ref name="people">{{cite news|url=http://www.people.com/people/article/0,,20771638,00.html|title=Beyoncé's Embarrassed to Play Songs from Her New Album for Her Mom|date=January 3, 2014|first=K.C|last=Blumm|work=[[People (magazine)|People]]|access-date=January 19, 2014|archive-date=January 18, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140118205650/http://www.people.com/people/article/0,,20771638,00.html|url-status=dead}}</ref>

The song was produced at the [[Jungle City Studios]] and [[Oven Studios]] in [[New York City]].<ref name="credits" /> Once the musical arrangements were done by the production team, the music was [[sound recording and reproduction|recorded]] by Stuart White, [[Chris Godbey]], Ann Mincieli and Bart Schoudel.<ref name="credits" /> White and [[Tony Maserati]] [[audio mixing (recorded music)|mixed]] the track while the [[audio engineer]]ing was carried out by James Krausse and Matt Weber with assistance from Justin Hergett, Chris Tabron and Matt Wiggers; the [[First assistant engineer|second engineer]] was Ramon Rivas.<ref name="credits" /> The [[audio mastering]] was done by Tom Coyne and Aya Merrill.<ref name="credits" /> Timberlake and Timbaland served as [[backing vocalist]]s and recorded the vocals of Beyoncé.<ref name="credits" /> The spoken section towards the end of "Partition" was recorded by Hajiba Fahmy, one of Beyoncé's dancers.<ref name="credits" /> "Partition" officially impacted US [[urban contemporary]] radio on February 25, 2014 as the third single from ''Beyoncé''.<ref name="urban">{{cite web|url=http://www.allaccess.com/urban/future-releases |title=Urban/UAC Future Releases |publisher=All Access Music Group |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140225142501/http://www.allaccess.com/urban/future-releases |archive-date=February 25, 2014 |url-status=dead }}</ref> "Partition" also officially impacted radio stations in Italy on March 28, 2014.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.radioairplay.fm/RadioDate/PublicDetails/61978|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140331214206/http://www.radioairplay.fm/RadioDate/PublicDetails/61978|url-status=dead|archive-date=March 31, 2014|title=BEYONCE' Partition (Columbia) - Radio Date 28/03/2014|publisher=Radioairplay.fm|access-date=June 30, 2014}}</ref>

==="Yoncé"===
==== Background and concept====
The video for "Yoncé" was directed by Ricky Saiz, and features fashion and video models  [[Chanel Iman]],  [[Jourdan Dunn]] and [[Joan Smalls]].<ref name="credits" /><ref name="fusetv">{{cite web|url=http://www.fuse.tv/2013/12/beyonce-album-track-by-track|title=Beyonce's Surprise Album: A Track-by-Track Breakdown|publisher=[[Fuse (TV channel)|Fuse]]|first=Joe|last=Lynch|date=December 13, 2013|access-date=December 18, 2013|archive-date=September 28, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200928033144/https://www.fuse.tv/2013/12/beyonce-album-track-by-track|url-status=live}}</ref> Filming took place on the streets of [[Brooklyn]], [[New York (state)|New York]].<ref name="toronto sun"/> The director wanted to add "a raw, kind of lo-fi, very New York kind of grimy, dark aesthetic" to the video.<ref name="Vena" /> Through the clip, Beyoncé introduces viewers to her new alter ego Yoncé, whose preferences include Brooklyn, her [[Grill (jewelry)|grill]], and "being the hottest girl in the club".<ref name="fusetv"/><ref name="Vena" /> Beyoncé was inspired by the visuals of the 1990 [[David Fincher]]-directed video for [[George Michael]]'s "[[Freedom! '90|Freedom]]" and she conceptualized of a "contemporary, street version" of the clip for "Yoncé".<ref name="express">{{cite news|url=http://www.express.co.uk/news/showbiz/449608/Beyonce-inspired-by-George-Michael-s-Freedom-video|title=Beyoncé inspired by George Michael's Freedom video|work=[[Daily Express]]|date=December 19, 2013|access-date=January 4, 2014|archive-date=January 4, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140104204004/http://www.express.co.uk/news/showbiz/449608/Beyonce-inspired-by-George-Michael-s-Freedom-video|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="take 40">{{cite web|url=http://www.take40.com/news/43973/beyonce's-clip-for-yonce-inspired-by-george-michael's-freedom-video!-|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131226042842/http://www.take40.com/news/43973/beyonce%27s-clip-for-yonce-inspired-by-george-michael%27s-freedom-video%21-|url-status=dead|archive-date=December 26, 2013|title=Beyoncé's Clip For Yoncé Inspired By George Michael's Freedom Video!|publisher=[[Take 40 Australia]]|date=December 19, 2013|access-date=January 4, 2014}}</ref> "Freedom" similarly featured the lip-sync performance of several contemporary supermodels.<ref name="toronto sun">{{cite news|url=http://www.torontosun.com/2013/12/24/beyonce-inspired-by-george-michael-while-making-yonce-video|title=Beyonce inspired by George Michael while making 'Yonce' video|date=December 24, 2013|access-date=January 4, 2014|newspaper=[[Toronto Sun]]|archive-date=December 27, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131227205824/http://www.torontosun.com/2013/12/24/beyonce-inspired-by-george-michael-while-making-yonce-video|url-status=live}}</ref>

Saiz wanted to keep the video mostly in the moment without much narrative; he said, "[I] wanted it to be more about the girls, doing the performance with Beyoncé taking a backseat as a kind of madam character"; in order to convey that idea, the singer does not sing the song's lyrics in the video.<ref name="Vena" /><ref name="nymag"/> He envisioned the clip as being "sexy and provocative, but not so overt", leaning to more sophisticated visuals. In an interview with ''[[New York (magazine)|New York]]'' magazine, Saiz affirmed that there are many references to 1990s eroticism and the 1995 video for "[[Human Nature (Madonna song)|Human Nature]]" by American singer [[Madonna (entertainer)|Madonna]] in the clip for "Yoncé".<ref name="buzzfeed"/> Elaborating further on the video shoot, Saiz told BuzzFeed, "The girls were incredible. Everyone kind of checked whatever ego at the door and we were all there to make something fun and special."<ref name="buzzfeed"/> He described the cast as being "spontaneous and not at all contrived".<ref name="nymag"/> Joan Smalls revealed during an interview that the crew did not follow a certain script while filming and added it was "up for interpretation" - she mentioned the scene where she licks Beyoncé's breast as her own idea, getting inspiration from the song's lyrics.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=http://www.vanityfair.com/online/daily/2014/03/joan-smalls-beyonce-lick-yonce-sweet|title=Joan Smalls Says Beyoncé Tastes Sweet, After Licking Her in "Yoncé" Video|magazine=Vanity Fair|first=Bennett|last=Marcus|date=March 20, 2014|access-date=December 8, 2014|archive-date=October 15, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141015043446/http://www.vanityfair.com/online/daily/2014/03/joan-smalls-beyonce-lick-yonce-sweet|url-status=live}}</ref>

==="Partition"===
====Background and concept====
[[File:Crazy-Horse-Saloon-P1000388.jpg|thumbnail|right|A street view of cabaret club [[Crazy Horse (cabaret)|Crazy Horse]] in Paris, where the video for "Partition" was filmed]]
The video for "Partition" was directed by [[Jake Nava]] and was shot at the [[Paris]]ian [[cabaret]] club, [[Crazy Horse (cabaret)|Crazy Horse]],<ref name="buzzfeed">{{cite web|url=https://www.buzzfeed.com/azafar/yonce-video-director-ricky-saiz|title=How The Sexiest Video On Beyoncé's New Album Came Together|last=Zafar|first=Aylin|date=December 14, 2013|publisher=[[BuzzFeed]]|access-date=January 4, 2014|archive-date=October 9, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191009152618/https://www.buzzfeed.com/azafar/yonce-video-director-ricky-saiz|url-status=live}}</ref> with opening scenes filmed at the [[Château de Ferrières]]. Jay Z made a brief cameo in the video. It was filmed quickly as he had to travel to London for a concert after the shooting. In a video commentary to the album, Beyoncé said: "The day that I got engaged was my husband's birthday and I took him to Crazy Horse. And I remember thinking, 'Damn, these girls are fly.' I just thought it was the ultimate sexy show I've ever seen. And I was like, 'I wish I was up there, I wish I could perform that for my man...' So that's what I did for the video."<ref name="liberation"/> Talking about the video, Nava noted that for the shooting Beyoncé was trying to be "even more risque than she's been in the past". While filming, he further tried to "create and capture a bit of genuine intimacy" between the singer and her husband.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://abcnews.go.com/Entertainment/scenes-beyonces-racy-partition-video-jay/story?id=22903360|title=Go Behind the Scenes of Beyoncé's Racy 'Partition' Video with Jay Z|publisher=ABC News|first=Lesley|last=Messer|date=March 14, 2014|access-date=May 16, 2014|archive-date=March 19, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140319013551/http://abcnews.go.com/Entertainment/scenes-beyonces-racy-partition-video-jay/story?id=22903360|url-status=live}}</ref> During a commentary about the video for the song, Beyoncé revealed her motivation to film it by saying,
<blockquote>
"I was 195 pounds when I gave birth... I worked crazily to get my body back. I wanted to show my body. I wanted to show that you can have a child and you can work hard and you can get your body back. I know that there's so many women that feel the same thing after they give birth. You can have your child and you can still have fun and still be sexy and still have dreams and still live for yourself. I'm not embarrassed about it, and I don't feel like I have to protect that side of me because I do believe that sexuality is a power we all have."<ref name="liberation">{{cite AV media |people=Knowles, Beyoncé (Director) |title="Self Titled" Part 4. Liberation |date=December 30, 2013 |medium=Short film, documentary |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1b1loWJfxaA |access-date=January 3, 2014 |archive-date=January 2, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140102225207/http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1b1loWJfxaA |url-status=live }}</ref>
</blockquote>

====Synopsis====
The story of the video was meant to illustrate the lyrics of the song. It depicts the sexual fantasies of a rich and bored housewife played by Beyoncé who tries to seduce her man while having breakfast at her house wearing a white robe. The video opens with various shots of a big mansion in which the singer is seen in one of the rooms. She tries to get her man's attention while he reads a newspaper. As he does not notice her, she throws a napkin on the floor and her female servant comes to lift it.<ref name="role" /> The clip transitions to scenes of Beyoncé dressed in a Victorian jeweled outfit with a mask in her hands, lip-syncing the lyrics and dancing to the rhythm of the song. Afterwards, she is seen entering a limousine in which [[Jay-Z]] is already inside. Scenes of Beyoncé with a black coat and lingerie walking in front of the car are also intertwined throughout.<ref name="role" />

The car stops in front of the club Crazy Horse. The following scenes show the singer emerging in a different set with a black background, as well as dancing atop a grand piano. Later, she is seen with female hands on her body as she dances seductively. Other scenes show her wearing lingerie and performing a pole dance with several other female dancers. Shortly afterwards, Beyoncé performs a silhouette choreographed chair dance across a purple background as Jay-Z is seen in the audience watching her while smoking a cigar. The end of the video shows the singer dancing with another woman next to her to a projected cheetah print. The last seconds cut to the breakfast table where Beyoncé is seen.<ref name="seduce" /> For the video, the singer wears an archival corset by Christian Lacroix, lace gloves by [[Chanel]], and a crystal mask by Atelier Swarovski.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.nytimes.com/slideshow/2013/12/19/fashion/BEYONCE-FASHION-ALBUM-3.html|title=Beyoncé's 'Visual Album' Fashions|work=The New York Times|date=December 19, 2013|first=Joanna|last=Nikas|page=3|access-date=August 27, 2014|archive-date=September 3, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140903053210/http://www.nytimes.com/slideshow/2013/12/19/fashion/BEYONCE-FASHION-ALBUM-3.html|url-status=live}}</ref>
