"'''Heart Attack'''" is a song recorded by American singer [[Demi Lovato]]. It was released on February 25, 2013, as the lead single from her fourth studio album, ''[[Demi (album)|Demi]]'' (2013). The song was produced by [[Mitch Allan]] and [[Jason Evigan]] of The Suspex, who co-wrote the song along with Lovato, [[Sean Douglas (songwriter)|Sean Douglas]], [[Nikki Williams]], and Aaron Phillips. "Heart Attack" is an [[electropop]] song that employs cardiac motifs to represent the fear of falling in love. It received acclaim from music critics, many of whom complimented its lyrics and Lovato's vocals.

The song sold 215,000 copies in the US in its first week, her best sales week for a song download, and peaked at number ten on the US [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]], becoming Lovato's second solo top-ten single on the chart. It was later certified 6× platinum by the [[Recording Industry Association of America]] (RIAA). "Heart Attack" also performed well internationally, reaching the top 10 in Canada, Ireland, Lebanon, New Zealand, Scotland, and the United Kingdom.

An accompanying music video for "Heart Attack" was directed by [[Chris Applebaum]] and premiered through [[Vevo]] on April 9, 2013. Lovato has performed the track on several television programs and included it on the set lists for all of her headlining concert tours from [[the Neon Lights Tour]] (2014) to the [[Holy Fvck Tour]] (2023). For the song's tenth anniversary, Lovato re-recorded "Heart Attack" and included it on her first remix album, ''[[Revamped]]'' (2023).

She explained: "At the time I was going through some relationship stuff, and I was just so depressed, so when I got into the session I thought, 'You know what? I'm gonna pour it all out there, and get it off my chest'".<ref name="idolator1">{{cite web|last=Lansky|first=Sam|title=Nikki Williams Talks Writing Demi Lovato's "Heart Attack" & Tackling Ultra|url=http://idolator.com/7446154/nikki-williams-demi-lovato-heart-attack-ultra%7C|archive-url=https://archive.today/20131214171804/http://idolator.com/7446154/nikki-williams-demi-lovato-heart-attack-ultra%7C|url-status=dead|archive-date=December 14, 2013|website=[[Idolator (website)|Idolator]]|access-date=April 4, 2013|date=March 12, 2013}}</ref> The song was first intended for Williams, but her record label rejected it.<ref name="MTV3"/> [[Pia Toscano]] then recorded the song in 2011 intending it for her debut album with [[Interscope Records]], but it did not materialize. She would later state that some of her version would be kept as background vocals in the final version of the song.<ref>{{cite magazine|last=Toscano|first=Pia|title=Confessions of An Almost Pop Star, by Pia Toscano|url=https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/pia-toscano-essay-american-idol-1235127895/|date=August 18, 2022|access-date=August 29, 2023|magazine=Billboard|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230830042459/https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/pia-toscano-essay-american-idol-1235127895/|archive-date=August 30, 2023|quote=That song was called "Heart Attack," and if you listen really closely you could still hear some of my vocals singing in the background.|url-status=live}}</ref> When Lovato heard the song, she recorded it before altering a few lyrics. In an interview with [[MTV]], Evigan explained:{{quote|A lot of people wanted the song. And then Demi came in to do another song called 'Two Pieces' and then we played her 'Heart Attack' and she freaked out about it. So she came in to record it and then she actually had a couple of lyric changes that were really good. She added that super high note at the end and she added some really cool things to it and she brought that song to life. Now it's taking off.<ref name="MTV3">{{cite news|last=Vena|first=Jocelyn|title=Demi Lovato Rescued 'Heart Attack' And Brought It 'To Life'|url=http://www.mtv.com/news/articles/1704949/demi-lovato-heart-attack-producer.jhtml|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130406050956/http://www.mtv.com/news/articles/1704949/demi-lovato-heart-attack-producer.jhtml|url-status=dead|archive-date=April 6, 2013|publisher=MTV News|access-date=April 5, 2013|date=April 3, 2013}}</ref>}}

According to him, the first version also contained a [[dubstep]] breakdown, which was removed for Lovato. He also stated, "Demi has magic to it where she has so much sass in her voice but yet so much power and all those really high notes at the end, she added that on the spot."<ref name="MTV3"/> Williams described the song, said: "It's about me being really scared to fall in love again after being rejected, after feeling so fragile and vulnerable all the time&nbsp;— thinking, I don't know if I can do this ever again!"<ref name="idolator1"/> Lovato told [[MTV]] that the song's lyrics address "falling in love and taking that risk. But, she's terrified and, by 'she,' I mean me. But, I think everyone comes to a point where they feel very vulnerable in falling in love and that's what I'm talking about."<ref name="MTV2">{{cite news|last=Vena|first=Jocelyn|title=Demi Lovato Releases 'Risk'-Filled 'Heart Attack'|url=http://www.mtv.com/news/articles/1702548/demi-lovato-heart-attack-single.jhtml|archive-url=https://archive.today/20130411101057/http://www.mtv.com/news/articles/1702548/demi-lovato-heart-attack-single.jhtml|url-status=dead|archive-date=April 11, 2013|publisher=MTV News|access-date=February 25, 2013|date=February 25, 2013}}</ref>

A trailer of "Heart Attack" was released on February 12, 2013. The song was originally set to premiere on ''[[On Air with Ryan Seacrest]]'', but leaked online on February 24, 2013, springing [[Hollywood Records]]' marketing department into early action. Lovato prompted fans to unlock the video by tweeting song lyrics and the hashtag #UnlockHeartAttack, which became a worldwide trending topic on Twitter.<ref name="Billboard1"/><ref name="iTunesUSA">{{cite web|title=Heart Attack – Single |first=Demi |last=Lovato |url=https://itunes.apple.com/us/album/heart-attack-single/id605843703 |publisher=[[iTunes Store]] |access-date=February 25, 2013 |date=February 25, 2013 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130228191820/https://itunes.apple.com/us/album/heart-attack-single/id605843703 |archive-date=February 28, 2013 }}</ref><ref>{{cite web|last=Murray |first=Michael |title=WORLD PREMIERE: Listen to Demi Lovato's New Song 'Heart Attack' |url=http://www.ryanseacrest.com/2013/02/24/world-premiere-listen-to-demi-lovatos-new-song-heart-attack/ |publisher=RyanSeacrest.com |access-date=February 25, 2013 |date=February 24, 2013 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130226134145/http://ryanseacrest.com/2013/02/24/world-premiere-listen-to-demi-lovatos-new-song-heart-attack/ |archive-date=February 26, 2013 }}</ref>

==Composition==
"Heart Attack" is an [[electropop]] song,<ref name="billboard2">{{cite magazine|last=Lipshutz|first=Jason|title=Demi Lovato's 'Heart Attack' Single Causes Palpitations: Listen|url=https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/demi-lovatos-heart-attack-single-causes-palpitations-listen-1549863/|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|access-date=February 25, 2013|date=February 25, 2013}}</ref> with a length of three minutes and thirty seconds. The song is composed in the [[Key (music)|key]] of [[F minor]] and features a moderate [[tempo]] of 92 [[beats per minute]]. Lovato's vocal range spans from the low note of F<sub>3</sub> to the high note of G<sub>5</sub>.<ref>{{cite web|title=Heart Attack Demi Lovato Digital Sheet Music|url=http://www.musicnotes.com/sheetmusic/mtdFPE.asp?ppn=MN0114736|work=Music Notes| date=March 11, 2013 |access-date=April 28, 2013}}</ref> In the first verses, Lovato sings among a "booming" [[beat (music)|beat]]: "Never put my love out on the line/ Never said 'yes' to the right guy... When I don't care/ I can play 'em like a Ken doll/ Won't wash my hair/ Then make 'em bounce like a basketball."<ref name="MTV2"/> The song features a [[power pop]] [[refrain|chorus]] where Lovato sings accompanied by a [[guitar]].<ref name="MTV2"/>

==Commercial performance==
"Heart Attack" made its chart debut on the [[Mainstream Top 40]] chart at number 35. ''Billboard'' predicted the song would debut on the [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]] the following week on March 4, 2013, with an expected sales figure of 200,000 in first-week digital downloads, surpassing Lovato's "[[Skyscraper (song)|Skyscraper]]" (2011).<ref name="billboardtwitter"/> Having sold 215,000 digital copies in the first week, "Heart Attack" debuted at number 12 on the Hot 100 on the week ending on March 8, 2013, and at the time was the second highest debut sales week of any artist for 2013. Along with the [[lead single]]'s Hot 100 position, it debuted at number four on the [[Digital Songs]] chart, number 70 on the [[Radio Songs (chart)|Radio Songs]] chart, and just under the top twenty on the newly implemented [[Streaming Songs]] chart.<ref name="heartattackbillboard">{{cite magazine|last=Trust|first=Gary|title=Baauer's 'Harlem Shake' Still Atop Hot 100, Although Lead Shrinks|url=http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/1550739/baauers-harlem-shake-still-atop-hot-100-although-lead-shrinks|magazine=Billboard|access-date=March 7, 2013|location=New York|date=March 6, 2013}}</ref> On April 27, 2013, "Heart Attack" became Lovato's third top ten hit in the United States, reaching number 10.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/1558437/pink-powers-to-no-1-on-hot-100-with-just-give-me-a-reason|title=P!nk Powers to No. 1 on Hot 100 With 'Just Give Me A Reason'|last=Trust|first=Gary|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]] |access-date=April 22, 2013}}</ref>
In April 2013, "Heart Attack" was [[Record certification|certified gold]] by the [[RIAA]] with sales brimming 500,000 in the space of only four weeks and as of 2014 has sold over 2,000,000 copies in the United States.<ref name="usacert">{{cite web|url=https://www.riaa.com/goldandplatinumdata.php?resultpage=1&table=SEARCH_RESULTS&action=&title=ATTACK&artist=Demi+Lovato&perPage=25|title=RIAA - Gold & Platinum Searchable Database: Demi Lovato - Heart Attack|publisher=[[RIAA]]|access-date=April 28, 2013}}</ref>
In the United Kingdom and Ireland, "Heart Attack" debuted at number three, becoming Lovato's highest-charting single at the time in both countries, until "[[Solo (Clean Bandit song)|Solo]]", which reached number one on both charts in 2018. It received a platinum certification in the UK for sales exceeding 600,000 units.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/daft-punks-get-lucky-already-second-biggest-selling-single-of-the-year-2228/ |title=Daft Punk's Get Lucky score second biggest selling single of the year! |publisher=Officialcharts.com |date=May 19, 2013 |access-date=September 4, 2013}}</ref>

In January 2022, Lovato held a "funeral" for her pop music ahead of the release of her eighth studio album ''[[Holy Fvck]]'' in August of that year.<ref>{{Cite magazine|last=Bowenbank|first=Starr|date=January 21, 2022|title=Demi Lovato Hosts 'Funeral' Mourning Their Pop Music, Teases New Rock Song|url=https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/demi-lovato-funeral-pop-music-teases-rock-song-1235021727/|access-date=May 14, 2023|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|language=en-US|archive-date=June 7, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220607120002/https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/demi-lovato-funeral-pop-music-teases-rock-song-1235021727/|url-status=live}}</ref> The album, which embraced a heavier rock sound that departed from the pop sound of Lovato's previous releases, was supported by the [[Holy Fvck Tour]]. The setlist included several rock versions of her older pop songs, including "Heart Attack".<ref>{{cite web|first=Jared|last=Stossel|url=https://consequence.net/2022/09/demi-lovato-concert-review-2022-tour-setlist/|title=Demi Lovato Rages at "HOLY FVCK" Tour Stop in Sacramento: Review, Photos and Setlist|work=[[Consequence (publication)|Consequence]]|date=September 23, 2022|access-date=May 14, 2023}}</ref>

On March 20, 2023, Lovato announced via social media that she would release a studio recording of the rock version of "Heart Attack" to mark the song's 10th anniversary.<ref>{{Cite magazine|first=Rania|last=Aniftos|url=https://www.billboard.com/music/rock/demi-lovato-heart-attack-rock-version-1235289944/|title=Demi Lovato Is Putting a Rock Spin on This 2013 Track|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|date=March 20, 2023|access-date=May 14, 2023}}</ref> It was released digitally on March 24, 2023.<ref name="iHeartGonzalezRockVersion">{{cite web|first=Rebekah|last=Gonzalez|url=https://www.iheart.com/content/2023-03-24-demi-lovato-re-records-heart-attack-for-10-year-anniversary/|title=Demi Lovato Re-Records 'Heart Attack' For 10-Year Anniversary|work=[[iHeart Radio]]|date=March 24, 2023|access-date=May 14, 2023}}</ref>

===Production===
"Heart Attack (Rock Version)" was produced by [[Warren "Oak" Felder]], a frequent collaborator of Lovato who produced the majority of ''Holy Fvck'', and [[Mitch Allan]], who co-produced the original version.<ref name="EliteRockVersion">{{cite web|first=Adrianne|last=Reece|url=https://www.elitedaily.com/entertainment/demi-lovato-rock-version-heart-attack-differences|title=Demi Lovato's Rock Version of "Heart Attack" Goes Off|work=[[Elite Daily]]|date=March 24, 2023|access-date=May 14, 2023}}</ref> Felder explained that he initially found it challenging to remake "Heart Attack" because he "loved" the original, saying that "you just can't get the original version outta your head".<ref name="BillboardRockVersionProfile">{{cite magazine|first=Stephen|last=Daw|url=https://www.billboard.com/music/rock/demi-lovato-heart-attack-10-years-later-rock-version-1235290706/|title='Heart Attack' 10 Years Later: How Demi Lovato Reinvented a Seminal Hit After Going to 'Hell and Back'|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|date=March 24, 2023|access-date=May 14, 2023}}</ref> Lovato re-recorded her vocals; Felder stated that "Demi's voice now is a witness and testament to the things that she's been through as a person".<ref name="BillboardRockVersionProfile"/> He added that "there is a lot more experience and understanding of those emotions behind the way that she's singing it now" and that this, along with listening to Lovato's song "[[La La Land (Demi Lovato song)|La La Land]]" from her debut album ''[[Don't Forget]]'' (2008), helped him create a new rock production for "Heart Attack".<ref name="BillboardRockVersionProfile"/>

Lovato described herself as "a completely different person" at the time she recorded the original;<ref name="BillboardRockVersionProfile"/> she said in a press release, "I'm so happy to be able to give a new life to 'Heart Attack' with a sound that reflects where I am with my music. This one is for the fans who have shown so much love to the song over the last decade, thank you for riding with me!"<ref name="EliteRockVersion"/> Critics considered the intro as being reminiscent of the original song but described the sound of the re-recording as heavier. According to Tomás Mier of ''[[Rolling Stone]]'', "Heart Attack (Rock Version)" consists of "a heavy drum beat and electric guitar",<ref name="RollingStoneRockVersion">{{cite magazine|first=Tomás|last=Mier|url=https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/demi-lovato-releases-heart-attack-rock-version-1234702543/|title=Demi Lovato Reimagines 'Heart Attack' as a Rock Anthem for Song's 10th Anniversary|magazine=[[Rolling Stone]]|date=March 24, 2023|access-date=May 14, 2023}}</ref> while Adrianne Reece of ''[[Elite Daily]]'' noted "punk-styled drums and a gritty baseline".<ref name="EliteRockVersion"/> Both Mier and Reece called Lovato's re-recorded vocals "mature" and praised her high notes.<ref name="EliteRockVersion"/><ref name="RollingStoneRockVersion"/>
