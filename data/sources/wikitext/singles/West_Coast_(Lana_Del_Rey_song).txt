"'''West Coast'''" is a song by American singer-songwriter [[Lana Del Rey]] from her third [[Album#Types of album|studio album]], ''[[Ultraviolence (album)|Ultraviolence]]'' (2014). Written by Del Rey and [[Rick Nowels]], it is a [[Melancholia|melancholy]] [[love song]] about a woman torn between love and ambition, and as a dedication to the [[West Coast of the United States]]. A [[psychedelic rock]] and [[soft rock]] [[ballad]], the song was noted to be an evolution and more [[guitar]]-orientated in sound for Del Rey and was produced by [[Dan Auerbach]] of [[The Black Keys]]. Described as a two-in-one song, its shifting [[tempo]] transitions downward by nearly 60 beats per minute at the [[Refrain|chorus]] in a [[rhythm]]ical shift reminiscent of [[The Beatles]]' 1965 [[Single (music)|single]] "[[We Can Work It Out]]", introduced by the guitar [[Lick (music)|lick]] that begins The Beatles' 1964 single "[[And I Love Her]]". In the song, Del Rey's vocals comprises a more sensual and demented tone than in her previous releases, and are often sung in an anxious "[[Breathy voice|breathy]]" style.

Released as the [[lead single]] from ''Ultraviolence'' on April 14, 2014, "West Coast" received widespread acclaim from [[Music journalism|music critic]]s who commended its unconventional [[Composition (music)|composition]] and Del Rey's uncharacteristic vocal delivery. Several news media sources went on to list it as one of the best songs of 2014, including [[Consequence of Sound]], ''[[NME]]'' and ''[[Spin (magazine)|Spin]]''. "West Coast" debuted at number 17 on the [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]], becoming Del Rey's highest debut and third highest-charting single in the United States, while also being her first single to enter ''[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]''{{'s}} [[Billboard charts#Rock/Alternative|Rock Airplay]] chart where it reached number 26. "West Coast" marked Del Rey's eighth [[Top 40]] [[Hit song|hit]] in the United Kingdom where it peaked at number 21 on the [[UK Singles Chart]].

The accompanying [[black-and-white]] [[music video]], directed by Vincent Haycock, was released on May 7, 2014. Filmed at [[Marina del Rey, California|Marina del Rey]] and [[Venice, Los Angeles|Venice]], [[California]], it uses a minimalist and melancholy approach to depict Del Rey torn between two love interests, one of which is portrayed by [[Hollywood, Los Angeles|Hollywood]] tattoo artist [[Mark Mahoney]]. The music video was well received by critics and was nominated for a 2014 [[MTV Video Music Award for Best Cinematography]]. Del Rey promoted "West Coast" with live performances at [[Coachella Valley Music and Arts Festival]], [[Austin City Limits Music Festival]] and [[Glastonbury Festival 2014]]. lyrics and melody, and Nowels its chords.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.krone.at/Musik/Lana_Del_Rey_Ich_wurde_sehr_oft_missverstanden-Krone-Interview-Story-410322|title=Lana Del Rey: 'Ich wurde sehr alone,<ref name="Clash 5"/> and produced the track by herself from a guitar before later hiring session drummer Maximilian Weissenfeldt,<ref name="Notes"/> while Nowels would come by "every now and then".<ref name="Intro">"Ich will ich sein Lana Del Rey" (in German), ''Intro'', Germany, June 2014.</ref> Together, they produced the initial recording of "West Coast" as a classic rock-inspired song.<ref name="Clash 5">{{cite news|author=Zadeh, Joe|url=http://www.clashmusic.com/features/american-dreamer-lana-del-rey-interviewed|title=American Dreamer: Lana Del Rey Interviewed|work=[[Clash dedication to the [[West Coast of the United States]],<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.kiss957.com/onair/munchie-41790/12337716|title=Lana Del Ray opens up about the men who inspire her resulted in the track becoming distant from that of the [[verse–chorus form]]. She felt the song's slow, shifting [[tempo]] was important because it reflected her mental state during its writing. Del Rey initially completed her third studio album ''[[Ultraviolence (album)|Ultraviolence]]'' in December 2013,<ref name="Guardian Interview"/> but at the time was dissatisfied with a demo she had recorded for "West Coast".<ref name="CBS Interview">{{cite web|author=Sterling, Scott T.|url=http://radio.com/2014/06/12/lana-del-rey-interview-maleficent-ultraviolence/|title=Interview: Lana Del Rey Goes From 'Maleficent' to 'Ultraviolence' Up to the Top|publisher=[[CBS Corporation]]|date=June 12, 2014|access-date=February 8, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150209224102/http://radio.com/2014/06/12/lana-del-rey-interview-maleficent-ultraviolence/|archive-date=February 9, 2015|url-status=dead}}</ref> She then met [[Dan Auerbach]] of [[The Black Keys]] by chance at Electric Lady Studios and then again on a night out in [[Queens]], New York,<ref name="Complex Interview">{{cite news|author=Droppo, Dana|url=http://www.complex.com/covers/lana-del-rey-interview-against-the-grain-2014-cover-story/|title=Lana Del Rey Interview: Against the Grain 2014 Cover Story|work=[[Complex (magazine)|Complex]]|date=July 22, 2014|access-date=February 8, 2015|archive-date=April 1, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170401061938/http://www.complex.com/covers/lana-del-rey-interview-against-the-grain-2014-cover-story/|url-status=live}}</ref> and realized she needed to re-record the track to incorporate his looser production techniques.<ref name="Guardian Interview"/> That night Del Rey explained to Auerbach her interest to develop a song containing jazz undertones, "West Coast fusion" influenced by [[The Beach Boys]] and the [[Eagles (band)|Eagles]], and a [[1970s in music|1970s]] [[Laurel Canyon]]-type revival.<ref name="CBS Interview"/>

Subsequently, Del Rey traveled to [[Nashville, Tennessee]] to re-record "West Coast" and tracks from its parent album with Auerbach in a 3-week session at his studio,<ref name="Complex Interview"/> Easy Eye Sound.<ref name="Nashville"/> She invited him to add a more casual, California vibe to the song by recording in single takes,<ref name="Guardian Interview"/> with a blues-like [[Shure SM58]] microphone.<ref name="Intro"/> According to Auerbach, the recording of "West Coast" was sung "99% live" by Del Rey in a room adjacent to one where a seven-piece band were recording the track's instrumentation.<ref>{{cite web|author=Savage, Mark|url=https://www.bbc.com/news/entertainment-arts-27375409|title=The Black Keys on new album, rabid fans and Lana Del Rey|work=[[BBC News]]|date=May 13, 2014|access-date=February 8, 2014|archive-date=May 13, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140513194547/http://www.bbc.com/news/entertainment-arts-27375409|url-status=live}}</ref> Auerbach produced the song's electric guitar, [[shaker (percussion)|shaker]], [[12-string guitar|12-string acoustic guitar]] and synthesizer instrumentation, while [[Nick Movshon]] was credited as a bassist and drummer on "West Coast". The track's strings were recorded separately from the main recording at a studio in [[Glendale, California]] called The Bridge.<ref name="Notes"/>

Del Rey opined that the track's demo sounded very different to Auerbach's reproduction.<ref name="CBS Interview"/> When Del Rey first played "West Coast" to her record label, [[Interscope Records]], they were dissatisfied with its chorus being slower in beats per minute than its verses. They responded to her, stating, "None of these songs are good for radio and now you're slowing them down when they should be speeded up". In an interview with ''[[The Guardian]]'', Del Rey explained that she felt "murky" with her life during the song's writing, and it was that feeling which influenced it to sound disconnected from mainstream pop.<ref name="Guardian Interview">{{cite news|author=Jonze, Tim|url=https://www.theguardian.com/music/2014/jun/12/lana-del-rey-ultraviolence-album|title=Lana Del Rey: 'I wish I was dead already'|work=[[The Guardian]]|date=June 12, 2014|access-date=February 3, 2015|archive-date=December 25, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171225084058/https://www.theguardian.com/music/2014/jun/12/lana-del-rey-ultraviolence-album|url-status=live}}</ref>

Incorporating an ease to her vocal delivery,<ref name="AV Club"/> Del Rey eased her vocal delivery by crooning the hook "Move baby, move baby / Ooh baby, ooh baby" throughout the chorus shift,<ref name="Grantland"/> in a style that recalls [[Stevie Nicks]]' "[[Edge of Seventeen]]" (1982).<ref name="Music Times"/> Del Rey uses an inexpressive, ventriloquizing tone in the song, with her voice being layered into the track's production by means of [[reverberation]].<ref name="Music Times"/> In addition, her vocals use a more sensual and sinister tone than in her previous discography,<ref name="Grantland"/> and are often sung in an anxious and "breathy" style,<ref name="Guardian"/> and in an [[octave]] just above a whisper.<ref name="TheWrap">{{cite news|author=Bramlett, Matthew|url=http://www.thewrap.com/lana-del-rey-west-coast-video/|title=Lana Del Rey Dances in Flames in New 'West Coast' Music Video|work=[[TheWrap]]|date=May 7, 2014|access-date=February 3, 2015|archive-date=February 9, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150209233915/http://www.thewrap.com/lana-del-rey-west-coast-video/|url-status=dead}}</ref> She also sings the track's backing vocals which in part contribute to the song's ethereal feel,<ref name="AltSounds"/> through distant, faraway moans.<ref name="Pigeons"/> MuuMuse's Bradley Stern observed that Del Rey's voice skipped between "breathy franticness and slurred, drugged-out ecstasy".<ref name="MuuMuse"/> Throughout the song, she restrains her delivery during its verses, but sings in a sweeping, cinematic style in its chorus.<ref name="Exclaim"/>

"West Coast" was noted to be a cross between the works of bands [[Portishead (band)|Portishead]] and [[Beach House]].<ref name="Music Times"/> It was also highlighted that the track deviated from Del Rey's formula in ''[[Born to Die (Lana Del Rey album)|Born to Die]]'' and ''[[Paradise (Lana Del Rey EP)|Paradise]]'' (2012), as an evolution of their sound, though without doing so in its entirety.<ref name="Grantland"/> Harriet Gibsone of ''The Guardian'' described it as "side-stepping slowly from the ''[[Twin Peaks]]'' melodrama of ''Born to Die'',"<ref name="Guardian"/> while Tim Jonze of the same publication said the song "ditched the hip-hop trappings of ''Born to Die''".<ref name="Guardian Interview"/> Its eeriness, omnipresent in previous Del Rey tracks, takes on a full-on [[psychedelia]] sound, recalling [[The Turtles]]' "[[You Showed Me]]" (1969).<ref name="Grantland"/> "West Coast" is set in the simple [[time signature]] of {{music|time|4|4}}, with a moderately fast tempo of 123 beats per minute in its verses, and a slow tempo of 65 beats per minute in its chorus. It is composed in the [[key (music)|key]] of [[F-sharp minor|F{{music|sharp}} minor]], with Del Rey's vocals spanning the [[Vocal range|range]] from F{{music|sharp}}<sub>2</sub> to C{{music|sharp}}<sub>5</sub>. The track has basic a [[chord progression]] of F{{music|sharp}}m-F{{music|sharp}}m-E-D-F{{music|sharp}}m-E-D in its first verse, and Bm-D-F{{music|sharp}}m-F{{music|sharp}}m-C{{music|sharp}}m-D-D-E in its chorus.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.musicnotes.com/sheetmusic/mtdFPE.asp?ppn=MN0134435|title=Lana Del Rey 'West Coast' Sheet Music|date=April 25, 2014 |publisher=Musicnotes.com|access-date=February 10, 2015|archive-date=February 10, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150210020325/http://www.musicnotes.com/sheetmusic/mtdFPE.asp?ppn=MN0134435|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Critical reception==
"West Coast" received universal acclaim from [[Music journalism|contemporary music critic]]s.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://thehonestyhour.com/2014/07/new-music-travis-garland-west-coast-lana-del-rey-remix/|title=New Music: Travis Garland – 'West Coast' (Lana Del Rey Remix)|publisher=The Honesty Hour|date=July 10, 2014|access-date=February 15, 2015|archive-date=February 15, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150215104833/http://thehonestyhour.com/2014/07/new-music-travis-garland-west-coast-lana-del-rey-remix/|url-status=live}}</ref> Michael Nelson of ''[[Stereogum]]'' commented, "This is just a haunting, hypnotic piece of music that sends chills up and down my entire skeleton, especially when the tempo shifts downward on the chorus".<ref name="Stereogum"/> ''[[Nylon (magazine)|Nylon]]'' writer Liza Darwin deemed the track "silky-smooth" and "total ear candy".<ref name="Nylon">{{cite news|author=Darwin, Liza|url=http://www.nylon.com/articles/lana-del-rey-west-coast-listen|title=Lana Del Rey West Coast Listen|work=[[Nylon (magazine)|Nylon]]|date=April 14, 2014|access-date=February 3, 2015|archive-date=February 9, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150209223211/http://www.nylon.com/articles/lana-del-rey-west-coast-listen|url-status=live}}</ref> Lauren Valenti of ''[[Marie Claire]]'' lauded the song as "a slice of retro heaven".<ref name="Marie Claire 2"/> The track was noted by Stern to be "revolutionary" in the sense that it sounded "entirely different from anything being offered in pop music at the moment" and deemed it "the very antithesis of everything radio in 2014".<ref name="MuuMuse"/> Kyle Fowle of ''[[The A.V. Club]]'' named "West Coast" as the album's most accomplished track and "the one song on ''Ultraviolence'' that gets the formula of Lana Del Rey right".<ref name="AV Club"/> [[AltSounds]] deemed the track as one of Del Rey's best works and one of the best singles released in 2014. The publication went on to opine, "when songs defy descriptions, and artists defy expectation, they tend to stick around long after the 'pop hits' and divas have suck back into obscurity".<ref name="AltSounds"/> ''[[Oyster (magazine)|Oyster]]'' journalist Jerico Mandybur commented, "there's no denying that the song has excellent aural pulling power, especially if you like your odes to LA mysterious, darkly surfy and dripping in femme fatale".<ref name="Oyster">{{cite news|author=Mandybur, Jerico|url=http://www.oystermag.com/watch-lana-del-rey-west-coast|title=Watch: Lana Del Rey 'West Coast'|work=[[Oyster (magazine)|Oyster]]|date=May 8, 2014|access-date=February 3, 2015|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150209224030/http://www.oystermag.com/watch-lana-del-rey-west-coast|archive-date=February 9, 2015|df=mdy-all}}</ref>

Carolyn Menyes of the ''Music Times'' named "West Coast" as one of her favorite Del Rey songs to-date, highlighting Auerbach's influence as what benefited it most. In her review, she went on to opine, "Her voice plays off it well, too, and she actually feels passionate on this song, as opposed to her typical bored thing". While Caitlyn Carter, Joey DeGroot and Kyle Dowling of the same publication named it a stand-out on ''Ultraviolence''.<ref name="Music Times 3"/> Kate Hutchinson of ''The Guardian'' called it "one of the finest pop songs of the last five years".<ref name="Guardian 3"/> While [[Kitty Empire]] of the same publication wrote, "The engaging West Coast improves on every hearing, a clever two-in-one track rich with detail, rhythm, atmospherics and class".<ref name="Kitty Empire">{{cite news|author=Empire, Kitty|url=https://www.theguardian.com/music/2014/jun/15/ultraviolence-review-lana-del-ray-more-same|title=Ultraviolence review: Lana Del Ray delivers more of the same|work=The Guardian|date=June 15, 2014|access-date=February 8, 2015|archive-date=July 15, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210715155753/https://www.theguardian.com/music/2014/jun/15/ultraviolence-review-lana-del-ray-more-same|url-status=live}}</ref> ''[[Drowned in Sound]]''{{'s}} Robert Leedham said that with "West Coast", Del Rey developed a song to "trump" "[[Video Games (Lana Del Rey song)|Video Games]]" (2011), and a "new found guttural swagger". Leedham also commended Del Rey's key changes, "masterful" sway from tension, and reverberation in the song.<ref name="Drowned in Sound"/> Scott T. Sterling of the [[CBS Corporation]] opined that the track expanded the dimensions of Del Rey's sound,<ref name="CBS Interview"/> and that it was "one of the most distinctive hits of 2014".<ref name="CBS 2"/> ''[[The Boston Globe]]'' writer James Reed commented, "'West Coast' has a noirish sensuality, which opens into a chorus that mimicks the heady rush of a first toke".<ref>{{cite news|author=Reed, James|url=https://www.bostonglobe.com/arts/music/2014/06/16/lana-del-rey-ultraviolence/A8aGVFG9eAxTzMyNeuTVdO/story.html|title=Lana Del Rey, 'Ultraviolence'|work=[[The Boston Globe]]|date=June 16, 2014|access-date=February 8, 2015|archive-date=December 17, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141217090939/https://www.bostonglobe.com/arts/music/2014/06/16/lana-del-rey-ultraviolence/A8aGVFG9eAxTzMyNeuTVdO/story.html|url-status=live}}</ref>

Caryn Ganz of ''[[Rolling Stone]]'' called the track a "striking departure" in sound for Del Rey, and said its groove transformed her "from chanteuse into frontwoman for a few glorious moments".<ref>{{cite magazine|author=Ganz, Caryn|url=https://www.rollingstone.com/music/albumreviews/ultraviolence-20140620|title=Lana Del Rey Ultraviolence Album Review|magazine=Rolling Stone|date=June 20, 2014|access-date=February 8, 2015|archive-date=June 23, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140623133054/http://www.rollingstone.com/music/albumreviews/ultraviolence-20140620|url-status=dead}}</ref> In a review of ''Ultraviolence'', Mike Diver of ''[[Clash (magazine)|Clash]]'' said that in the context of the album, "West Coast" took on greater significance by serving as a fulcrum which balances the record into "leaving a better impression than it otherwise might".<ref>{{cite news|author=Diver, Mike|url=http://www.clashmusic.com/reviews/lana-del-rey-ultraviolence|title=Lana Del Rey – Ultraviolence|work=Clash|date=June 13, 2014|access-date=February 8, 2015|archive-date=June 4, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160604225049/http://www.clashmusic.com/reviews/lana-del-rey-ultraviolence|url-status=live}}</ref> ''[[Consequence of Sound]]''{{'s}} Sasha Geffen described the song as "a counterintuitive gem" and "the sort of thing that rings beautifully from old car windows on hot summer evenings,"<ref name="COS 3"/> while Marissa G. Muller of ''Vice'' called the track "one of the Golden State's best-ever anthems".<ref>{{cite news|author=Muller, Marissa G.|url=https://www.vice.com/en/article/the-church-of-lana-del-rey/|title=The Church of Lana Del Rey|work=Vice|date=October 22, 2014|access-date=February 13, 2015|archive-date=February 13, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150213184223/http://m.noisey.vice.com/en_dk/blog/the-church-of-lana-del-rey|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Commercial performance==
Despite not being heavily promoted to radio in the United States at the time, "West Coast" debuted at number 17 on the [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]] for the week ending May 3, 2014. It marked Del Rey's highest debut and her second-highest charting single on the chart, behind "[[Summertime Sadness]]" which peaked at number 6 in September 2013. 68% of the single's chart points in its opening week were attributed to digital sales of 118,000 copies, while the remaining 32% were attributed to 2.7&nbsp;million streams. "West Coast" consequently debuted at number 6 and number 16 on the [[Digital Songs]] and [[Streaming Songs]] charts respectively.<ref name="Billboard Debut">{{cite magazine|author=Caulfield, Keith|url=https://www.billboard.com/pro/lana-del-reys-west-coasts-onto-hot-100-at-no-17/|title=Lana Del Rey's 'West' Coasts Onto Hot 100 At No. 17|magazine=Billboard|date=April 23, 2014|access-date=February 6, 2015|archive-date=October 14, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141014165236/http://www.billboard.com/articles/columns/chart-beat/6062928/lana-del-reys-west-coasts-onto-hot-100-at-no-17|url-status=live}}</ref> The song fell off the ''Billboard'' Hot 100 in its second week, but re-entered the chart at number 100 on July 5, 2014, following the release of ''Ultraviolence''.<ref>{{cite web|author=Grein, Paul|url=https://www.yahoo.com/music/bp/chart-watch-ed-sheeran-week-000011685.html|title=It's Ed Sheeran's Week|work=[[Yahoo!]]|date=June 26, 2014|access-date=February 7, 2015|archive-date=February 9, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150209223752/https://www.yahoo.com/music/bp/chart-watch-ed-sheeran-week-000011685.html|url-status=live}}</ref> "West Coast" became Del Rey's first single to chart on the [[Billboard charts|Rock Airplay]] chart where it peaked at number 26 and spent a total of 9 weeks on the chart.<ref name="Rock"/>

===Development and release===
The accompanying music video for "West Coast" was directed by Vincent Haycock,<ref name="Oyster"/> and shot at [[Marina del Rey, California]] and in [[Venice, Los Angeles]],<ref name="Clash"/> in early April 2014.<ref name="Gigwise"/> Aside from Del Rey, the clip also features Hollywood tattoo artist [[Mark Mahoney]] who plays the singer's love interest in the clip's chorus segment,<ref name="Clash"/> and American model Bradley Soileau who plays the same role in another scene.<ref name="Refinery29">{{cite web|author=Manders, Hayden|url=http://www.refinery29.com/2014/05/67514/lana-del-rey-west-coast-music-video|title=Lana Del Rey West Coast Music Video – Ultraviolence|publisher=[[Refinery29]]|date=May 7, 2014|access-date=February 3, 2015|archive-date=May 10, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140510232930/http://www.refinery29.com/2014/05/67514/lana-del-rey-west-coast-music-video|url-status=live}}</ref> Del Rey described Mahoney as her [[confidante]], lover and [[Procuring (prostitution)|procurer]] in the video. ===Analysis and reception===
The music video for "West Coast" was noted to share several similarities with Del Rey's previous videography. Its black-and-white, old-school imagery recall her video for "Blue Jeans" and the introduction to "[[National Anthem (Lana Del Rey song)|National Anthem]]". While the home-recorded footage scenes were a technique previously used in "Video Games" and "Summertime Sadness", and its tattooed hand close-ups recall those of "National Anthem". One of Del Rey's love interests in the clip, Soileau, previously appeared in the same role in "Blue Jeans" and "[[Born to Die (Lana Del Rey song)|Born to Die]]", while another shares appearance similarities to her love interest in "[[Ride (Lana Del Rey song)|Ride]]". Del Rey's characteristic cat eye-make up and swaying actions were also highlighted.<ref name="E!"/> Speculation arose that her mature love interest in the visual was inspired by the "old man" character she sang of in "[[Off to the Races (song)|Off to the Races]]" and her then-fiancé, [[Kassidy|Barrie-James O'Neill]].<ref name="Contact"/> The clip garnered comparisons to [[Chris Isaak]]'s music video for "[[Wicked Game]]" (1990).<ref name="LA Times"/>

The visual was well received by music critics and was nominated for a 2014 [[MTV Video Music Award for Best Cinematography]], but lost to [[Beyoncé]]'s music video "[[Pretty Hurts]]".<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.capitalfm.com/events/mtv-vmas/news/video-music-awards-2014-winners/|title=MTV VMA 2014 Winners List Results In Full: Beyonce And Miley Cyrus Win Awards|publisher=[[Capital (radio network)|Capital]]|date=August 25, 2014|access-date=February 6, 2015|archive-date=August 13, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140813043348/http://www.capitalfm.com/events/mtv-vmas/news/video-music-awards-2014-winners/|url-status=live}}</ref>  Chad Saville of the [[Greenspun Media Group]] called the clip a "mysterious, yet artful celebration of all things California".<ref name="Saville">{{cite web|author=Saville, Chad|url=http://la-confidential-magazine.com/the-latest/people-and-parties/postings/bradley-soileau-on-working-with-lana-del-rey|title=Bradley Soileau Talks Working with Lana Del Rey on 'West Coast'|publisher=[[Greenspun Media Group]]|date=May 13, 2014|access-date=February 3, 2015|archive-date=May 17, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140517032314/http://la-confidential-magazine.com/the-latest/people-and-parties/postings/bradley-soileau-on-working-with-lana-del-rey|url-status=live}}</ref>  Opining that the music video was "Lana Being Lana," ''Billboard'' journalist Jason Lipshutz commented: "Will she burn her past and head towards the west coast in search of stardom?
