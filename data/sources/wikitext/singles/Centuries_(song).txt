"'''Centuries'''" is a song by American [[Rock music|rock]] band [[Fall Out Boy]], released September 9, 2014 as the lead single from their sixth studio album, ''[[American Beauty/American Psycho]]'' (2015).<ref>{{cite web|url=http://propertyofzack.com/post/103479459743/fall-out-boys-album-is-titled-american-beauty|title=Fall Out Boy's Album Is Titled 'American... — PropertyOfZack|work=propertyofzack.com}}</ref> Co-written by Fall Out Boy members and producers [[J. R. Rotem]] and Omega, the single reached number 10 on the US [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]], the band's fourth top ten hit and first in eight years, since "[[This Ain't a Scene, It's an Arms Race]]" in 2007, and was certified quadruple Platinum by the RIAA. It reached number one on the [[Lists of UK Rock & Metal Singles and Albums Charts number ones|UK Rock and Metal Chart]]. A gladiator-themed music video was created for the song. In 2015, "Centuries" was nominated for the [[Kerrang! Award for Best Single]].<ref>{{cite news|last1=Carter|first1=Emily|title=The Relentless Kerrang! Awards 2015 Winners List|url=http://www.kerrang.com/32514/relentless-kerrang-awards-2015-winners-list/|access-date=19 October 2015|work=Kerrang!|date=11 June 2015}}</ref> Fall Out Boy has played the song numerous times on televised performances, and it was used as [[ESPN]]'s official theme song for sports coverage.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Fall Out Boy Apologizes To America For ESPN Overplaying The Sh-t Out Of Their Song |url=https://www.mtv.com/news/ck5gni/fall-out-boy-apologizes-espn |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230710043325/https://www.mtv.com/news/ck5gni/fall-out-boy-apologizes-espn |url-status=dead |archive-date=July 10, 2023 |access-date=2023-07-10 |website=MTV |language=en}}</ref>

==Background==
"Centuries" was written by [[Pete Wentz]], [[Patrick Stump]], [[Joe Trohman]], [[Andy Hurley]], [[J.R. Rotem]], [[Justin Tranter]], [[Raja Kumari]] and Julian Lennartz in mid-2014 and was produced by Omega and Rotem. Stump originally came forward with the song while Fall Out Boy were on the [[Monumentour]] tour with fellow American band [[Paramore]].<ref name="FALL OUT BOY’S PETE WENTZ: THERE WILL BE AN ALBUM.">{{cite web |url=http://www.rocksound.tv/features/read/fall-out-boys-pete-wentz-there-will-be-an-album |title=Fall Out Boy's Pete Wentz: 'There Will Be An Album.' |publisher=Rock Sound |access-date=September 8, 2014 |archive-date=September 13, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140913041840/http://www.rocksound.tv/features/read/fall-out-boys-pete-wentz-there-will-be-an-album |url-status=dead }}</ref> In an interview with ''[[Kerrang!]]'', Wentz described the idea of the song as a "[[David]] vs. [[Goliath]] story", stating "We wanted to write a song that empowered people who are a little weird."<ref name=kerrang>{{cite web |url=http://www.kerrang.com/22452/fall-boy-stream-new-song-centuries/|title=FALL OUT BOY STREAM NEW SONG, CENTURIES' |work=[[Kerrang!]] |access-date=September 9, 2014}}</ref> Tranter later confirmed the song was partially inspired by [[Marsha P. Johnson]].<ref>{{Cite news |last=Reynolds |first=Daniel |date=October 19, 2017 |title=This Fall Out Boy Hit Is Actually About a Trans Pioneer |url=https://www.advocate.com/music/2017/10/19/fall-out-boy-hit-actually-about-trans-pioneer |access-date=June 14, 2026 |work=Advocate}}</ref>

The song contains part of the melody from the 1987 song "[[Tom's Diner]]" by [[Suzanne Vega]]. The segment is not a sample, but instead an [[Interpolation (popular music)|interpolation]] as it was actually re-recorded by American singer [[Lolo (singer)|Lolo]] for the track.<ref name="kroq">{{cite web |date=September 8, 2014 |title=Fall Out Boy Discusses New Song "Centuries," Forthcoming Album With Kat Corbett |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7nKoyPcp80o&t=71 |access-date= |website=[[YouTube]] |publisher=[[KROQ-FM]]}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Hear Fall Out Boy's 'Centuries': New Song Samples Suzanne Vega's 'Tom's Diner' |url=http://www.billboard.com/articles/6244059/fall-out-boy-centuries-suzanne-vega-sample|website=Billboard.com|access-date=June 20, 2024}}</ref> Stump described the inclusion as "a tip of the hat" to "Tom's Diner", a song which the band wanted to "re-inject" into [[popular culture]]. Vega is credited as a co-writer for "Centuries." The song is written in the key of [[E minor]].<ref>{{Cite web|last1=Fonseca|first1=Michael J.|first2=Andrew|last2=Hurley|last3=Kumari|first3=Raja|first4=Jonathan|last4=Rotem|first5=Patrick|last5=Stump|first6=Justin|last6=Tranter|first7=Joseph|last7=Trohman|first8=Suzanne|last8=Vega|first9=Peter|last9=Wentz|date=2014-09-26|title=Centuries|url=https://www.musicnotes.com/sheetmusic/mtd.asp?ppn=MN0139617|access-date=2021-06-08|website=Musicnotes.com}}</ref>

Days after the ending of co-headlining the [[Monumentour]] tour with [[Paramore]], on September 4, 2014, Fall Out Boy released a teaser video depicting the title of the song in [[Morse code]].<ref name="Centuries Teaser">{{Cite web |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VeOYu-zp0R4 |title=Centuries Teaser |publisher=[[YouTube]] |date=September 4, 2014 |access-date=September 4, 2020 |author=Fall Out Boy}}</ref> "Centuries" received its worldwide premiere on [[BBC Radio 1]] on September 8, 2014, before being released worldwide the next day.<ref name="Fall Out Boy Premiere New Song on Radio 1"/> The song is featured as the second track of ''[[Now That's What I Call Music! 54 (U.S. series)|Now! 54]]'' (2015). The same day as its release, the song impacted modern rock radio in the United States. It impacted contemporary hit radio in the country on September 16, and hot adult contemporary radio on October 6.<ref>[https://www.amazon.com/Thats-What-Call-Music-Vol/dp/B00UL90820 NOW That's What I Call Music Vol. 54]. Amazon.com. Retrieved June 6, 2015.</ref>

==Critical reception==
"Centuries" has received positive reviews from music critics. [[MTV]] described the song as "The ultimate battle cry of a track".<ref name="MTV – Fall Out Boy's ‘Centuries’ Is Here And DAMN">{{Cite web |url=http://www.mtv.com/news/1925215/fall-out-boy-centuries-single-release/|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140911063817/http://www.mtv.com/news/1925215/fall-out-boy-centuries-single-release/|url-status=dead|archive-date=September 11, 2014|title=Fall Out Boy's 'Centuries' Is Here And DAMN |publisher=[[MTV]] |date=September 9, 2014 |access-date=September 9, 2014 |author=Brenna Ehrlich }}</ref> While Under the Gun described its hook as the band "Taking yet another step in their evolution from pop punk giants to bonafide pop stars."

Vidette provided a positive review, stating {{"'}}Centuries' displays a definite pop vibe and certainly does not reflect much of the band's earlier albums. Fall Out Boy seems to have produced this song with greater musing in mind than their concept album." B-Sides on Air also applauded the song, noting how it acknowledges contemporary radio trends and calling it "An infusion of electronic elements to up the ante on the band's next step in their musical journey". However, the reviewer criticized Stump's vocals as "raw" and "overdone", causing the track to lose its "charm".

==Music video==
The official music video, which features the band members in a [[gladiator]]-style battle, and a cameo from rapper [[Rick Ross]], was released on October 17, 2014.<ref name="Fall Out Boy – Centuries (Official Video)">{{cite web |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LBr7kECsjcQ |title=Fall Out Boy – Centuries (Official Video) |publisher=[[YouTube]] |date=October 17, 2014 |access-date=October 17, 2014 |author=Fall Out Boy}}</ref> The video features strong Christian imagery and thematic elements, including a scene of a crucified figure, a cross in place of the "T" in the song title, the use of a [[sling (weapon)|sling]] against a "giant" (in reference to the Hebrew story of [[David]] and [[Goliath]]), and an angelic figure depicted as a human with white wings. The "giant" is portrayed by Canadian actor Jon Ambrose,<ref>[https://www.imdb.com/name/nm6152417/ Jon Ambrose on IMDb]. [[IMDb]]. Retrieved February 21, 2015.</ref> and the band's gladiator doubles were [[Simu Liu]],<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.imdb.com/name/nm4855517/|title=Simu Liu|website=[[IMDb]] |access-date=18 August 2016}}</ref> Ben Devries,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.imdb.com/name/nm7095214/|title=Ben Devries|website=[[IMDb]] |access-date=18 August 2016}}</ref> Sebastian Deery <ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.imdb.com/name/nm5951506/|title=Sebastian Deery|website=[[IMDb]] |access-date=18 August 2016}}</ref> and Paul Ebejer.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.imdb.com/name/nm1654755/|title=Paul Ebejer|website=[[IMDb]] |access-date=18 August 2016}}</ref>  "Centuries" was shot on-site at [[Fort Henry, Ontario|Fort Henry National Historic Site]] in Kingston, Ontario, Canada.<ref name="Secret video shoot wraps at Fort Henry">{{cite web |url=http://www.ckwstv.com/2014/08/29/secret-video-shoot-wraps-at-fort-henry/ |title=Secret video shoot wraps at Fort Henry |publisher=CKWS |date=August 29, 2014 |access-date=October 17, 2014 |author=CKWS}}</ref> Prior to the release of an official music video, Fall Out Boy released a video for "Centuries" filmed in [[Chicago]] featuring the use of the mobile app [[Hyperlapse (app)|Hyperlapse]] on September 8, 2014.<ref name="Fall Out Boy – Centuries (Hyperlapse Edition)">[http://tuftsdaily.com/arts/2014/09/18/fall-boys-new-single-centuries-provides-exciting-glimpse-upcoming-album-2/ Fall Out Boy's new single ‘Centuries’ provides exciting glimpse into upcoming album] tuftsdaily.com. Retrieved January 11, 2015.</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sCbS-TLEoRA |title=Fall Out Boy – Centuries (Hyperlapse Edition) |publisher=[[YouTube]] |date=September 8, 2014 |access-date=September 8, 2014 |author=Fall Out Boy}}</ref>

The official video features four gladiators doomed for some unknown reason prior to entering the [[Colosseum]] in [[Rome]] for the entertainment of the crowds. They are given two lengths of rope, a cloth, and a stone by a mysterious cloaked figure. This is followed by scenes of a woman styled as the Virgin Mary opening her arms to a crowd, another woman with two lions behind her, a "Christ" figure, and a decadent emperor spreading his arms showing several women sitting at his feet. The ostensible "crowd favourite", a large, muscular man, easily defeats the four, cheered on by the crowd and their laughing emperor. In desperation, the four, having realized that they need to work together, combine their items into a sling, which is then used to slay the warrior. The video ends as another, unknown challenger enters the ring while the emperor laughs.

==Commercial performance==
"Centuries" debuted at No. 22 on the US [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]] and No. 4 on Digital Songs with 133,000 first week sales.<ref>[http://headlineplanet.com/home/2014/09/17/fall-boys-centuries-debuts-133000-sales/ FALL OUT BOY’S “CENTURIES” DEBUTS WITH 133,000 SALES] headlineplanet.com. Retrieved September 20, 2014.</ref> It also opened at No. 2 on [[Hot Rock Songs]] and No. 14 on [[Alternative Songs]].<ref>[http://www.billboard.com/articles/6258918/ryan-adams-interpol-fall-out-boy-rock-charts-return Ryan Adams, Interpol, Fall Out Boy Return To Rock Charts] [[Billboard (magazine)|''Billboard'']]. Retrieved September 20, 2014.</ref> "Centuries" peaked at No. 4 on Alternative Songs in December on strong airplay, the band's third highest position on that chart behind "[[Dance, Dance (Fall Out Boy song)|Dance, Dance]]" and "[[Sugar, We're Goin Down]]". As a cross-over radio success, it reached No. 16 on mainstream [[Pop Songs]] by January 2015. In its nineteenth week on the Hot 100, it reached No. 12, selling 111,000 downloads that week, and propelling the band to No. 10 on the Artist 100.<ref>[https://www.billboard.com/pro/meghan-trainor-1-billboard-artist-100/ Meghan Trainor Dethrones Taylor Swift on Billboard Artist 100] ''Billboard'' Retrieved January 23, 2015.</ref> It eventually peaked at No. 10 on the ''Billboard'' Hot 100 in its 20th week (week ending February 7, 2015; the same as ''American Beauty/American Psycho'''s debut), becoming the band's first top 10 in almost eight years after "[[This Ain't A Scene, It's An Arms Race]]" peaked at No. 2 in February 2007;<ref>[https://www.billboard.com/pro/mark-ronson-bruno-mars-rule-hot-100-fall-out-boy-top-10/ Mark Ronson, Bruno Mars Rule Hot 100, Fall Out Boy Hits Top 10] ''Billboard''. Retrieved January 31, 2015.</ref> "Centuries" also reached a new high of No. 15 on Pop Songs. In its 21st week, it held at No. 10 on the Hot 100 and reached No. 13 on both Pop and Adult Pop with heavy airplay. It became their fourth and last top ten hit in the US.

"Centuries" has spent twelve consecutive weeks in the top 20 of the Hot 100 and twenty-two consecutive frames at No. 2 on Hot Rock Songs.<ref>[https://www.billboard.com/pro/fall-out-boy-uma-thurman-rock-chart/ Fall Out Boy's 'Uma Thurman' Dances Onto Hot Rock Songs Chart] ''Billboard''. Retrieved January 23, 2015.</ref><ref name="acharts">[http://acharts.us/song/87718 Fall Out Boy - Centuries]. acharts.us. Retrieved March 7, 2015.</ref> It was certified 2× Platinum by the [[Recording Industry Association of America|RIAA]] in December 2014 and was upgraded to 8× Platinum in November 2023.<ref name="RIAA"/> In Australia, the song debuted at No. 59 but rose to No. 55 in the following week. "Centuries" debuted at No. 36 on the [[Canadian Hot 100]] and peaked at No. 26 in its fifteenth week.<ref name="acharts"/> In the UK, "Centuries" made its appearance in the UK top 40 singles chart at No. 22, scoring the band another top 40 hit. On the singles sales format of the chart, the song reached No. 12. As of June 2015, the song has spent 21 weeks in the UK top 100 and has been certified 2× Platinum by the BPI, denoting 1,200,000 copies.<ref name="BPI"/>

==Media usage and live performances==

On September 10, 2014, [[ESPN]] announced "Centuries" as a promotional song for its [[ESPN College Football|coverage]] of the inaugural [[College Football Playoff]]. The song was featured across promotions for the event throughout the [[2014 NCAA Division I FBS football season|2014 college football season]], and during coverage of the semi-final and [[2015 College Football Playoff National Championship|national championship]] games.<ref name="MTV – Fall Out Boy's ‘Centuries’ Is Here And DAMN"/><ref name="espn-centuries">{{cite news|title=College playoff to feature Fall Out Boy|url=https://www.espn.com/blog/music/post/_/id/7648/college-football-playoff-to-feature-fall-out-boy|access-date=21 September 2014|publisher=ESPN|date=10 September 2014}}</ref> In a January 2015 interview with [[FoxSports.com]], the band apologized for ESPN's overexposure of the song; it was estimated that across its coverage of the two semi-final bowls, along with the National Championship game, that "Centuries" had been played a total of 45 times.<ref name="sportingnews-apology">{{cite web|title=Fall Out Boy apologizes for 'Centuries'|url=http://www.sportingnews.com/ncaa-football/story/2015-01-31/fall-out-boy-apologizes-for-centuries-played-during-college-football-playoffs|website=The Sporting News|access-date=2 February 2015|archive-date=2015-10-05|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151005070021/http://www.sportingnews.com/ncaa-football/story/2015-01-31/fall-out-boy-apologizes-for-centuries-played-during-college-football-playoffs|url-status=dead}}</ref>
