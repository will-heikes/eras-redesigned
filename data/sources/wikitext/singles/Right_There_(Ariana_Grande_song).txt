"'''Right There'''" is a song by American singer-songwriter [[Ariana Grande]], featuring American rapper [[Big Sean]]. It was released on August 6, 2013, by [[Republic Records]] as the third and final single from Grande's debut studio album ''[[Yours Truly (Ariana Grande album)|Yours Truly]]'' (2013), It was written by [[Harmony Samuels]], [[Carmen Reece|Helen "Carmen Reece" Culver]], [[Lonny Bereal|J. "Lonny" Bereal]], James "J-Doe" Smith, [[Al Sherrod Lambert]], Grande, Sean, and [[Jeff Lorber]]. It features a prominent sample of the jazz instrumental song "[[Water Sign (Jeff Lorber album)|Rain Dance]]" (1979) by [[The Jeff Lorber Fusion]]. The song is a throwback to [[Contemporary R&B|R&B]] music of the 1990s, a sonority that predominates the production of ''Yours Truly'' and the first single "[[The Way (Ariana Grande song)|The Way]]", which Grande considered "Right There" as a [[sequel]] to, that uses heavy programmed [[snare drum]]s, snaps and synthesizers as complement of its sound. In the lyrics, Grande expresses her passion to her love interest affirming that she will always be there for him, with Sean responding to her affirmation while using several [[double entendres]] in a more sexual perspective.

The song was praised by [[music critics]] who complimented its old-school production, which they felt recalled music released by [[Mariah Carey]] in the 1990s. In the United States, the song reached a peak of at number 84 on the [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]], while achieving top ten positions on the [[Dance Club Songs]] and [[Rhythmic (chart)|Rhythmic]] charts. The single was later [[Music recording certification|certified]] Platinum in the United States by the [[RIAA]], for selling over 1,000,000 copies.

Its accompanying music video was released on October 30, 2013, directed by the Young Astronauts team who added a concept inspired by [[William Shakespeare]]'s work ''[[Romeo and Juliet]]''. The video takes place at a [[Victorian era]] [[costume party]] in a mansion. Grande appears dressed as [[Juliet]], while [[Patrick Schwarzenegger]] makes a cameo as [[Romeo]]. The video received positive commentaries by some publications. To promote the song, Grande performed for some radio stations across the United States including [[KPWR|Los Angeles Power 106]] and [[Capital XTRA]], she also promoted it on the televised show, ''[[Jimmy Kimmel Live!]]''. The song was included as part of her set list for her first headlining tour called [[The Listening Sessions]] (2013), [[The Honeymoon Tour]] (2015) and the [[Sweetener World Tour]] (2019).

==Background and composition==
[[File:Big Sean 2015.jpg|thumb|left|180px|alt= A grey photo of a man on a stage holding a microphone in his left hand.|The rapper [[Big Sean]] (''pictured'') appears as featuring artist in "Right There." In his verses, Sean uses several [[double entendres]] about [[sexual attraction]].]]
"Right There" was written by [[Ariana Grande]], [[Harmony Samuels]], [[Carmen Reece|H. "Carmen Reece" Culver]],
[[Lonny Bereal|J. "Lonny" Bereal]], James "J-Doe" Smith, Al Sherrod Lambert, [[Jeff Lorber]] and [[Big Sean]]. Grande recorded her lines at the London Bridge's Studios in [[Los Angeles, California]], while Sean's verse was recorded at Studios de la Reine in [[Paris, France]]. "Right There" samples the 1979 jazz instrumental song "[[Water Sign (Jeff Lorber album)|Rain Dance]]" by [[The Jeff Lorber Fusion]]; it is the same sample that was used for the 1997 [[Lil' Kim]] song "[[Crush on You (Lil' Kim song)|Crush on You]]". Grande has referred to "Right There" as "sort of a sequel" to her first top-ten single "[[The Way (Ariana Grande song)|The Way]]" due to the songs' similarities in melody and composition. The singer spoke of the mutual interest she and rapper Big Sean felt in wanting to collaborate but mentioned that it took some time for them to find the right song. She said, "We were talking about a few other songs together but it just never worked out and it never was the right moment and then finally 'Right There' came along. And we were like 'Oh this is it'."<ref>{{cite web|title=Ariana Grande Taps 'Good Dude' Big Sean For 'The Way' Sequel|url=http://www.mtv.com/news/articles/1711378/ariana-grande-big-sean-the-way-sequel.jhtml|publisher=[[MTV News]]|first=Jocelyn|last=Vena|date=July 26, 2013|access-date=July 7, 2017|archive-date=November 3, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131103093452/http://www.mtv.com/news/articles/1711378/ariana-grande-big-sean-the-way-sequel.jhtml|url-status=dead}}</ref>

==Commercial performance==
In the United States, "Right There" debuted at its peak position of number 84 on the [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]] chart issued for August 24, 2013, giving Grande her fifth entry on the chart and Sean his eleventh.<ref>{{cite magazine|url= https://www.billboard.com/charts/hot-100/2013-08-24|title= Music: Top 100 Songs – 2013-08-24|date= August 24, 2013|magazine= Billboard|access-date= March 6, 2018|archive-date= February 19, 2018|archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20180219205106/https://www.billboard.com/charts/hot-100/2013-08-24|url-status= live}}</ref> It left the chart the following week and returned at number 100 with contributions from the music video, released on October 20, 2013.<ref>{{cite magazine|url= https://www.billboard.com/charts/hot-100/2013-11-16|title= Music: Top 100 Songs – 2013-11-16|date= November 16, 2013|magazine= Billboard|access-date= March 6, 2018|archive-date= February 19, 2018|archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20180219205132/https://www.billboard.com/charts/hot-100/2013-11-16|url-status= live}}</ref> The song spent eight weeks on the Hot 100 and placed in the top ten on other ''Billboard'' component charts including the [[Dance Club Songs]] and [[Rhythmic (chart)|Rhythmic]] charts, peaking at number eight in both.<ref name="Danceclub"/><ref name="Rhythmic"/> It was certified [[gold certification|gold]] by the [[Recording Industry Association of America]] (RIAA) on July 30, 2014, for selling over 500,000 units in the United States. In the United Kingdom, although it failed to appear on the [[UK Singles Charts]] top 100 – it eventually reached number 112 – the song found success on the [[UK R&B Chart]], where it reached number 15.<ref name="UKCharts"/><ref name="UKR&B"/> For the week ending August 24, 2013, "Right There" debuted at number 100 on the [[Gaon Chart|South Korean International Singles Chart]], becoming Grande's first entry on the chart.<ref name="SouthKorea"/>

===Critics reception===
Jocelyn Vena of MTV News found an "old school" vibe similar to the song's sound and to that of Grande's previous released single "[[Baby I]]" which also draws from 1990s influences. Cena also claimed that the video for "Right There" does not stray too far from the events written by Shakespeare and this is the kind of "schmaltz that Luhrmann would appreciate."<ref name="MTV14"/> Jason Lipshutz from ''Billboard'' considered the video as an understated tribute to the annual holiday [[Halloween]] – which started on October 31, a day following the video's release – for its modern-day "masquerade ball, complete with flowing gowns, wacky masks and Shakespearean nods."<ref name="Billb">{{cite magazine|url= https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/ariana-grande-big-sean-masquerade-in-right-there-video-watch-5770708/|title= Ariana Grande, Big Sean Masquerade in 'Right There' Video: Watch|magazine= Billboard|first= Jason|last= Lipshutz|date= October 30, 2013|access-date= March 6, 2018|archive-date= May 14, 2018|archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20180514031641/https://www.billboard.com/articles/columns/pop-shop/5770708/ariana-grande-big-sean-masquerade-in-right-there-video-watch|url-status= live}}</ref> The ''[[Los Angeles Times]]''{{'}}s Mikael Wood wrote that Grande puts her "well-established love of princess dresses to work" in the music video.<ref name="LATimes"/>
