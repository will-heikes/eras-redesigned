"'''Wrecking Ball'''" is a song recorded by American pop singer [[Miley Cyrus]] for her fourth studio album, ''[[Bangerz]]'' (2013). It was released on August 25, 2013, by [[RCA Records]], as the album's second single. The song was written by [[Mozella]], [[Stephan Moccio]], and [[Sacha Skarbek]], with [[Dr. Luke]] and [[Cirkut (record producer)|Cirkut]], who also served as the producers, credited as co-writers along with [[Kiyanu Kim|David Kim]]. The song was recorded from 2012 to 2013, across a multitude of studios in California and New York in the United States and Toronto in Canada. "Wrecking Ball" is a [[pop ballad]] which lyrically discusses the deterioration of a relationship.

"Wrecking Ball" debuted at number fifty on the [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]], and subsequently became Cyrus' first number-one song on the chart after the release of its controversial music video; it retained the peak position during the following week. Nine weeks later, the track returned to number one, and consequently had the largest gap between number-one sittings in ''Billboard'' Hot 100 history within a single chart run. Outside of the United States, "Wrecking Ball" topped the charts in Canada, Hungary, Israel, Lebanon, Spain, and the United Kingdom, and peaked within the top ten of the charts throughout much of continental Europe and Oceania. It was the best-selling song of 2013 in Finland. It is Cyrus's first and only song to crack the ''Billboard'' Decade-End chart, doing so at number 99 on the 2010s chart.<ref>{{Cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/charts/decade-end/hot-100|title=Hot 100 Songs - Decade-End|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]}}</ref> As of January 2014, "Wrecking Ball" had sold three million digital copies in the United States. It has been certified nine-times platinum in the US, eight-times platinum in Australia, five-times platinum in Norway, four-times platinum in Canada, triple platinum in Sweden, and double platinum in Italy, Spain, and the UK.

An accompanying music video for "Wrecking Ball" was released on September 9, 2013. It features close-up scenes of Cyrus tearfully singing, reminiscent of the video for "[[Nothing Compares 2 U]]" by [[Sinéad O'Connor]], interspersed with footage of a nude Cyrus swinging on an actual [[wrecking ball]]. Critics were divided in their opinions of the music video, feeling that it was more provocative than the video for her previous single "[[We Can't Stop]]". "Wrecking Ball" previously held the [[Vevo]] record for the most views in the first 24 hours after its release with 19.3 million views. At the [[2013 MTV Europe Music Awards]], Cyrus took home the award for Best Video and the award for Video of the Year at the [[2014 MTV Video Music Awards]]. Adding to the video's success, Cyrus won the award for World's Best Video at the [[2014 World Music Awards]] in Monte Carlo. Cyrus performed "Wrecking Ball" during several live performances, including the [[iHeartRadio]] Music Festival, an episode of ''[[Saturday Night Live]]'' and the "TikTok Tailgate" before [[Super Bowl LV]]. In 2023, Cyrus re-recorded the song as a duet with American [[Country music|country]] singer [[Dolly Parton]] for Parton's album ''[[Rockstar (Dolly Parton album)|Rockstar]]''.

==Background==
[[File:Beyonce.jpg|thumb|upright|"Wrecking Ball" was written with [[Beyoncé]] (''pictured'') in mind before being offered to Cyrus.]]

Before directly stating that "Wrecking Ball" was originally written with [[Beyoncé]] in mind, songwriter [[Sacha Skarbek]] tweeted "Beyonce song now becoming a Miley Cyrus song?!! Good/bad? I don't know??!!!!" in 2012.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Drewett |first=Meg |date=2014-01-03 |title='Wrecking Ball' started in BeyoncÃ© session |url=https://www.digitalspy.com/music/a541314/miley-cyruss-wrecking-ball-written-in-session-meant-for-beyonc/ |access-date=2023-12-10 |website=[[Digital Spy]] |language=en-GB}}</ref>

The song, and the cover of its parent album ''[[Bangerz]]'' (2013), were unveiled on August 25, 2013, before Cyrus' performance at the [[2013 MTV Video Music Awards]]. She released the cover artwork for "Wrecking Ball" on September 6, which shows Cyrus dressed in a sleeveless white shirt and underwear while swinging on a [[wrecking ball]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.sugarscape.com/main-topics/music/951308/miley-cyrus-unveils-wrecking-ball-artwork-and-announces-music-video-premier|title=Miley Cyrus unveils 'Wrecking Ball' artwork and announces music video premiere|author=Carl Smith|website=[[Sugarscape.com]]|date=September 6, 2013|access-date=January 15, 2014}}</ref> The song was serviced to [[contemporary hit radio]] stations in the United States on September 17.<ref name="CHR"/> It serves as the second single from ''Bangerz'', following the [[lead single]] "[[We Can't Stop]]", which was released in June.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.rcarecords.com/news/miley-cyrus-scores-her-first-billboard-hot-100-1-%E2%80%9Cwrecking-ball%E2%80%9D|title=Miley Cyrus Scores Her First Billboard Hot 100 #1 with 'Wrecking Ball'|publisher=[[RCA Records]]|date=September 18, 2013|access-date=January 26, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160306015326/http://www.rcarecords.com/news/miley-cyrus-scores-her-first-billboard-hot-100-1-%E2%80%9Cwrecking-ball%E2%80%9D|archive-date=March 6, 2016|url-status=dead}}</ref>

==Critical reception==
"Wrecking Ball" was met with praise from [[Music journalism|music critics]]. Writing for ''[[The A.V. Club]]'', Marah Eakin provided a favorable review, calling it a "solid ballad" and describing it as "a modern day '[[My Heart Will Go On]]' that discussed a deteriorated, shortlived young romance."<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.avclub.com/review/miley-cyrus-ibangerzi-103885|title=Miley Cyrus: Bangerz: Music: MusicalWork Review|author=Mariah Eakin|work=[[The A.V. Club]]|date=October 8, 2013|access-date=October 10, 2013}}</ref> [[Kitty Empire]] from ''[[The Observer]]'' opined that "the heartbreak section of ''Bangerz'' mostly repays your attention" and mentioned "Wrecking Ball" to be a part of it.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/culture/2013/oct/04/miley-cyrus-bangerz-review|title=Miley Cyrus: Bangerz – review|author=Kitty Empire|newspaper=[[The Observer]]|date=October 3, 2013|access-date=October 4, 2013}}</ref> Although he found the song "predictable," Evan Sawdey of ''[[PopMatters]]'' gave it an overall positive review and complimented its production and the build up to its chorus, calling it "the kind of broad mainstream song that shows you how to properly build up to a chorus before hitting us over the head with it.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.popmatters.com/review/175706-miley-cyrus-bangerz/|title=Miley Cyrus: Bangerz|author=Evan Sawdey|website=[[PopMatters]]|date=October 6, 2013|access-date=October 10, 2013}}</ref> Jon Dolan of ''[[Rolling Stone]]'' felt that it "[rides] the hunger and confusion that make great coming-of-age pop."<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.rollingstone.com/music/albumreviews/bangerz-20131004|title=Miley Cyrus 'Bangerz' Review|author=Jon Dolan|magazine=Rolling Stone|date=October 4, 2013|access-date=October 4, 2013}}</ref> Writing for [[AllMusic]], Heather Phares considered the track to be a standout from ''Bangerz''.<ref name=AllMusic>{{cite web|url=https://www.allmusic.com/album/bangerz-mw0002577863|title=Bangerz – Miley Cyrus|author=Heather Phares|website=[[AllMusic]]|date=October 8, 2013|access-date=October 6, 2013}}</ref>

Writing for ''[[Digital Spy]]'', Robert Copsey questioned Cyrus' decision to premiere a ballad in the midst of controversies regarding her "salacious exploits", but enjoyed the song itself and opined that it "proves that there's method behind the madness."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.digitalspy.com/music/news/a515810/miley-cyrus-wrecking-ball-single-review.html|title=Miley Cyrus: 'Wrecking Ball' – Single review|author=Robert Copsey|website=[[Digital Spy]]|date=September 16, 2013|access-date=January 27, 2014}}</ref> In a more mixed review, Joseph Atilano from the ''[[Philippine Daily Inquirer]]'' appreciated the lyrics for seeming heartfelt, but felt that its production was "comparatively weaker" by comparison to her earlier projects.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://entertainment.inquirer.net/115965/single-review-wrecking-ball-by-miley-cyrus|title=Single Review: 'Wrecking Ball' by Miley Cyrus|author=Joseph Atilano|newspaper=[[Philippine Daily Inquirer]]|date=October 9, 2013|access-date=January 27, 2014}}</ref> Mikael Wood of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' felt that the track proved that Cyrus "isn't just a [[Twerking|twerk]]-bot programmed to titillate", but suggested that her "singing throbs with what feels like an embarrassment of emotion" and found it peculiar that the song discussed the singer's relationship with Hemsworth when her public behavior seemingly suggested that she "couldn't care less about" it.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.latimes.com/entertainment/music/posts/la-et-st-miley-cyrus-review-20131002-story.html|title=Review: Miley Cyrus' 'Bangerz' a rebel yell with a big bang|author=Mikael Wood|newspaper=[[Los Angeles Times]]|date=October 2, 2013|access-date=October 2, 2013}}</ref>

After a month of voting, on March 31, 2014, ''[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]'' declared the song to be the winner of the 2014 Hot 100 March Madness and their favorite Top 5 single from the past year. It defeated songs like "[[Holy Grail (Jay-Z song)|Holy Grail]]" by [[Jay-Z]] and [[Justin Timberlake]], "[[Best Song Ever (song)|Best Song Ever]]" by [[One Direction]], "[[Applause (Lady Gaga song)|Applause]]" by [[Lady Gaga]], "[[Timber (Pitbull song)|Timber]]" by [[Pitbull (rapper)|Pitbull]] and [[Kesha]] and finally "[[Just Give Me a Reason]]" by [[Pink (singer)|Pink]] and [[Nate Ruess]].<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/hot-100-march-madness-2014-the-winner-is-6029403/|title=Hot 100 March Madness 2014: The Winner Is…|date=March 31, 2014|magazine=Billboard|access-date=August 4, 2014}}</ref>

==Commercial performance==
Before officially being serviced as a single, "Wrecking Ball" made a "hot shot debut" at number 50 on the US [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]] for the week issued on September 7, 2013, with 90,000 downloads in two days.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/biz/articles/news/chart-alert/5679851/chart-moves-lorde-keeps-leaping-with-royals-miley-cyrus-debuts|title=Chart Moves: Lorde Keeps Leaping With 'Royals'; Miley Cyrus Debuts Next Single; Keith Urban Tops Country Airplay|author=Gary Trust|magazine=Billboard|date=August 30, 2013|access-date=January 18, 2014}}</ref> It reached number 14 the following week with 201,000 downloads.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/articles/news/5680131/katy-perry-dethrones-robin-thicke-atop-hot-100|title=Katy Perry Dethrones Robin Thicke Atop Hot 100|author=Gary Trust|magazine=Billboard|date=September 4, 2013|access-date=January 18, 2014}}</ref> After its official premiere as the second single from ''Bangerz'', the track peaked at number one in the United States in its fourth week, heavily assisted by online streaming credits from its recently released music video; this gave Cyrus her first number-one single in the country. It sold 477,000 copies that week, becoming the third-highest single sales week of 2013, behind the 582,000 units moved by "[[I Knew You Were Trouble]]" by [[Taylor Swift]] and the 557,000 units moved by "[[Roar (song)|Roar]]" by [[Katy Perry]].<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/articles/news/5695590/miley-cyrus-wrecking-ball-swings-to-top-of-hot-100|title=Miley Cyrus' 'Wrecking Ball' Swings Atop Hot 100|author=Gary Trust|magazine=Billboard|date=September 18, 2013|access-date=January 18, 2014}}</ref> In December, "Wrecking Ball" returned to the peak position after being stuck at number two or three the previous nine weeks as Lorde's "[[Royals (Lorde song)|Royals]]" topped the Hot 100, with assistance from a viral parody video and consequently became the largest gap between number-one sittings in ''Billboard'' history within a single chart run. The next week it tumbled to number 5 as Eminem's "The Monster" featuring Rihanna topped the chart.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/articles/news/5812407/miley-cyrus-wrecking-ball-swings-back-to-no-1-on-hot-100|title=Miley Cyrus' 'Wrecking Ball' Swings Back To No. 1 On Hot 100|author=Gary Trust|magazine=Billboard|date=December 4, 2013|access-date=December 5, 2013}}</ref> As of December 2014, "Wrecking Ball" has sold 3,640,000 copies in the United States.<ref name=us_sales>{{cite web |url=http://www.hitsdailydouble.com/news/newsPage.cgi?news10227m01 |title=The Big 9 in 2014: RCA |website=HITS Daily Double |date=December 11, 2014 |access-date=December 11, 2014 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://archive.today/20141211200721/http://www.hitsdailydouble.com/news/newsPage.cgi?news10227m01 |archive-date=December 11, 2014 }}</ref> The track peaked at number one on the [[Canadian Hot 100]],<ref>{{cite magazine|url=http://www.billboard.com/artist/311278/Miley+Cyrus/chart?f=793|title=Miley Cyrus – Chart history|magazine=Billboard|access-date=January 18, 2014}}</ref> and was later certified quadruple-platinum in the country.<ref name=Platinum4/> It reached the peak position on the Mexico Ingles Airplay.<ref name="Mexico">{{cite magazine|url=http://www.billboard.com/biz/charts/2013-11-02/mexico-ingles|title=Mexico Ingles Airplay|magazine=Billboard|date=November 2, 2013|access-date=January 18, 2014}}</ref>

In the United Kingdom, "Wrecking Ball" and ''[[Bangerz]]'' both debuted at the top of the [[UK Singles Chart]] and [[UK Albums Chart]] respectively on October 13, 2013 – for the week ending date October 19, 2013 – making Cyrus the first artist of 2013 to achieve an [[Official Charts Company]] "chart double".<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/entertainment-arts-24513946|title=Miley Cyrus achieves UK chart double|work=BBC News|date=October 13, 2013|access-date=October 13, 2013}}</ref> It was later certified platinum by the [[British Phonographic Industry]].<ref name="UKcert" /> Additionally, the song became Cyrus' second chart-topping song in Britain after "[[We Can't Stop]]" in August 2013. In Belgium, "Wrecking Ball" peaked at numbers four and five on the Belgian [[Ultratop]] in the Flanders and Wallonia regions respectively,<ref name="Charts">{{cite web|url=https://www.ultratop.be/nl/song/121956/Miley-Cyrus-Wrecking-Ball|title=Miley Cyrus – Wrecking Ball|publisher=[[Ultratop]]|access-date=January 18, 2014}}</ref> and was eventually awarded a gold certification.{{Certification Cite Ref|region=Belgium|title=Wrecking Ball|artist=Miley Cyrus|type=single|certyear=2013|access-date=January 18, 2014}} In Germany, "Wrecking Ball" peaked at number six on the [[German Singles Chart]],<ref name="Charts"/> and was later certified platinum.{{Certification Cite Ref|region=Germany|title=Wrecking Ball|artist=Miley Cyrus|type=single|access-date=August 6, 2014}}

==Music video==

===Synopsis===
An accompanying music video for "Wrecking Ball" was directed by [[Terry Richardson]], filmed in July 2013 and was premiered through [[Vevo]] on September 9, 2013. The clip features close-up footage of Cyrus emotionally singing to the camera against a white backdrop, having been inspired by the music video for "[[Nothing Compares 2 U]]" by [[Sinéad O'Connor]].<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.nydailynews.com/2013/10/04/sinead-oconnor-pens-open-letter-to-miley-cyrus-dont-let-them-make-a-prostitute-of-you/|title=Sinead O'Connor pens open letter to Miley Cyrus: Don't let them 'make a prostitute of you'|author1=Chiderah Monde|author2=Stephen Rex Brown|work=[[Daily News (New York)|Daily News]]|date=October 3, 2013|access-date=January 15, 2014}}</ref> Interspersed throughout are scenes of Cyrus licking a [[sledgehammer]] and swinging on a wrecking ball. At first, she is shown dressed in a midriff-baring tank top, panties and maroon [[Dr. Martens|Doc Martens]] boots; as the video progresses, she is shown wearing only the boots.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=http://popstyle.ew.com/2013/09/09/wrecking-ball-miley-cyrus-isnt-totally-naked-shes-wearing-shoes/|title='Wrecking Ball' Miley Cyrus isn't totally naked – she's wearing shoes|author=Lindsey Scharf|magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]]|date=September 9, 2013|access-date=January 14, 2014|url-status=dead|archive-date=February 21, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140221155848/http://popstyle.ew.com/2013/09/09/wrecking-ball-miley-cyrus-isnt-totally-naked-shes-wearing-shoes/}}</ref>

The music video received generally negative reviews from critics, who were divided in their opinions regarding Cyrus's increasingly provocative image. Writing for ''[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]'', Jason Lipshutz stated that the "nude Cyrus shown straddling a swinging wrecking ball" was the most surprising piece of the clip.<ref name="jason">{{cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/miley-cyrus-strips-swings-around-naked-in-wrecking-ball-video-5687261/|title=Miley Cyrus Strips, Swings Around Naked in 'Wrecking Ball' Video: Watch|author=Jason Lipshutz|magazine=Billboard|date=September 9, 2013|access-date=January 15, 2014}}</ref> The staff from ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'' joked that viewers would be "scandalized/titillated/disappointed in [[Billy Ray Cyrus]]'s parenting skills" after seeing his daughter nude and "fellating a sledgehammer".<ref>{{cite magazine|url=http://music-mix.ew.com/2013/09/09/miley-cyrus-wrecking-ball-video/|title=Miley Cyrus takes it all off in new 'Wrecking Ball' video – Watch it here|magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]]|date=September 9, 2013|access-date=January 15, 2014}}</ref> Writing for ''[[The Guardian]]'', Michael Hann criticized Cyrus's attempts to distance herself from her innocent image from starring in the teen TV drama series ''[[Hannah Montana]]''. He  disapproved of the manner in which she transitioned into a career beyond her childhood success, specifically panning her for "exploring the iconography of [[porn]]."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/music/musicblog/2013/sep/10/miley-cyrus-wrecking-ball|title=Miley Cyrus's new Wrecking Ball video says young women should be sexually available|author=Michael Jann|newspaper=[[The Guardian]]|date=September 10, 2013|access-date=January 15, 2014}}</ref> As of January 2015, ''Billboard'' named the video as the fourth-best music video of the 2010s (so far).<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/best-music-videos-2010s-6436761/|title=The 20 Best Music Videos of the 2010s (So Far)|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|access-date=January 15, 2015}}</ref>

James Montgomery from [[MTV News]] noted that the controversy surrounding Cyrus's nudity in the video was "to be expected" given her recent controversies, and complimented Richardson "for toning down the ''salacious'' scenes (if only for an instant), and allowing the softer, more genuine sides to shine through."<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.mtv.com/news/1713726/miley-cyrus-wrecking-ball-video/|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140521084536/http://www.mtv.com/news/1713726/miley-cyrus-wrecking-ball-video/|url-status=dead|archive-date=May 21, 2014|title=Miley Cyrus Strips Down (In More Ways Than One) With 'Wrecking Ball' Video|author=James Montgomery|website=MTV News|date=September 9, 2013|access-date=January 15, 2014}}</ref> Mikael Wood of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' opined that the music video tried to paint Cyrus in a more serious light with its scenes of her emotionally singing and crying.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.latimes.com/entertainment/music/posts/la-et-ms-watch-video-wrecking-ball-miley-cyrus-20130909-story.html#ixzz2qVv7tQIP|title=Watch the video for 'Wrecking Ball' by Miley Cyrus|author=Mikael Wood|newspaper=Los Angeles Times|date=September 9, 2013|access-date=January 15, 2014}}</ref>
