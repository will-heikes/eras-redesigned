"'''Worth It'''" is a song by American [[girl group]] [[Fifth Harmony]], featuring American rapper [[Kid Ink]].<ref>{{cite web|last=Lindner|first=Emilee|url=http://www.mtv.com/news/2053459/fifth-harmony-worth-it-dirty-kid-ink/|archive-url=https://archive.today/20150602190019/http://www.mtv.com/news/2053459/fifth-harmony-worth-it-dirty-kid-ink/|url-status=dead|archive-date=June 2, 2015|title=Is Fifth Harmony's New Song 'Worth It' Even Bossier Than 'Bo$$'?|publisher=MTV|access-date=January 20, 2015|date=January 20, 2015}}</ref> It impacted American [[rhythmic crossover]] radio on March 2, 2015, as the third and final single from the group's debut studio album, ''[[Reflection (Fifth Harmony album)|Reflection]]'' (2015). Written by [[Priscilla Renea]] and its producers [[Stargate (music producers)|Stargate]], "Worth It" is a [[dance-pop]] and [[Contemporary R&B|R&B]] song that incorporates a strong use of [[Balkan music]] and [[Middle Eastern music]] in its production. The lyrics discuss themes of [[feminism]], self-worth and confidence, and include [[double entendre]]s.

The song became a [[sleeper hit]] on the US [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]] where it peaked at number 12, marking the group's highest-charting single at the time<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/pro/omi-cheerleader-third-week-hot-100/|title=OMI's 'Cheerleader' No. 1 for Third Week on Hot 100 &#124; Billboard|magazine=Billboard|date=July 27, 2015 |access-date=September 13, 2015}}</ref> and the first top 20 single by a girl group in the US since "[[Jai Ho! (You Are My Destiny)]]" by [[The Pussycat Dolls]] in 2009. It reached number one in Lebanon, number three on the [[UK Singles Chart]], and the top 10 in Australia and Hungary. It is certified Quadruple Platinum in the US and Gold or higher in eleven additional countries. The song has over 1.1 billion streams on [[Spotify]]. Directed by Cameron Duddy, the music video has themes of female empowerment with Fifth Harmony dominating men as business executives. It has received over two billion views on [[YouTube]]. The group promoted "Worth It" with several televised performances, including the [[Dancing with the Stars (U.S. season 21)|season twenty-one]] finale of ''[[Dancing with the Stars]]'', ''[[Jimmy Kimmel Live!]]'' and at the [[2015 MTV Europe Music Awards]].

==Background and development==
[[File:Ori Kaplan.jpg|thumb|215px|alt=|[[Ori Kaplan]], who produced "[[Talk Dirty (Jason Derulo song)|Talk Dirty]]" by [[Jason Derulo]], also co-produced and co-wrote "Worth It".]]
[[File:KidInkMarch2012.jpg|thumb|right|Kid Ink, pictured in March 2012, features in the song.]]
"Worth It" was produced and co-written by Norwegian production company, [[Stargate (production team)|Stargate]], consisting of duo Mikkel S. Eriksen and Tor Erik Hermansen and [[Ori Kaplan]], with additional writing from [[Priscilla Renea]]. The song was released on January 15, 2015, on streaming and downloading services but officially impacted American [[rhythmic crossover]] radio as a single on March 2, 2015.<ref name="Top 40 Rhythmic Future Releases">{{cite web|title=Top 40 Rhythmic Future Releases|url=http://www.allaccess.com/top40-rhythmic/future-releases|publisher=All Access Media Group|access-date=January 30, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150130230409/http://www.allaccess.com/top40-rhythmic/future-releases|archive-date=January 30, 2015}}</ref> A Spanish version subtitled "'''Dame Esta Noche'''" ([[Spanish language|Spanish]] for "Give Me This Night") was released to [[iTunes]] on July 10, 2015, following the success of the single.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://music.apple.com/us/album/worth-it-dame-esta-noche-feat-kid-ink-single/1018370557?app=itunes|title=Worth It (Dame Esta Noche) [feat. Kid Ink&#93; - Single by Fifth Harmony on iTunes|publisher=iTunes|date=July 10, 2015|access-date=September 13, 2015}}</ref> The track features Kid Ink's English rap verse, while the group sings in Spanish.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/music/latin/fifth-harmony-dame-esta-noche-worth-it-spanish-version-6633540/|title=Fifth Harmony Drop 'Dame Esta Noche,' Spanish Version of 'Worth It': Listen|magazine=Billboard|access-date=September 13, 2015}}</ref>

Originally intended for recording by rapper [[Kid Ink]], who ended up collaborating on the song, the lyrics and melody were changed to fit a female perspective. Kid Ink noted during an interview with ''[[Forbes]]'' that while he was "writing on their album", he was able to collaborate with "producers Stargate and [[Cashmere Cat]]" and this led to the development of the song.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.forbes.com/sites/shawnsetaro/2015/03/31/kid-inks-furious-rise-to-the-top/|title=Kid Ink's Furious Rise To The Top|magazine=Forbes|date=March 31, 2015|access-date=September 13, 2015}}</ref> One of Kid Ink's verses from "Wit It" (the original version of the song) appears on this song.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dpsEa-3JMQg|title=Kid Ink - Wit It (Feat. The Ranger$)|publisher=YouTube|date=March 5, 2015|access-date=September 13, 2015}}</ref> Speaking to ''[[Complex (magazine)|Complex]]'', Dinah Jane explained that the song was unplanned for the album, saying "there were no females on the song". After the song was presented to them by production team Stargate, they changed part of the lyrics and bits "of the concept" to "fit [their] perspective". Lauren Jauregui also shared similar sentiments, saying that she "didn't think" the track would "make the album".<ref>{{cite magazine|url=http://www.complex.com/music/2015/06/fifth-harmony-interview-worth-it-reflections-tour|title= Interview: Fifth Harmony Talk Their Platinum Hit "Worth It" and Their Love for Drake, Fetty Wap, and Kendrick Lamar|magazine= Complex|date= June 22, 2015|access-date= March 10, 2017}}</ref> In an interview with ''[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]'', music director David Armbrecht explained that the song is a "message of [[self-empowerment]], which is made clear in its triumphant 'Give it to me, I'm worth it!' chorus". The song speaks to women of "all demographics", he said, explaining that this can be applied to anyone in a "relationship" or "getting out of one" and it is likely that one can get "tired of the games" and deserve better, using the song to do just that.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/pro/fifth-harmony-selena-gomez-demi-lovato-hot-100/|title=Hot 100 Chart Moves: Fifth Harmony Ups Its 'Worth,' Selena Gomez & Demi Lovato Gain|magazine=Billboard|date= July 22, 2015|access-date= March 29, 2017}}</ref>

Musically, "Worth It" incorporates [[dance-pop]] and [[contemporary R&B]] with influences of [[hip hop music|hip hop]] and [[Balkan music]].<ref name="Salon">{{cite news|author=Sonia Saraiya |url=http://www.salon.com/2015/08/11/im_worth_it_how_a_self_help_refrain_became_the_pop_anthem_of_the_summer/|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150814024442/http://www.salon.com/2015/08/11/im_worth_it_how_a_self_help_refrain_became_the_pop_anthem_of_the_summer/|url-status=live|archive-date=August 14, 2015|title="I'm worth it": How a self-help refrain became the pop anthem of the summer|work=[[Salon (website)|Salon]]|date=August 11, 2015|access-date=September 13, 2015}}</ref><ref name="m.mic.com">{{cite web|author=Mic|url=http://m.mic.com/articles/121460/14-recent-pop-songs-that-are-basically-the-same-song|title=14 Recent Pop Songs That Are Basically the Same Song - Mic|publisher=.Mic|date=June 30, 2015|access-date=September 13, 2015}}</ref><ref name="time">{{cite magazine|author=Jamieson Cox|url=https://time.com/3692465/fifth-harmony-reflection-album-review/|title=Fifth Harmony 'Reflection' Album Review|magazine=Time|date=February 3, 2015|access-date=April 6, 2015|archive-date=April 15, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150415051627/http://time.com/3692465/fifth-harmony-reflection-album-review/|url-status=dead}}</ref><ref name="mtv1">{{cite web|url=http://www.mtv.com/news/2053459/fifth-harmony-worth-it-kid-ink/|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150121073003/http://www.mtv.com/news/2053459/fifth-harmony-worth-it-kid-ink/|url-status=dead|archive-date=January 21, 2015|title=Is Fifth Harmony's New Song 'Worth It' Even Bossier Than 'Bo$$'?|publisher=MTV|date=January 20, 2015|access-date=September 13, 2015}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://poptius.com/2015/01/22/fifth-harmony-get-horny-with-worth-it-review/ |title=Fifth Harmony Get Horny With "Worth It" (Review)|publisher=Poptius|date=January 22, 2015|access-date=September 13, 2015}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://axs.com/the-top-10-best-fifth-harmony-songs-79234|title=The Top 10 Best Fifth Harmony Songs|work=AXS |date=May 21, 2016|access-date=March 29, 2017}}</ref> The song contains elements from a variety of music genres. It can be noted mainly in its instrumentation, which incorporates a [[Balkan music|Balkan]]-inflected [[saxophone]],<ref name="Salon"/><ref>{{cite magazine|url=http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/billboard-lists/6859179/zumba-pop-playlist|title= Pop Workout Playlist: 15 Body-Movin' Tunes to Rock Your Fitness Routine|magazine= Billboard|date= February 2, 2016|access-date= March 29, 2017}}</ref> [[trance music|trance]] [[synth]]s and a [[Trap music (hip hop)|trap]]-inspired [[Roland TR-808]] [[drum]] machine.<ref name =pitchfork>{{cite web|last=Garvey|first=Meaghan|date=February 11, 2015|url=http://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/20253-reflection/|title=Fifth Harmony:Reflection|publisher=[[Pitchfork Media]]|access-date=February 11, 2015}}</ref> Songwriting and production is largely based on previous Kaplan and Stargate's productions, most notably [[Jason Derulo]]'s "[[Talk Dirty (Jason Derulo song)|Talk Dirty]]", which shows similar song scheme and use of [[horn (instrument)|horn]]s during the [[Motif (music)|hook]].<ref name="fuse">{{cite web|url=http://www.fuse.tv/videos/2015/03/fifth-harmony-worth-it-video-kid-ink|title=Fifth Harmony Step Up as New-Age Feminist Icons in "Worth It" Video|website=[[Fuse (TV channel)|Fuse]] |access-date= June 13, 2015|date=March 30, 2015}}</ref><ref name="pitchfork"/><ref name="m.mic.com"/> The song is written from the perspective of a woman telling a man that she is "Worth It", implying sexual connotation.<ref name="plugged">{{cite web|url=http://www.pluggedin.com/music-reviews/track/fifth-harmony-worth-it/|title= Fifth Harmony - Wroth It|work= Plugged In|date= March 3, 2015|access-date= March 6, 2017}}</ref> However, it can be considered as a [[feminism|feminist]] anthem, according to Jeff Benjamin from ''[[Fuse (magazine)|Fuse]]'', it can inspire "young girls to truly believe they're 'Worth It' and can own [[Wall Street]] or any other place on which they set their sights". This latter view of the song is highlighted in its music video.<ref name="fuse"/>

The song opens with a [[saxophone]] sample that introduces the repetitive hook, where the group sings repeatedly the phrase "Give it to me, I'm worth it", backed by heavy [[bass (sound)|bassline]] and "trappy beats"<ref name="fuse"/><ref name="Salon"/><ref name="plugged"/><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.theedgesusu.co.uk/records/singles/2015/07/08/review-fifth-harmony-ft-kid-ink-worth-it/|title=Review: Fifth Harmony ft. Kid Ink – 'Worth It'|publisher=The Edge|date=July 18, 2015|access-date= March 29, 2017}}</ref><ref name="Digitalspy"/> Followed by [[Kid Ink]]'s rapped verse, Emilee Linder of [[MTV]] noted he insists that they "bring it back like they left something".<ref name="mtv1"/> Member [[Dinah Jane]] is then introduced, back by [[Clapping|handclaps]]. [[Normani|Normani Kordei]] performs the pre-chorus (which is then performed by [[Ally Brooke]] after the second verse),<ref name="m.mic.com"/> while the second verse is sung by [[Camila Cabello]], who delivers the song's most suggestive lines, "Come harder just because/I don't like it, like it too soft/I like it a little rough/Not too much, but maybe just enough".<ref name="mtv1"/><ref name="plugged"/> [[Lauren Jauregui]] is the only member of the band that doesn't sing a solo in the song. According to the sheet music published by [[Sony Music Publishing]] at Musicnotes.com, the song is written in the key of [[C minor]] and is set in a  {{music|time|4|4}} [[time signature]] with a moderate [[tempo]] of 100 [[beats per minute]]. Fifth Harmony and Kid Ink's vocal range spans from the low note of G<sub>3</sub> to the high note of A{{music|flat}}<sub>5</sub>. The saxophone's melody spans the tonal range of E<sub>4</sub> to C<sub>6</sub>.

==Critical reception==
"Worth It" received mixed reviews from music critics, receiving praise for its catchy rhythm and attitude but was criticized for sounding similar to Jason Derulo's "Talk Dirty". Rick Florino from [[Artistdirect]] praised "Worth It", giving it a five out of five star rating, claiming the song has "an empowering refrain that's impossible to shake, especially when coupled with a slippery beat".<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.artistdirect.com/entertainment-news/article/album-review-fifth-harmony-reflection-5-out-of-5-stars/11314051|title=Album Review: Fifth Harmony "Reflection" – 5-out-of-5 stars|date=February 4, 2015|access-date=April 12, 2015|archive-date=September 23, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150923200223/http://www.artistdirect.com/entertainment-news/article/album-review-fifth-harmony-reflection-5-out-of-5-stars/11314051|url-status=dead}}</ref> FDRMX also reviewed it positively, mentioning that "the production is solid and the lyrics are catchy" and also stating that the single is Fifth Harmony's most mature record yet.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://fdrmx.com/fifth-harmony-worth-single-review/|title=REVIEW: Fifth Harmony: 'Worth It' Single Review|date=January 20, 2015|access-date=June 13, 2015|archive-date=June 14, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150614175514/http://fdrmx.com/fifth-harmony-worth-single-review/|url-status=dead}}</ref> Several music publications considered it a strong contender for song of the summer.<ref>{{cite magazine|last1=Trust|first1=Gary|title=Song of the Summer: Taylor Swift, Britney Spears & Iggy Azalea, Maroon 5 and Other Early Contenders|url=https://www.billboard.com/pro/2015-song-of-the-summer-taylor-swift-britney-spears-iggy-azalea-maroon-5/|magazine=Billboard|access-date=July 3, 2017|date=May 15, 2015}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|last1=Lamarre|first1=Carl|title=Interview: Fifth Harmony Talk Their Platinum Hit "Worth It" and Their Love for Drake, Fetty Wap, and Kendrick Lamar|url=http://www.complex.com/music/2015/06/fifth-harmony-interview-worth-it-reflections-tour|website=Complex|access-date=July 3, 2017|date=June 22, 2015}}</ref><ref>{{cite 2015}}</ref>

Rebecca Mattina, from ''Andpop'' gave the song a mixed review, noting too-close a similarity to "[[Talk Dirty (Jason Derulo song)|Talk Dirty]]" but also stating that the track is "filled with lots of attitude" and rhythmic bounce.<ref name="andpop">{{cite web|url=http://www.andpop.com/2015/01/27/track-track-review-reflection-fifth-harmony/|title=Track-by-Track Review: Reflection by Fifth Harmony|date=January 27, 2015|access-date=April 12, 2015|archive-date=February 4, 2015|archive-url=https://archive.today/20150204112749/http://www.andpop.com/2015/01/27/track-track-review-reflection-fifth-harmony/|url-status=dead}}</ref> Conversely, Music Times gave it a positive review stating 'the intense, creeping music of "Worth It" is matched with deep harmonies from 5H and personally positive lyrics". They made a comparison to the first lead single of ''Reflection'' (2015), "[[Boss (Fifth Harmony song)|Boss]]".<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.musictimes.com/articles/25224/20150120/fifth-harmony-worth-it-review-listen-stream-reflection.htm|title=REVIEW: Fifth Harmony Prove They're "Worth It"|date=January 20, 2015|access-date=June 13, 2015}}</ref> In a review for their debut album, ''Reflection'' (2015), Meaghan Garvey of ''[[Pitchfork (website)|Pitchfork]]'' commented that the song is an "exhaustive exquisite corpse of practically every radio hit of the last year and a half" and criticizes the "wholly unremarkable rap verse" performed by Kid Ink.<ref>{{cite web|last1=Garvey|first1=Meaghan|title=Fifth Harmony: Reflection Album Review|url=http://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/20253-reflection/|website=Pitchfork|access-date=June 18, 2017}}</ref>

In a negative review, Jason Lipshutz from ''[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]'', stated that the song sounded "oddly incomplete." Lipshutz also highlighted the song's "repetitive phrasing."<ref name=Billboard>{{cite magazine|last=Lipshutz|first=Jason|date=February 2, 2015|url=http://www.billboard.com/articles/review/6458265/fifth-harmony-reflection-track-by-track-review|title=Fifth Harmony Shows Who's Bo$$ on 'Reflection': Track-by-Track Review|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|publisher=[[Prometheus Global Media]]|access-date=February 3, 2015}}</ref> In a mixed-positive review, Amy Davidson, from ''[[Digital Spy]]'', noted that "trumpets and saxophones have experienced a resurgence [and Fifth Harmony] have laid it on thick for some serious sax-sampling. 'Give it to me I'm worth it,' they determine, dripping with sass." Davidson also stated, "It mightn't be the most groundbreaking of tracks, but it's got enough attitude to forgive that."<ref name="Digitalspy">{{cite web|last=Davidson|first=Amy|url=http://www.digitalspy.co.uk/music/feature/a656469/this-weeks-new-singles-reviewed-years--years-fifth-harmony-conor-maynard.html#~piYY7pv98mUCRv|title=This week's new singles reviewed: Years & Years, Fifth Harmony, Conor Maynard - Music Feature|website=Digital Spy|date=July 5, 2015|access-date=September 13, 2015}}</ref> For their year-end list of best songs, ''[[Associated Press]]'' listed it at number three<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.berkshireeagle.com/entertainment/ci_29274230/associated-press-rates-best-songs-2015|title=The Associated Press rates the best songs of 2015|work=Berkshire Eagle|access-date=March 3, 2016|archive-date=March 2, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160302235347/http://www.berkshireeagle.com/entertainment/ci_29274230/associated-press-rates-best-songs-2015|url-status=dead}}</ref> and ''[[Spin (magazine)|Spin]]'' ranked it at 68.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.spin.com/2015/11/the-101-best-songs-of-2015/2/|title=The 101 Best Songs of 2015|work=Spin|date=November 30, 2015 }}</ref> [[Artistdirect]] ranked the song at number one in their mid-year report.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.artistdirect.com/entertainment-news/article/15-best-songs-of-2015-so-far/11752359|title=15 Best Songs of 2015 So Far|work=Artistdirect|access-date=March 30, 2016|archive-date=October 14, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161014135601/http://www.artistdirect.com/entertainment-news/article/15-best-songs-of-2015-so-far/11752359|url-status=dead}}</ref>

===Synopsis===
The video begins with shots of the skyline of [[Hong Kong]], where most of the setting takes place. An image of a stock market appears, zooming in on what appears to say, "@FifthHarmony [[Women in positions of power|women in power]]". The girls then appear in silhouettes and begin walking confidently as men stand next to a wall, appearing to stand in attention to the ladies. Individual shots of every girl are shown, giggling and smiling at the camera, before switching back to the stock market background. The group perform a choreographed dance move, and a [[Newton's cradle]] is shown being used, in rhythm to the song. The words "break through the [[glass ceiling]]" appear in stock market background, a reference to the obstacles that minorities, particularly minority women, have when climbing the corporate ladder.
[[File:Fifth Harmony - Worth It ft. Kid Ink Music Video.png|thumb|250px|right|alt=|The members of [[Fifth Harmony]] dress in business-esque attire, demonstrating themes of [[feminism]] and [[Self-esteem|self-worth]].]]
The verse changes to Kid Ink, who is dressed in almost all-black clothing, he doesn't move his body and is stationary, which is to show he is being forced into humiliation. He appears to be sitting in an office room with two attractive women approaching him as he sings. The women are dressed in business attire and are shown to have green eyes, in this case it is to show their possessiveness over him, tricking him into signing a fake cheque into fraud. A man is shown cleaning Dinah's shoe, as she controls him. She then kicks him to show she is not controlled by anyone. The scene shifts to Normani, who is sporting a fashionable business outfit with a white blazer. She then dramatically stands up from her chair, while a man, who appears to be her accountant, looks at her in a frightened motion. Just like the women sitting next to Kid Ink, he also appears to have green eyes, but in this case it is to show his jealousy of them, as shown in Shakespeare. All members perform a dance move, when the setting shifts to Camila getting ready to play golf in her office. Ally is then shown with a man who also appears to be her accountant, similar to Normani. Lauren is seen in the back of a convertible black car with a man in the driver's seat. Each girl either flicks the man next to them or kicks them in frustration.

The ladies are now in the stock market background, telling their lover that they're "Worth It". They also seem to rush their accountants, who are continuously typing on their calculators. The words ''"[[feminism]] is sexy"'' appear in the stock market background, before switching to Kid Ink, where the women move in-sync to the rhythm as he raps. The camera zooms into the eyes of a woman, with her eyes appearing bright green. The group perform more choreographed dance moves individually and as a group. Shots of all the members winking their eye, smiling, and showing confidence are shown. The video ends with the group facing away from the camera and looking at the stock market behind them, then turn around and move their hands upward, a similar ending to the group's past videos.

It is also incorporated some elements from "[[All in My Head (Flex)]]" and [[Leon Haywood]]'s "I Want'a Do Something Freaky to You" during the pre-recorded Kid Ink's verse. Despite the departure of former member Camila Cabello, her pre-recorded vocals was remained intact in chorus parts.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Irvin |first1=Jack |title=Fifth Harmony Reunites for First Performance Since 2018 as Surprise Guests at Jonas Brothers' Dallas Show |url=https://people.com/fifth-harmony-reunites-first-performance-since-2018-jonas-brothers-dallas-11801129 |website=People.com |date=1 September 2025|access-date=8 September 2026}}</ref>
