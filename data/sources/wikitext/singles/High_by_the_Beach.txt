"'''High by the Beach'''" is a song recorded by American singer-songwriter, and record producer [[Lana Del Rey]] for her fourth studio album, ''[[Honeymoon (Lana Del Rey album)|Honeymoon]]'' (2015). Written by Del Rey, [[Rick Nowels]], and Kieron Menzies, it was released as the [[lead single]] from the album on August 10, 2015. A [[synthesizer|synth]]-led [[Trap music (hip hop)|trap]]-[[pop music|pop]] and [[power pop]] [[Sentimental ballad|ballad]], it is more uptempo and pop-indebted than Del Rey's previous releases but prominently recalls the hip hop and [[trip hop]] influences of her 2012 album, ''[[Born to Die]]''. The song is based around [[Electronic music|electronic]] production, a trap beat, and an orchestral [[Organ (music)|organ]] arrangement.

"High by the Beach" received widespread acclaim, with critics praising the song's production, Del Rey's vocals and cited it as more radio-friendly than her previous songs. It debuted at number 51 on the [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]] a week after its release, spending three weeks on the chart altogether.<ref name="Billboard.com"/> The song also ranked at number 89 in [[Triple J Hottest 100, 2015|Triple J's Hottest 100 songs of 2015]] in Australia.

==Background==
"High by the Beach" was written by Lana Del Rey, [[Rick Nowels]], and Kieron Menzies<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.qobuz.com/fr-fr/album/high-by-the-beach-lana-del-rey/0060254752447|archive-url=https://archive.today/20150813190249/http://www.qobuz.com/fr-fr/album/high-by-the-beach-lana-del-rey/0060254752447|url-status=dead|archive-date=August 13, 2015|title=High By The Beach|language=fr|publisher=[[Qobuz]]|access-date=August 11, 2015}}</ref> and was recorded as one of the last tracks for Del Rey's ''Honeymoon''. Its development began with its chorus, which was inspired by a period when Lana Del Rey frequently drove by the beach.<ref name="Rolling Stone">{{cite magazine|author=Reed, Ryan|url=https://www.rollingstone.com/music/news/hear-lana-del-reys-synth-driven-new-song-high-by-the-beach-20150810|title=Hear Lana Del Rey's Synth-Driven New Song, 'High By the Beach'|magazine=[[Rolling Stone]]|date=August 10, 2015|access-date=August 11, 2015}}</ref> In an interview for [[Apple Music]]'s Beats 1, she said: "It had an [[Andrews Sisters]] vibe. The harmony sounded almost monotone. It had this weird drone to it, but with this beat it had a trap aspect".<ref name="Entertainment Weekly">{{cite magazine|author=Goodman, Jessica|url=https://www.ew.com/article/2015/08/10/lana-del-rey-high-by-the-beach|title=Lana Del Rey's 'High by the Beach' is her catchiest song yet|magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]]|date=August 10, 2015|access-date=August 11, 2015}}</ref>

Lyrically, "High by the Beach" is a self-assured kiss-off [[torch song]]<ref name="Stereogum" /><ref name="HitFix" /> containing several lyrical themes, including [[self-deprecation]], [[nihilism]], independence, and indolence.<ref name="New York" /><ref name="Stereogum" /><ref name="HitFix" /> A representation of the challenges of staying in love,<ref name="MTV News" /> it specifically details Del Rey being worn down by life and love, and in turn seeking sweet escape near the ocean to enjoy [[recreational drug use]].<ref name="Rolling Stone"/><ref name="Out"/> Also an evisceration of an unworthy ex-lover,<ref name="Slate"/><ref name="Stereogum"/> it features Del Rey lashing out in anger, telling her former partner, "Don't need your money, money to get me what I want / Lights, camera, acción / I'll do it on my own"—a reference to her early track "Put Me in a Movie" (2010).<ref name="Rolling Stone"/><ref name="Digital Spy 2"/><ref name="DIY"/> According to Jessica Goodman of ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'', the song is "a tongue-in-cheek meditation discarding the extra folks in your life, and quite literally, getting high by the beach".<ref name="Entertainment Weekly"/> Goodman noted that with the lyric, "You could be a bad motherfucker, but that don't make you a man. Now you're just another one of my problems because you got out of hand/ We won't survive, we're sinking into the sand," Del Rey "throws an old lover under the bus".<ref name="Entertainment Weekly"/> Erin Jensen of ''[[USA Today]]'' viewed it as a "bitter breakup ballad",<ref>{{cite news|author=Jensen, Erin|url=http://entertainthis.usatoday.com/2015/08/10/lana-del-rey-high-by-the-beach-new-single-latest-song/|title=Listen: Lana Del Rey is here to cure your 'Summertime Sadness' with her new song|work=[[USA Today]]|date=August 10, 2015|access-date=August 11, 2015}}</ref> while Hosken suggested that the song "could just represent a rough patch in an overall dramatic but very intense relationship".<ref name="MTV News"/> Hunt said that the lyrics were deceptive, writing, "Sacking off no good men, money-slinging bullshitters, and empty tributes in favour of beachy hedonism on 'High By The Beach,' this song is really a song about reveling in the escape of being yourself".<ref name="DIY"/> Del Rey's atypically assertive lyrics in the song drew comparisons to [[Rihanna]]'s "[[Bitch Better Have My Money]]" (2015).<ref name="New York"/><ref name="DIY"/><ref name="Idolator"/>

==Critical reception==
"High by the Beach"  received critical acclaim. Jason Lipshutz of ''Billboard'' deemed it "perhaps the most radio-friendly song of [Del Rey's] entire career", and praised the "gloriously whiny bounce" of its chorus and "sparkling Lana wisdom" in its lyrics.<ref name="Billboard"/> Similarly, ''[[Slant Magazine]]''{{'}}s Sal Cinquemani said, "Though it's an understated single by today's pop standards, [...], it's handily Del Rey's catchiest single since '[[Summertime Sadness]]', or at least '[[National Anthem (Lana Del Rey song)|National Anthem]]'".<ref name="Slant Magazine"/> Cinquemani also complimented Del Rey's "crisp" vocal and the track's "hypnotic" synth line, and quipped, "lazy, revenge- and smoke-filled summer days never sounded so sweet".<ref name="Slant Magazine"/> Jessica Goodman of ''Entertainment Weekly'' deemed it Del Rey's catchiest single yet, praising its "surprising" hip hop elements.<ref name="Entertainment Weekly"/> Writing in ''[[Time (magazine)|Time]]'', Nolan Feeney said that "High by the Beach" was Del Rey's "poppiest song in a while" and highlighted its "skittering beat and dizzying hook".<ref>{{cite magazine|author=Feeney, Nolan|url=https://time.com/3990519/lana-del-rey-high-by-the-beach/|title=Lana Del Rey Just Wants to Get 'High By the Beach' on Her New Song|magazine=[[Time (magazine)|Time]]|date=August 10, 2015|access-date=August 11, 2015}}</ref> Feeney's view was echoed by Constant Gardner of [[Pigeons & Planes]] who felt "High by the Beach" was "a pop song surely destined for radio play".<ref name="Pigeons & Planes"/> ''[[Stereogum]]''{{'}}s Peter Helman found "High by the Beach" to be "positively ''fun''" and "probably the straight-up catchiest thing she's done in a while", complimenting its "sultry keys" and the "refreshing independence" of its lyrics.<ref name="Stereogum"/> Rory Cashin of ''[[State (magazine)|State]]'' also felt the song was "far more commercially minded" and added, "if it wasn't for that title and the heavy drug references, we'd imagine this would fit quite snuggly on the charts and radio rotation".<ref>{{cite news|author=Cashin, Rory|url=http://state.ie/news/new-music-lana-del-rey-high-by-the-beach|title=New Music: Lana Del Rey – 'High By The Beach'|work=[[State (magazine)|State]]|date=August 9, 2015|access-date=August 11, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150810043133/http://state.ie/news/new-music-lana-del-rey-high-by-the-beach|archive-date=August 10, 2015|url-status=dead}}</ref>

Patrick Hosken of MTV News commented, "Lana's knack for capturing summer in songs is on point here".<ref name="MTV News"/> Similarly, [[HitFix]]'s Marcus Ezra stated that the track proved that Del Rey "knows how to do summer right", and praised its drum instrumentation which he felt lent the song "a harder edge".<ref name="HitFix"/> Writing in ''[[New York (magazine)|New York]]'', Jillian Mapes called the song "sonic perfection" and a late contender for the "Song of the Summer", and opined that it indicated that Del Rey "hasn't abandoned her whole 'gangster [[Nancy Sinatra]]' thing, and it might not want to make you cringe anymore".<ref name="New York"/> Mapes also commended the track's "several anthemic lines" and felt that despite it "reaching peak Sad Girl nihilism", it worked as "a chilled-out trap-pop single you can blast as a comedown from 'Bitch Better Have My Money'".<ref name="New York"/> Tommy Pisani of ''[[Out (magazine)|Out]]'' called the song a "new [[420 (cannabis culture)|420 anthem]]" and said its "strong harmonies" turned it into "the perfect end-of-summer jam".<ref name="Out"/> ''NME'' writer Leonie Cooper lauded the song as the "Slow-Burning Sound Of Late Summer".<ref name="NME"/> Robin Murray of ''[[Clash (magazine)|Clash]]'' opined that it was a "gorgeous, hazy summer hymn".<ref>{{cite news|author=Murray, Robin|url=http://www.clashmusic.com/news/listen-lana-del-rey-high-by-the-beach|title=Listen: Lana Del Rey – 'High By The Beach'|work=[[Clash (magazine)|Clash]]|date=August 11, 2015|access-date=August 11, 2015}}</ref>

Yasmeen Gharnit of ''[[Nylon (magazine)|Nylon]]'' opined that Del Rey had "perhaps delivered her greatest hazy anthem".<ref name="Nylon"/> Ian David Monroe of ''[[V (American magazine)|V]]'' wrote that the song marked "an evolved artist" and "sultry Lana at her best", and complimented its "surprising" trap beat.<ref>{{cite news|author=Monroe, Ian David|url=http://www.vmagazine.com/site/content/4701/getting-high-with-lana-del-rey|title=Getting High With Lana Del Rey|work=[[V (American magazine)|V]]|date=August 11, 2015|access-date=August 12, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150813225548/http://www.vmagazine.com/site/content/4701/getting-high-with-lana-del-rey|archive-date=August 13, 2015|url-status=dead}}</ref> ''[[Paper (magazine)|Paper]]'' writer Sandra Song deemed the ballad "an absolute stunner", commending Del Rey's "otherworldly" voice and highlighting the song's "most melancholy set of vintage California church keys you could imagine".<ref name="Paper"/> Jack Russell of ''[[American Songwriter]]'' called it "an interesting sound" and "everything and nothing like what we've come to expect from [Del Rey]".<ref name="American Songwriter"/> El Hunt of ''DIY'' praised the song's "unapologetic" production and Del Rey's coy, saccharine delivery which he felt lent itself to "one of the best deliveries of 'bullshit' this year".<ref name="DIY"/> While Laura Bradley of ''[[Slate (magazine)|Slate]]'' wrote, "Now, as the title suggests, all she wants to do is get high by the beach, and it's hard to resist coming along for the ride".<ref name="Slate"/>

''[[Rolling Stone]]'' ranked "High By the Beach" at number 18 on its year-end list of the 50 best songs of 2015.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.rollingstone.com/music/lists/50-best-songs-of-2015-20151203/titus-andronicus-dimed-out-20151203|title=50 Best Songs of 2015|date=December 3, 2015|magazine=[[Rolling Stone]]|access-date=February 15, 2017}}</ref> ''[[Pitchfork (website)|Pitchfork]]'' ranked the song at number 75 on its year-end list of the 100 best songs of 2015.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://pitchfork.com/features/lists-and-guides/9765-the-100-best-tracks-of-2015/?page=3|title=The 100 Best Tracks of 2015|last=Beauchemin|first=Molly|date=December 14, 2015|website=[[Pitchfork (website)|Pitchfork]]|access-date=February 15, 2017}}</ref>

==Commercial performance==
Due to an error in calculation by Nielsen, "High by the Beach" was originally reported to have debuted on the [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]] chart at number 7 with 248,000 copies sold. The error was corrected on the issue dated August 29, 2015, and the song was revealed to have debuted and peaked at number 51 on the Hot 100, and number 10 on the Digital Songs chart with 67,000 copies.<ref name="Billboard.com">{{cite web|url=https://www.billboard.com/pro/omi-cheerleader-no-1-return-hot-100-lana-del-rey-debut/|title=OMI's 'Cheerleader' Back to No. 1 on Hot 100|publisher=Billboard.com}}</ref> The song dropped 14 places to number 65 in its second week, and fell to number 97 by its third week.

"High by the Beach" was also a minor success in the United Kingdom, peaking at number 60 on the [[UK Singles Chart]].

==Music video==
On August 9, 2015, Del Rey posted a promotional image from the accompanying [[Jake Nava]]-directed [[music video]] for "High by the Beach" on Instagram.<ref name="Pigeons & Planes"/><ref name="Digital Spy"/> On August 10, Del Rey shared a short clip of the music video on Instagram.<ref name="OCC"/> On August 12, Del Rey shared a 15-second video preview on Instagram and announced that the full music video will be released on August 13.<ref name="Billboard 3">{{cite magazine|author=Frere, Jackie|url=https://www.billboard.com/music/pop/lana-del-rey-high-by-the-beach-video-tease-6663163/|title=Lana Del Rey Teases 'High by the Beach' Video, Out Thursday |magazine=Billboard|date=August 12, 2015|access-date=August 13, 2015}}</ref><ref name="Fuse 2">{{cite web|author=Sherman, Maria|url=http://www.fuse.tv/videos/2015/08/lana-del-rey-high-by-the-beach-video|title=Lana Del Rey Holds A Gun in 'High By The Beach' Video Tease|publisher=Fuse|date=August 12, 2015|access-date=August 13, 2015}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|author=O'Mance, Brad|url=http://www.popjustice.com/thenews/lana-del-reys-high-by-the-beach-video-will-be-online-today-at-some-point/137795/|title=Lana Del Rey's 'High By The Beach' video will be online today at some point|publisher=[[Popjustice]]|date=August 13, 2015|access-date=August 13, 2015}}</ref>

===Synopsis===
The video is primarily shot in a [[hand-held camera]] technique with very little editing or [[Cut (transition)|cuts]]. The video begins with Del Rey wearing a low-cut white sundress over a black bra and a sea glass blue robe entering a beach house as a paparazzi helicopter hovers above her. After entering the house, the camera follows Del Rey from behind as she walks upstairs, posing in front of a mirror and collapsing on her bed, rolling around on it. The camera continues to follow her downstairs where she begins flipping through the pages of a [[Tabloid (newspaper format)|tabloid]]. As the camera moves in front of her, the helicopter reappears closer to the house, hovering in front of the windows. A photographer takes photos of Del Rey and he and the rest of the paparazzi surround her with the helicopter's wind. Del Rey then runs outside onto the beach and pulls out a guitar case from beneath a pile of rocks. When she returns to the balcony, she opens the case and removes a large rocket launcher from it. She turns towards the helicopter and destroys it with the weapon, killing the paparazzi. In the aftermath of the explosion, flaming pieces of tabloid articles flutter through the air as Del Rey lowers the rocket launcher and re-enters the beach house.
