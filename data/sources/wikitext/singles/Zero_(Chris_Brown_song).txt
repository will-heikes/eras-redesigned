"'''Zero'''" is a song by American singer [[Chris Brown]] from his seventh studio album ''[[Royalty (Chris Brown album)|Royalty]]''. It was released as a single on September 18, 2015, by [[RCA Records]].<ref name="iTunes">{{cite web|url=https://music.apple.com/nz/album/zero-single/1040560422|title=Zero - Single by Chris Brown|date=18 September 2015 |publisher=[[iTunes Store]] (US)|access-date=September 25, 2015}}</ref>

The song received positive reviews from music critics, who noted it as one of the album's highlights and celebrated its production, praising his 1980s-inspired sound, including the use of [[talk box]]. The song peaked at number 80 on the [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]].

==Composition and lyrics==
"Zero" is a [[Disco music|disco]]-[[funk]] song.<ref name="allmusic.com">{{Cite web|url=https://www.allmusic.com/album/royalty-mw0002889076|title=Royalty - Chris Brown &#124; Songs, Reviews, Credits |website=[[AllMusic]]|access-date=9 January 2022}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.fuse.tv/2015/09/chris-brown-zero|title=Fuse|website=Fuse.tv|access-date=9 January 2022}}</ref><ref name="slantmagazine.com">{{Cite web|url=https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/chris-brown-royalty/|title=Review: Chris Brown, Royalty|first=Jesse|last=Cataldo|website=Slantmagazine.com|date=21 December 2015|access-date=9 January 2022}}</ref><ref name="chartsinfrance.net">{{Cite web|url=http://www.chartsinfrance.net/Chris-Brown/news-98963.html|title=Clip de "Zero" : Chris Brown danse pour panser ses blessures|website=Chartsinfrance.net|access-date=9 January 2022}}</ref> Critics compared it to the work of American band [[Chic (band)|Chic]] and French [[electronic music]] duo [[Daft Punk]].<ref name="slantmagazine.com"/><ref name="chartsinfrance.net"/><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.vibe.com/music/music-news/chris-brown-zero-374244/|title=Chris Brown Isn't Fazed Over An Ex On "Zero"|first=Christine|last=Imarenezor|website=Vice.com|date=17 September 2015|access-date=9 January 2022}}{{Dead link|date=August 2025 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref> Funk elements are evident in the bass line and the electric guitar rhythm in the chorus.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.melty.fr/chris-brown-zero-son-nouveau-son-a452936.html|title=Découvrez Zero, le nouveau single de Chris Brown|first=Laurine|last=Guilhen|date=18 September 2015|website=Melty.fr|access-date=9 January 2022}}</ref> The track also features a robotic [[vocoder]]ed voice reminiscent of Daft Punk. Lyrically, Brown sings about not caring over a breakup, stating, "Ask how many nights I've been thinking of you, zero".<ref name="billboard.com">{{Cite web|url=https://www.billboard.com/music/rb-hip-hop/chris-brown-zero-artwork-city-hunter-6746332/|title=Chris Brown's 'Zero' Artwork Draws Comparisons to Popular Manga Series|first=Adelle|last=Platon|website=Billboard.com|date=2 November 2015|access-date=9 January 2022}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.mtv.com/news/2859940/chris-brown-justin-bieber-royalty-music-videos/|archive-url=https://archive.today/20160531115851/http://www.mtv.com/news/2859940/chris-brown-justin-bieber-royalty-music-videos/|url-status=dead|archive-date=May 31, 2016|title=Chris Brown, Justin Bieber, and the Myth of Music Video Redemption|first=Molly|last=Beauchemin|website=MTV News|access-date=9 January 2022}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |url=http://hollywoodlife.com/2015/09/18/chris-brown-zero-new-song-royalty-album-listen/ |title=&#91;LISTEN&#93; Chris Brown's 'Zero' — Listen to His New Song from 'Royalty' - Hollywood Life |access-date=2016-04-23 |archive-date=2016-05-06 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160506161357/http://hollywoodlife.com/2015/09/18/chris-brown-zero-new-song-royalty-album-listen/ |url-status=dead }}</ref> Some critics speculated that the lyrics were dedicated to Brown's ex-girlfriend [[Karrueche Tran]].<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.inquisitr.com/2595888/chris-brown-dissing-karrueche-tran-zero-singer-says-he-doesnt-want-a-public-relationship/|title=Chris Brown Dissing Karrueche Tran? 'Zero' Singer Says He Doesn't Want A 'Public Relationship' |website=Inquisitr.com|date=28 November 2015 |access-date=9 January 2022}}</ref>

==Cover artwork==
The cover artwork is similar to artwork used in the 1985 manga ''[[City Hunter]]''. The layout is practically identical with only minor variations such as skin tone.<ref name="billboard.com"/><ref>{{cite web|url=http://kotaku.com/chris-browns-zero-artwork-sure-looks-familiar-1739996041 |title=Chris Brown's 'Zero' Artwork Sure Looks Familiar |date=2 November 2015 |publisher=Kotaku |access-date= December 17, 2015}}</ref> RCA does not give attribution for the song's artwork.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.rcarecordspress.com/artist/chris-brown#cover_art |title=Chris Brown - Official RCA Website |publisher=RCA Records |access-date= December 17, 2015}}</ref>

==Critical reception==
"Zero" received positive reviews from music critics. [[AllMusic]] editor Andy Kellman called the song a "disco-funk throwback replete with talkbox", noting some irony in writing, recording, and releasing a song bragging about not thinking of an ex.<ref name="allmusic.com"/> Marcus Dowling of ''[[HipHopDX]]'' said that the song and "No Filter", another song from ''[[Royalty (Chris Brown album)|Royalty]]'', "pack in all of the aforementioned Rodgers' chugging grooves from [[Chic (band)|Chic]]'s '[[Good Times (Chic song)|Good Times]]', [[David Bowie]]'s '[[Let's Dance (David Bowie song)|Let's Dance]]', and [[Daft Punk]]'s '[[Get Lucky (Daft Punk song)|Get Lucky]]' into one production".<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://hiphopdx.com/reviews/id.2595/title.chris-brown-royalty|title=Chris Brown – Royalty|date=18 December 2015|website=HipHopDX.com|access-date=9 January 2022}}</ref> Mike Pizzo of [[Las Vegas Weekly]] praised the sound but was less impressed with the lyrical content: "it's hard not to nod your head to the cheesy '80s grooves of '[[Fine by Me (Chris Brown song)|Fine by Me]]' or 'Zero', even if the message is endlessly eye rolling."<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://lasvegasweekly.com/ae/music/2015/dec/16/album-review-chris-brown-royalty/|title=Album review: Chris Brown's 'Royalty' - Las Vegas Weekly|date=16 December 2015|website=Lasvegasweekly.com|access-date=9 January 2022|archive-date=19 October 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231019153230/https://lasvegasweekly.com/ae/music/2015/dec/16/album-review-chris-brown-royalty/|url-status=dead}}</ref> Jesse Cataldo of ''[[Slant Magazine]]'' praised the song saying that "'Zero' similarly has a pretty good neo-disco aesthetic, with warm synth washes and some genial Vocoder noodling", comparing the track's musicality to Daft Punk's ''[[Random Access Memories]]''".<ref name="slantmagazine.com"/>

===Synopsis===
The [[music video]] for "Liquor/Zero" was directed by Brown.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.vice.com/en/article/a-film-critic-reviews-chris-browns-music-videos-from-royalty/|title=A Film Critic Reviews Chris Brown's Music Videos from 'Royalty'|website=Vice.com|date=23 December 2015 |access-date=9 January 2022}}</ref> In the “Zero” part of the video, Brown returns home after a long night and finds a girl he’s been seeing throwing his clothes off a balcony because she did not know where he was. Rather than get into an argument, Brown decides to gather his boys and go out, and after he dance starting in an alley and moves his way into a laundromat with his crew before taking the stage at downtown theater. At the end of the video there is a [[cameo appearance|cameo]] by [[Dan Bilzerian]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://emptylighthouse.com/dan-bilzerian-chris-brown-same-video-browns-liquorzero-2137686137|title=Dan Bilzerian & Chris Brown in the same video for Brown's "Liquor/Zero"|date=September 22, 2015}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://hollywoodlife.com/2015/09/22/chris-brown-music-video-liquor-zero-watch/|title=Chris Brown Shows Off Hot Dance Moves In Double Music Video 'Liquor/Zero' — Watch|first=Chloe|last=Melas|website=Hollywoodlife.com|date=22 September 2015|access-date=9 January 2022}}</ref>
