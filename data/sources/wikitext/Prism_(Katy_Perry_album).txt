'''''Prism'''''<!-- Do not add capitals as the stylization. This does not appear universally on print media and is linked to the font on the cover --> is the fourth studio album by American singer [[Katy Perry]].<!--Fourth is correct; first was Katy Hudson, second was One of the Boys, and third was Teenage Dream--> It was released by [[Capitol Records]] on October 18, 2013. Although the album was initially planned to be "darker" than her previous material, ''Prism'' ultimately became a prominently dance-inspired record. Perry worked with several past collaborators, while enlisting new producers and guest vocals. Perry partially handled production with [[Dr. Luke]], [[Max Martin]], [[Stargate (music producers)|Stargate]], [[Benny Blanco]], [[Greg Wells]], [[Cirkut]], and others. The album includes a guest appearance from [[Juicy J]] of [[Three 6 Mafia]]. Much of ''Prism'' revolves around the themes of living in the present, relationships, and [[self-empowerment]]. The album received generally positive reviews; critics praised its lyrics for being more "mature" and personal, while others considered ''Prism'' more formulaic than her previous work.

''Prism'' charted at number one in Australia, Canada, Ireland, New Zealand, Scotland, the United Kingdom, and the United States. It became Perry's fastest-selling album in the US, and the second-best-selling album in Australia in 2013. The [[International Federation of the Phonographic Industry]] (IFPI) reported that ''Prism'' was the sixth best-selling album in the world in 2013, the best-selling album in the world in 2013 released by a woman, and labeled Perry "a global phenomenon." It has sold more than eight million copies worldwide and received multi-Platinum certifications in Australia, Austria, Canada, Denmark, Finland, France, India, Indonesia, Mexico, New Zealand, Norway, the Philippines, Poland, and the US. ''Prism'' was also nominated for [[Grammy Award for Best Pop Vocal Album|Best Pop Vocal Album]] at the [[57th Annual Grammy Awards]].

"[[Roar (song)|Roar]]" was released on August 10, 2013, as the album's lead single and topped the US [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]]. A second single, "[[Unconditionally]]", debuted on October 16, 2013, and reached the top 10 in Italy. "[[Dark Horse (Katy Perry song)|Dark Horse]]" followed as the album's third single three months later on December 17, 2013, and reached number one in the United States. "[[Birthday (Katy Perry song)|Birthday]]" and "[[This Is How We Do]]" were the fourth and fifth singles, respectively. The track "[[Walking on Air (Katy Perry song)|Walking on Air]]" was also released as a [[promotional single]]. ''Prism'' was promoted further through the [[Prismatic World Tour]]. A concert from the tour was eventually released as ''[[The Prismatic World Tour Live]]'' in 2015.

== Recording ==
Development of ''Prism'' began while Perry was on the [[California Dreams Tour]]. She began with a process she deemed "slow cooking", in which she recorded random "fragments of ideas" into her mobile phone's [[dictaphone]] application. A member of Direct Management Group, Ngoc Hoang, then transcribed the audio files and placed them into what Perry described as a "treasure chest"; Perry consulted this material later during the album's recording sessions. Although Perry officially started recording the album in November 2012, accompanied by [[Greg Wells]] and [[Greg Kurstin]], she noted that she was still in a "dark place", and that she had not "let the light in". The sessions began again in March 2013, following a trip to Madagascar which Perry credited as having "put [her] priorities in perspective", leading her to "do more work on [her]self". Perry also viewed a video made by [[Eckhart Tolle]], which discusses loss. She commented: "When you lose something, all your foundations crumble—but that also leaves a big hole that's open for something great to come through."<ref name=BillboardCoverStory/>

Once she felt prepared to record again, she reunited with her team from ''Teenage Dream''—[[Dr. Luke]], [[Bonnie McKee]], and [[Cirkut (record producer)|Cirkut]]—in Perry's hometown of [[Santa Barbara, California]], where they spent a month recording at Playback Recording Studio. She then went to [[Stockholm]], where she worked with Scandinavian record producer [[Max Martin]] to do what Perry called "put[ting] the ice on the cake". She also recruited other collaborators, such as Norwegian team [[Stargate (music producers)|Stargate]], Bloodshy, [[Benny Blanco]], [[Jonatha Brooke]] and [[Sia]].<ref name=BillboardCoverStory/> By April 2013, recording for the album was halfway complete, and Perry described to the [[American Society of Composers, Authors and Publishers|ASCAP]] how she worked with these collaborators. She said Wells allowed her to "vomit words"; with Martin, she shaped the songs' melodies; Luke mostly helmed the production, and she worked with "topline and melody". Perry described writing sessions with McKee as "emotional abuse", adding that they argue over the "best lyric", as if they were fighting in a boxing ring.<ref name="doublerainbow">{{cite magazine|url=http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/1558827/katy-perry-is-halfway-there-on-third-album-talks-new-songs-like-double-rainbow |title=Katy Perry Is Halfway There on Third Album, Talks New Songs Like 'Double Rainbow' |magazine=Billboard |access-date=March 15, 2014 |date=April 18, 2013 |first=Gallo |last=Phil |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140327021904/http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/1558827/katy-perry-is-halfway-there-on-third-album-talks-new-songs-like-double-rainbow |archive-date=March 27, 2014 }}</ref> McKee, who wrote four songs for ''Prism'', spoke with [[MTV]] about the album, describing it as "a little bit more grown up" and "interesting".<ref>{{cite web|title=Katy Perry's 'Soulmate' Gives Details On 'More Mature' Album |last=Vena |first=Jocelyn |url=http://www.mtv.com/news/articles/1709867/katy-perry-bonnie-mckee-new-album.jhtml |date=July 2, 2013 |publisher=MTV News. On April 3, 2014, "[[Birthday (Katy Perry song)|Birthday]]" was announced as the album's fourth official single. It debuted at number 91 on the ''Billboard'' Hot 100<ref name="BillboardDebut">{{cite magazine|url=http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/6055524/pharrell-williams-tops-hot-100-ed-sheeran-blasts-in-at-no-15 |title=Pharrell Williams Tops Hot 100; Ed Sheeran Blasts In at No. 15 |access-date=April 16, 2014 |date=April 16, 2014 |first=Gary |last=Trust |magazine=Billboard |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140418201239/http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/6055524/pharrell-williams-tops-hot-100-ed-sheeran-blasts-in-at-no-15 |archive-date=April 18, 2014 }}</ref> and was sent to [[Mainstream Top 40|mainstream]] radio in the U.S. on April 21, 2014.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.fmqb.com/Article.asp?id=16691 |title=FMQB: Radio Industry News, Music Industry Updates, Nielsen Ratings, Music News and more! |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140628011329/http://www.fmqb.com/article.asp?id=16691 |archive-date=June 28, 2014 }}</ref><ref>{{cite magazine|url=http://www.billboard.com/biz/articles/news/chart-alert/6042266/pharrell-williams-leads-hot-100-john-legend-gains-at-no-2 |title=Pharrell Williams Leads Hot 100, John Legend Gains At No. 2 |magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]] |date=April 9, 2014 |access-date=April 10, 2014 |author=Trust, Gary |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140411230757/http://www.billboard.com/biz/articles/news/chart-alert/6042266/pharrell-williams-leads-hot-100-john-legend-gains-at-no-2 |archive-date=April 11, 2014 }}</ref> The single peaked at number 17 in the US, becoming her 15th song to reach the top 20 in the nation.<ref name="charth"/>

"[[This Is How We Do]]" was released as the fifth official single from the album. Upon its release, ''Prism'' received generally favorable reviews from music critics. On [[Metacritic]], it has a score of 61 out of 100, based on 26 reviews.<ref name="Metacritic">{{cite web|url=http://www.metacritic.com/music/prism/katy-perry |title=Prism |publisher=[[Metacritic]] |access-date=November 15, 2013 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131209172300/http://www.metacritic.com/music/prism/katy-perry |archive-date=December 9, 2013 }}</ref> Jon Dolan from ''[[Rolling Stone]]'' awarded the album three out of five stars, writing that "Perry and her longtime collaborators [[Dr. Luke]] and [[Max Martin]] often go for a darker, moodier intimacy à la high-end Swedish divas [[Robyn]] and [[Lykke Li]]. Perry has always done a great job of letting us know she's in on the joke of pop stardom. Sadly, she doesn't always bring that same sense of humor and self-awareness to the joke of pop-star introspection. The album's raft of ripe-lotus ballads is larded with [[Alanis Morissette|Alanis]]-ian poesy she can't pull off".<ref name="RollingStoneReview">{{cite magazine|last=Dolan |first=Jon |title=Katy Perry 'Prism' Review |url=https://www.rollingstone.com/music/albumreviews/prism-20131016 |magazine=[[Rolling Stone]] |date=October 17, 2013 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160409165951/http://www.rollingstone.com/music/albumreviews/prism-20131016 |archive-date=April 9, 2016 }}</ref> Giving the album a B+ rating, Nick Catucci from ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'' stated that "Katy's superpower, now more than ever, is minting songs so relatable that their insights quickly scale up to inspirational." He also added that "now she grasps that she's making the mainstream, not just swimming in it".<ref name="EWREVIEW">{{cite magazine|last=Catucci |first=Nick |title=Prism review |url=https://ew.com/article/2014/01/08/prism-review/ |magazine=Entertainment Weekly |date=October 17, 2013 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141018084603/http://www.ew.com/ew/article/0%2C%2C20746141%2C00.html |archive-date=October 18, 2014 }}</ref>

Helen Brown from ''[[The Daily Telegraph]]'' gave the album five out of five stars, stating Perry "sounds like a woman, and an artist, who's finally found herself" and praised the "vulnerability" of the album.<ref name="TelegraphReview">{{cite news|last=Brown |first=Helen |title=Katy Perry, Prism, review 17, 2013 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140126035023/http://www.telegraph.co.uk/culture/music/rockandpopreviews/10386191/Katy-Perry-Prism-review.html |archive-date=January 26, 2014 }}</ref> [[Stephen Thomas Erlewine]] from [[AllMusic]], Alexis Petridis from ''[[The Guardian]]'', and Sal Cinquemani from ''[[Slant Magazine]]'' all also gave the album three out of five stars.<ref name=slant>{{cite web|last=Cinquemani |first=Sal |url=http://www.slantmagazine.com/music/review/katy-perry-prism |title=Katy Perry: Prism |website=Slant Magazine |date=October 18, 2013 ''Prism'' sold 453,000 copies worldwide in its first week of release.<ref>{{cite web|title=El cuarto álbum de Katy Perry vendió 2.8 millones de discos en su año de estreno|url=https://www.ciudad.com.ar/virales/cuarto-album-katy-perry-vendio-28-millones-discos-ano-estreno_106094/|access-date=May 24, 2026|date=September 6, 2018|publisher=[[Ciudad Magazine]]|language=es|trans-title=Katy Perry's fourth album sold 2.8 million records in its release year|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260523201553/https://www.ciudad.com.ar/virales/cuarto-album-katy-perry-vendio-28-millones-discos-ano-estreno_106094/|archive-date=May 23, 2026|url-status=live}}</ref> The [[International Federation of the Phonographic Industry]] (IFPI) reported that ''Prism'' was the best-selling album released by a woman in 2013 and ranked as the sixth best-selling album overall that year. The IFPI called Perry "a global phenomenon."<ref name=ifpi14/> The record was also the 26th best-selling album worldwide in 2014.<ref name=ifpi15/> ''Prism'' has sold more than eight million copies worldwide as of October 2015.<ref name=wwsales>{{cite news|title=La californiana Katy Perry regresa a la Argentina para presentar su disco ''Prism'' en el hipódromo de Palermo|url=http://noticias.terra.com.ar/argentina/la-californiana-katy-perry-regresa-a-la-argentina-para-presentar-su-disco-prism-en-el-hipodromo-de-palermo,8fb6fda031562ae84034a97d233c9113pch06jkj.html|access-date=July 28, 2025|date=October 2, 2015|agency=[[Terra (company)|Terra]]|language=es|trans-title=Californian Katy Perry returns to Argentina to present her album ''Prism'' at the Palermo racetrack|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151004175225/http://noticias.terra.com.ar/argentina/la-californiana-katy-perry-regresa-a-la-argentina-para-presentar-su-disco-prism-en-el-hipodromo-de-palermo,8fb6fda031562ae84034a97d233c9113pch06jkj.html|archive-date=October 4, 2015|url-status=dead}}</ref>

=== The Americas ===
In the United States, ''Prism'' debuted at number one on the [[Billboard 200|''Billboard'' 200]], becoming her second consecutive number one album in the country. The album sold 286,000 copies in its first week. ''Prism'' earned the largest first-week sales total by a female artist for 2013, surpassing [[Miley Cyrus]]' [[Bangerz]] (270,000 copies). However, this mark was surpassed by [[Beyoncé]]'s ''[[Beyoncé (album)|self-titled album]]'' (617,213 digital copies), released in December 2013.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/articles/news/5840086/its-official-beyonce-makes-history-with-fifth-no-1-album|title=It's Official: Beyoncé Makes History with Fifth No. 1 Album|date=December 17, 2013|last=Caulfield|first=Keith|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|access-date=October 20, 2025|url-access=subscription|archive-date=April 2, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140402041030/http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/5840086/its-official-beyonce-makes-history-with-fifth-no-1-album|url-status=live}}</ref> At the time of its release, ''Prism'' had the fourth-highest first-week sales of the year for a pop record, trailing behind [[Daft Punk]]'s May 2013 album ''[[Random Access Memories]]'', which sold 339,000 copies, and [[Justin Timberlake]]'s ''[[The 20/20 Experience]]'', which debuted to 968,000 copies and its follow-up, ''[[The 20/20 Experience – 2 of 2]]'', which debuted to just 350,000 copies. The first week's sales also marked Perry's highest weekly sales, with her previous album ''Teenage Dream'' selling 192,000 copies in its first week in August 2010. ''Prism'' has the highest first-week sales for a female pop artist since [[Madonna]]'s twelfth studio album, ''[[MDNA (album)|MDNA]]''.<ref>{{cite magazine|last=Caulfield|first=Keith|title=Katy Perry's ''Prism'' Shines at No. 1 on ''Billboard'' 200 |url=http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/5770710/katy-perrys-prism-shines-at-no-1-on-billboard-200|url-access=subscription|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|date=October 30, 2013|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131101190513/http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/5770710/katy-perrys-prism-shines-at-no-1-on-billboard-200|archive-date=November 1, 2013}}</ref> The album sold 92,000 copies during its second week of availability, falling to number two on the ''Billboard'' 200. This represented the smallest sales dip for a number one album in the US since the August 2013 release of ''[[The Civil Wars (album)|The Civil Wars]]''.<ref>{{cite magazine|last=Caulfield|first=Keith|title=Arcade Fire's ''Reflektor'' Debuts at No. 1 on ''Billboard''
