"'''Teenage Dream'''" is a song by American singer [[Katy Perry]]. It was released as the second single from her third studio album [[Teenage Dream|of the same name]] on July 23, 2010. She and [[Bonnie McKee]] co-wrote the track with its producers [[Dr. Luke]], [[Max Martin]], and [[Benny Blanco]]. The track was recorded at [[Conway Recording Studios]], based in [[Hollywood, Los Angeles]], and Playback Recording Studios, based in [[Santa Barbara, California]]. McKee describes "Teenage Dream" as a throwback song to the euphoric feelings of being in love as a [[Adolescence|teenager]] while Perry described the song as reminiscent of her youth. Musically, "Teenage Dream" is a mid-tempo pop song with a [[retro style|retro]] sound. It is styled in the genres of [[power pop]] and [[electropop]], while taking influence from other genres such as [[disco]] and [[pop rock]]. Perry starts the song in a high-pitched voice while her vocals grow stronger as the song progresses. Lyrically, "Teenage Dream" discusses being with a lover who makes one feel young again.

Critics were initially divided about the song at release. However, the song has received retrospective critical acclaim with [[Glamour (magazine)|''Glamour'']] calling it "one of the best pop songs of all time." The song topped the US [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]], becoming Perry's third number-one single on that chart, and her second consecutive number-one single after "[[California Gurls]]". It also reached number one in Ireland, New Zealand, and Slovakia. "Teenage Dream" entered the top ten in 13 countries in total. "Teenage Dream" has been certified Diamond in the United States, Australia, and Canada, and Gold or Platinum in ten additional countries. The song has sold 12 million copies worldwide.

A music video for the song was filmed in various locations around Perry's hometown in [[Santa Barbara, California]] and was directed by [[Yoann Lemoine]] (a.k.a. Woodkid). The video showcases Perry being in love with her high school boyfriend. Perry has performed the song on ''[[Saturday Night Live]]'', the [[2010 Teen Choice Awards]], [[53rd Annual Grammy Awards]], the [[Super Bowl XLIX halftime show]], and in every one of her [[concert tour]]s since the [[California Dreams Tour]]. "Teenage Dream" was nominated for the [[Grammy Award for Best Female Pop Vocal Performance]], becoming Perry's third nomination in the category. The song has been covered numerous times, appearing on ''[[American Idol]]'', ''[[The Voice (American TV series)|The Voice]]'', and ''[[Glee (TV series)|Glee]]''. ''Billboard'' ranked "Teenage Dream" among the "500 Best Pop Songs of All Time" in 2023 at number 38, and ''[[Rolling Stone]]'' ranked among "The 250 Greatest Songs of the 21st Century So Far" in 2025 at number 55.<ref name="billboard500bestpopsongs">{{cite magazine |date=November 25, 2024 |title=The 500 Best Pop Songs: Staff List |url=https://www.billboard.com/lists/best-pop-songs-all-time-hits/38-katy-perry-teenage-dream/ |access-date=November 25, 2024 |magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]}}</ref><ref name="rollingstone250greatestsongs">{{cite magazine |date=October 8, 2025 |title=The 250 Greatest Songs of the 21st Century So Far|url=https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-lists/best-songs-of-the-21st-century-1235410452/frank-ocean-novacane-2-1235433366/ |access-date=October 8, 2025 |magazine=[[Rolling Stone]]}}</ref>

==Background and writing==
[[File:BonnieMcKeePerforming.png|thumb|upright|[[Bonnie McKee]] (''pictured'') co-wrote "Teenage Dream".]]
When Perry and McKee first met in 2004, they both were "really into ''[[Lolita]]''" and "had a mutual fascination with the [[adolescence|adolescent]] state of mind." They explored the themes in their first records: Perry wrote ''[[One of the Boys]]'', which touched on the early stages of discovering that boys could be more than friends, whereas McKee wrote "Confessions of a Teenage Girl", which was about using sexual power to one's advantage. For the ''Teenage Dream'' sessions, Perry and McKee had a "forever young" idea in mind. Perry at first wrote a lyric about [[Peter Pan (character)|Peter Pan]], but they later deemed it as "too young" since "we wanted it to have more edge, more sex." Another version included the lines "And the next thing you know / You're a mom in a minivan", which made them laugh uncontrollably for an hour. There was a final version that was based around the metaphor of "[[wiktionary:try on|trying me on]]" comparing clothes to sex, in a similar manner to [[Madonna]]'s 1985 single "[[Dress You Up]]". It was rejected by the producers, as McKee explained, "Luke always makes us 'Benny Proof' everything. He says that if Benny doesn't get it, America won't get it." Blanco showed them [[The Teenagers (French band)|the Teenagers]]'s 2007 single "Homecoming" and asked them to write in a similar style. McKee said that "[We] looked at each other with dread, knowing we had to start all over again..... We were both so over it we just called it a night."<ref name=mckee />

[[File:Leonardo DiCaprio.jpeg|thumb|upright|[[Leonardo DiCaprio]] was one of the inspirations for  "Teenage Dream".]]
McKee continued working on the song the following days. She explained, "I thought about my own adolescent years, my own first love. I thought about watching [[Baz Luhrmann]]'s '[[Romeo + Juliet|Romeo and Juliet]]' and putting on a little mini [[disco ball]] light and just dreaming of [[Leonardo DiCaprio|Leo]]. I thought about me and my friends sitting around at slumber parties in the '90s, giddy even just THINKING about boys..... I thought about what Benny said and I listened to the song again, and I was like the Teenagers..... that's such a great word, Teenager. It is a very descriptive word; it packs a lot of emotion and imagery into three syllables..... I couldn't believe after all of our agonizing over 'youth' themes, that we had overlooked such an obvious one – the teenage condition." The following week, Perry invited McKee, Luke, and Martin to write in her hometown of [[Santa Barbara, California]]. McKee tried to approach Luke about her idea, but he was upset about the amount of time he had spent working in the chorus, so he banned them from changing it. They started working on the verses, where Perry had already prepared most of the imagery.<ref name=mckee>{{cite web|url=http://alexkazemi.com/2010/07/listensingle-review-teenage-dream-katy-perry/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100807001855/http://alexkazemi.com/2010/07/listensingle-review-teenage-dream-katy-perry/ |url-status=dead |archive-date=August 7, 2010 |title=Interview with Songwriter Bonnie McKee |last=Kazemi |first=Alex |date=July 22, 2010 |publisher=Alex Kazemi |access-date=July 25, 2010 }}</ref>

Perry described the process as "a very pure moment for me" and continued saying, "[It was] where I started my creative juices. And also it kind of exudes this [[Euphoria|euphoric]] feeling because everybody remembers what their teenage dreams were — all the girls that were on your poster walls."<ref name=mtvrw>{{cite web|url=http://www.mtv.com/news/1644311/katy-perry-says-new-single-teenage-dream-exudes-euphoric-feeling/|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150117045912/http://www.mtv.com/news/1644311/katy-perry-says-new-single-teenage-dream-exudes-euphoric-feeling/|url-status=dead|archive-date=January 17, 2015|title=Katy Perry Says New Single 'Teenage Dream' Exudes 'Euphoric' Feeling|last=Montgomery|first=James|date=July 23, 2010|publisher=[[MTV News]]|access-date=May 15, 2022}}</ref> After she recorded the vocals, McKee pulled Luke and Martin aside and told them her idea, to which they responded, "Well why didn't you say that in the first place?". The chorus was rewritten, and the line "Skin tight jeans" was taken from the early "trying me on" version. When the final version was finished, McKee said, "We were all so pumped that it had paid off. I remember Max sitting back and saying 'I wish we could bottle this feeling'. It was really magical."<ref name=mckee /> Perry commented that even though the song was rewritten many times, she was "so happy to finally get somewhere that we all agreed on" after it was finished.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/katy-perry-the-billboard-cover-story-957236/|title=Katy Perry: The Billboard Cover Story|last=Herrera|first=Monica|date=July 23, 2010|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|access-date=May 15, 2022}}</ref> During an interview in June 2010, Perry confirmed "Teenage Dream" as the second single from the album, and said that the song was about "kind of like feeling that way when you were a teenager: really emotional, really invested..... It's intense being in love and being a teenager."<ref name=tca>{{cite web|url=http://www.mtv.com/news/1641588/katy-perry-teases-her-teen-choice-awards-wardrobe/|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150417064837/http://www.mtv.com/news/1641588/katy-perry-teases-her-teen-choice-awards-wardrobe/|url-status=dead|archive-date=April 17, 2015|title=Katy Perry Teases Her Teen Choice Awards Wardrobe|last=Vena|first=Jocelyn|date=June 15, 2010|publisher=MTV News|access-date=May 15, 2022}}</ref> After confirming that "Teenage Dream" was also the title of the album, she explained that it was a throwback to her teenage years, as she thought back to her youth while contemplating marriage to her then-boyfriend [[Russell Brand]]. She added, "To me, this year is pretty heavy. I am going to be getting married and putting out this record, and there is so much going on that it's nice to think of those young dreams."<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.dailyrecord.co.uk/entertainment/celebrity/katy-perry-im-not-thinking-1063233|title=Katy Perry: I'm not thinking about having kids..... my new kitten is enough trouble|last=McMonagle|first=Mickey|date=July 2, 2010|newspaper=[[Daily Record (Scotland)|Daily Record]]|access-date=May 15, 2022}}</ref>

==Composition==
[[File:Katyperry-cdt-intro.jpg|thumb|left|220px|Perry performing "Teenage Dream" at the [[California Dreams Tour]]]]
"Teenage Dream" is a midtempo love song with a strong [[beat (music)|beat]] that follows the [[Retro (music)|retro]] sound of previous single "[[California Gurls]]".<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://ew.com/article/2010/07/22/katy-perryteenage-dream-new-single/|title=Katy Perry's new single 'Teenage Dream' hits the web|last=Greenblatt|first=Leah|date=July 22, 2010|magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]]|access-date=May 15, 2022}}</ref><ref name=mtvmv /> It is predominantly a [[power pop]] and [[electropop]] song,<ref name="50Best">{{cite magazine|url =https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-lists/50-best-songs-of-2010-148374|title =50 Best Songs of 2010: 4. Katy Perry, Teenage Dream|magazine =Rolling Stone|date =December 14, 2010|access-date =May 15, 2022}}</ref><ref name="power">{{cite web |url=http://grantland.com/hollywood-prospectus/just-a-couple-of-normal-people-on-a-stage-lcd-soundsystems-shut-up-and-play-the-hits-and-katy-perrys-part-of-me/ |title=Just A Couple Of Normal People On A Stage: LCD Soundsystem's ''Shut Up and Play the Hits'' and Katy Perry's ''Part of Me'' |first=Molly |last=Lambert |work=[[Grantland.com|Grantland]] |publisher=[[ESPN]] |date=July 27, 2012 |access-date=May 15, 2022}}</ref> which features influences of [[disco]], [[pop rock]], and [[rock music]].<ref name="disco">{{cite web |url=http://www.azcentral.com/thingstodo/music/articles/2011/07/26/20110726katy-perry-best-songs-hot-cold-firework-kissed-girl.html?page=3 |title=Essential Katy Perry songs, from 'Hot n Cold' to 'Firework |first=Ed |last=Masley |work=[[The Arizona Republic]]  |date= July 26, 2011|access-date=August 18, 2012 }}</ref><ref name="sheet" /><ref name="matters">{{cite web |url=https://www.popmatters.com/130887-katy-perry-teenage-dream-2496139770.html|title=Katy Perry: Teenage Dream|first=Steve |last=Leftridge|work=[[Pop Matters]]  |date=October 4, 2010 |access-date=May 15, 2022}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|first= Carrie|last= Ahlgrim|title= The 113 best songs of the past decade, ranked |website= [[Business Insider]] |date= December 13, 2019 |url= https://www.insider.com/best-songs-of-the-decade-2010s-ranked-2019-11|quote=  "Teenage Dream" is the pop song...rather than playing into her sugary caricature, the shimmering rock-tinged single gave Perry a kind of gravitas.|accessdate= December 30, 2022}}</ref> Musicnotes published this song in the [[Key (music)|key]] of [[B-flat major|B{{music|flat}} major]], with a tempo of 120 [[Tempo|beats per minute]]. It starts with the sequence of B{{music|flat}}–B{{music|flat}}<sub>2</sub> played by a guitar.<ref name="sheet" /> These intervals are played on guitar throughout the song, but with the addition of different bass notes from halfway through the first verse, the overall harmony can be heard as E{{music|flat}}<small>maj</small><sub>7</sub> – Gm – F<small>sus</small><sub>4</sub>. The general [[chord progression]] of the song is made from the progression E{{music|flat}} - Gm – F.  Perry's vocals in the song span from the note of B{{music|flat}}<sub>3</sub> to the note of F<sub>5</sub>.<ref name="sheet">{{cite web|url =http://www.musicnotes.com/sheetmusic/mtdVPE.asp?ppn=MN0085950|title =Katy Perry – Teenage Dream Sheet Music (Digital Download)|website =Musicnotes|date =August 17, 2010|publisher =[[Alfred Music Publishing]]|access-date =January 16, 2011}}</ref> The song starts with relatively high vocals, with Perry singing in [[head voice]]; it also continues during the second verse.<ref name=aol>{{cite web|url=http://www.aolradioblog.com/2010/07/22/katy-perry-teenage-dream-song/|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100725073324/http://www.aolradioblog.com/2010/07/22/katy-perry-teenage-dream-song/|url-status=usurped|archive-date=July 25, 2010|title=Katy Perry, 'Teenage Dream' |last=Anderson|first=Sarah D|date=July 22, 2010|publisher=[[AOL Radio]]|access-date=July 23, 2010}}</ref> Amos Barshad of ''[[New York (magazine)|New York]]'' said that "if not for a bluntly muscular chorus ('You! ..... make! ..... me!'), [the song] is nearly a ballad."<ref name=ny /> The lyrics refer to being with a lover that makes one feel like a teenager. According to James Montgomery of [[MTV]], the lyrics refer to being in love, and about the feelings of commitment and security that it brings.<ref name=mtvrw /> They also talk about taking the relationship further in lines such as: "Let's go all the way tonight / No regrets, just love / We can dance until we die / You and I / We'll be young forever".<ref name=aol />

In a 2014 ''[[Slate (magazine)|Slate]]'' article, composer [[Owen Pallett]] analyzed the song using [[music theory]] to explain its success. He focused on two factors: the [[tonality|tonal]] interplay of the chords and [[melody]], and Perry's use of [[syncopation]] in her vocals. "This song is all about suspension—not in the voice-leading 4–3 sense, but in the emotional sense, which listeners often associate with 'exhilaration,' being on the road, being on a roller coaster, travel," he explained.<ref name="Owen Pallett analysis">{{cite news|last=Pallett|first=Owen|author-link=Owen Pallett|title=Skin Tight Jeans and Syncopation|url=https://slate.com/culture/2014/03/katy-perrys-teenage-dream-explaining-the-hit-using-music-theory.html|magazine=[[Slate (magazine)|Slate]]|date=March 25, 2014|access-date=May 15, 2022}}</ref>

That suspension is created "simply, by denying the listener any I chords," Pallett explains. After the first few lines, the B{{music|flat}} [[tonic (music)|tonic]] chord that repeats throughout the song is offset by bass notes that give it a more [[Dominant (music)|dominant]] [[Seventh chord|7th]] feel. "The I chord will never appear again," he observes. At the same time, however, Perry begins singing the melody on that note, and returns to it frequently, even when it clashes with the dominant V chord as she sings "feel like I'm living a". "[T]he vocal melody establishes the key so clearly that there is no doubt ... Her voice is the sun and the song is in orbit around it," he concludes. "The insistence of the tonic in the melody keeps your ears' eyes fixed on the destination, but the song never arrives there. Weightlessness is achieved. Great work, songwriters!" Pallett notes other classic pop songs, such as [[Fleetwood Mac]]'s "[[Dreams (Fleetwood Mac song)|Dreams]]" and [[Coldplay]]'s "[[Viva la Vida]]", that employ the same device.<ref name="Owen Pallett analysis" />

"The second key to this song's Enormous Chart Success," Pallett continues, "has to do with the weighting of the melody lines." Perry begins singing each verse on the beat ("you think I'm pretty ...") but then falls off it before the end of the first line ("... makeup on"), syncopating the melody, and creating additional tension. "Her lines dovetail elegantly into each other. This contributes to the feeling of suspension that I mentioned above. As listeners, we're waiting for her to get to the point." Pallett says she does indeed do this in the [[pre-chorus]] when she accentuates the [[Imperative mood#English|imperatives]] ("Let's go all the way tonight ...") by returning to the beat. Pallett then calls it "genius" that the chorus itself inverts the weighting of the verses, as the line "You make me feel like I'm living a teenage dream" goes from off beat to on and then back again, in a single line.<ref name="Owen Pallett analysis" />

Further, Pallett writes, in the song's [[Bridge (music)|bridge]], Perry adds more complexity to the song—and makes it more memorable, in his opinion—by taking the simple step of singing the "let you put your hands on me in my skintight jeans" lyrics entirely on beat, including the reiteration of the song's title. "How brilliant. The title of the song is rhythmically weighted two ways—it's like a flank attack. Two sides of the same face. You WILL remember the name of this song."<ref name="Owen Pallett analysis" />

==Critical reception==
Critics were divided by "Teenage Dream" at the time of its release. Craig Marks of ''[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]'' said that along with "California Gurls", the songs "are textbook summer singles whose ingenious pop hooks and zillion-dollar drum tracks are stamped with Perry's knack for a memorable line and frothy, gum-snapping persona."<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/katy-perry-summer-album-preview-2010-958026/|title=Katy Perry: Summer Album Preview 2010|last=Marks|first=Craig|date=May 26, 2010|magazine=Billboard|access-date=May 15, 2022}}</ref> ''Billboard'' named "Teenage Dream" as the "second best song of the 2010s so far" in 2015.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/music/pop/20-best-songs-2010s-half-decade-6436552/|title=The 20 Best Songs of the 2010s (So Far)|date=January 12, 2015|magazine=Billboard|access-date=May 15, 2022}}</ref> James Montgomery of [[MTV]] commented that "Teenage Dream" showed a "definite change of pace for Perry. While it's still as saccharine sweet as her previous hits, there's also a rather, well, ''mature'' quality to it (despite the title)."<ref name=mtvrw /> Amos Barshad of ''New York'' said, "Right now, it's feeling a bit too earnest, and not nearly as catchy as the follow-up single from Perry's first album ('[[Hot n Cold]]', most definitely still a jam). That said, we are now mentally preparing ourselves for its ubiquity."<ref name=ny>{{cite web|url=https://www.vulture.com/2010/07/katy_perry_dreamed_a_dream.html|title=Katy Perry Dreamed a Dream|last=Barshad|first=Amos|date=July 22, 2010|work=Vulture|access-date=May 15, 2022}}</ref> Alyssa Rosenberg of ''[[The Atlantic]]'' dismissed the lyrics as "simply annoying and incoherent" and added that "the hook and chorus aren't nearly compelling enough to pull me back into it."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.theatlantic.com/entertainment/archive/2010/07/katy-perrys-teenage-dream-isnt-mine/60342/|title=Katy Perry's Teenage Dream Isn't Mine|last=Rosenberg|first=Alyssa|date=July 29, 2010|work=[[The Atlantic]]|access-date=May 15, 2022}}</ref>

There's something holy about it".<ref>{{cite news|last=Lakshmin|first=Deepa|date=April 12, 2017|title=Lorde Explains Why Katy Perry's 'Teenage Dream' Is 'Holy'|url=http://www.mtv.com/news/3002219/lorde-katy-perry-teenage-dream-pop-music/|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170413144533/http://www.mtv.com/news/3002219/lorde-katy-perry-teenage-dream-pop-music/|url-status=dead|archive-date=April 13, 2017|access-date=May 11, 2021|agency=MTV News}}</ref> Country musician [[Maren Morris]] said she grew up listening to "Teenage Dream" and called the song "one of the greatest songs of all time".<ref>{{cite magazine|last=Hiatt|first=Brian|date=July 29, 2019|title=Maren Morris on the Highwomen, the Dixie Chicks and Her 'Playboy' Controversy|url=https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/maren-morris-highwomen-playboy-controversy-dixie-chicks-862114/|access-date=May 11, 2021|magazine=Rolling Stone}}</ref>

''Billboard'' ranked "Teenage Dream" among the "500 Best Pop Songs of All Time" in 2023 at number 38.<ref name="billboard500bestpopsongs"/>

==Commercial performance==
[[File:Katy Perry at The Palace of Auburn Hills 01.jpg|thumb|Perry performing "Teenage Dream" at the [[Prismatic World Tour]]]]
"Teenage Dream" debuted at number 20 on the US [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]] dated August 7, 2010, becoming the week's Hot Shot debut. It also debuted on the [[Hot Digital Songs]] chart at number 11 with 84,000 downloads and on the [[Hot 100 Airplay (Radio Songs)|Radio Songs]] chart at number 75. According to [[Nielsen Broadcast Data Systems]], "Teenage Dream" set the record for most added song in a week with 64 new stations and 14.9 million plays.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/eminem-and-rihanna-stand-strong-on-hot-100-957151/|title=Eminem and Rihanna Stand Strong on Hot 100|last=Pietroluongo|first=Silvio|date=July 29, 2010|magazine=Billboard|access-date=May 15, 2022}}</ref> The following week, it jumped to number seven on the Hot 100, due to a radio audience of 29.5 million impressions and an increase of 183,000 downloads. On the issue dated September 18, 2010, the song topped the chart becoming her second consecutive number one-single and her third overall number one-single.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/eminem-and-rihanna-lie-still-atop-hot-100-957022/|title=Eminem and Rihanna Lie 'Still' Atop Hot 100|last=Pietroluongo|first=Silvio|date=August 5, 2010|magazine=Billboard|access-date=May 15, 2022}}</ref> As of July 2024, the song has been certified Diamond by the [[Recording Industry Association of America]] (RIAA)<ref name=RIAA/> and sold 10 million copies only in the United States, becoming her sixth song reach this status.<ref name="USsales">{{cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/pro/katy-perry-career-bests-american-idol/|title=Ask Billboard: Katy Perry's Career Bests, From 'One of the Boys' to 'Smile,' & Every Hot 100 'Idol' Top 10|last=Trust|first=Gary|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|date=August 28, 2020|access-date=May 15, 2022}}</ref>

The same week the song topped the charts in the US, it debuted at number 20 in the [[Canadian Hot 100]].<ref>{{cite magazine|url=http://acharts.us/canada_singles_top_100/2010/31|title=Canada Singles Top 100 – Music Charts|date=August 7, 2010|magazine=Billboard|access-date=July 30, 2010}}</ref> On the September 2 issue of the [[Canadian Singles Chart|Canadian Digital Download Chart]], "Teenage Dream" rose to number one, making it her third number one on that tally.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://jam.canoe.com/Music/Charts/DIGITAL.html |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160205175952/http://jam.canoe.com/Music/Charts/DIGITAL.html |url-status=dead |archive-date=February 5, 2016 |title=2 September Download Chart |date=September 2, 2010 |publisher=Jam |access-date=September 4, 2010 }}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://en-ca.nielsen.com/content/dam/nielsen/en_ca/documents/pdf/newsletters/billboard_canadian-update/Nielsen%20Music%20Canadian%20Update.pdf |title=Nielsen Canadian Update |date=September 1, 2010 |access-date=September 4, 2010 |url-status=unfit |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110409071903/http://en-ca.nielsen.com/content/dam/nielsen/en_ca/documents/pdf/newsletters/billboard_canadian-update/Nielsen%20Music%20Canadian%20Update.pdf |archive-date=April 9, 2011 }}</ref> It went to number one in New Zealand on the August 30 chart making it her third number one in New Zealand that year, preceded by "[[If We Ever Meet Again]]" with Timbaland in early 2010 and "California Gurls". Overall the song remained at the top for four weeks in New Zealand. It was her fourth number one overall achieving this with "[[I Kissed a Girl]]" in 2008. On September 5, 2010, "Teenage Dream" debuted on the [[UK Singles Chart]] at number two selling over 86,000 units in its debut week. It was kept off the top spot by the release of [[Olly Murs]]' debut single "[[Please Don't Let Me Go]]". The following week it stayed at number two behind [[Alexandra Burke]]'s "[[Start Without You]]", before moving down to number three the following week. The song reached number one on the Scottish Singles Chart.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.officialcharts.com/charts/scottish-singles-chart/20100905/41/ |title=Scottish Singles September 5, 2010 |publisher=[[Official Charts Company]] |date=September 11, 2010 |access-date=December 2, 2010}}</ref>

Internationally, the song met a well commercial success, topping in Slovakia and reaching the top five in Austria, Croatia, Bulgaria and Israel, top ten ten in Germany, Switzerland and Italy, top twenty in Spain, Belgium, Finland and Denmark and top thirty in Brazil, Sweden and Hungary. In France, although it failed to enter in the top 50 from the main chart, the [[French Singles Chart]], the song reached the top ten in [[SNEP|French Download Singles 50]], at number 6, becoming her sixth top ten at the time.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://lescharts.com/weekchart.asp?cat=si&date=20100911|title= Les charts français|date=September 11, 2010|access-date=December 31, 2012}}</ref> It entered at number 75 several months after its release in the [[SNEP|French Singles Chart]] and stayed 6 weeks, becoming her shortest-running week until "[[Walking on Air (Katy Perry song)|Walking On Air]]" who stayed 5 weeks and debuted at number 25.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://lescharts.com/showitem.asp?interpret=Katy+Perry&titel=Teenage+Dream&cat=s|title=Les charts français - Katy Perry - Teenage Dream|date=January 29, 2011|access-date=April 23, 2014}}</ref> As of May 2022, "Teenage Dream" has sold 12 million copies worldwide.<ref name="wwsales">{{cite web|title=Se cumplen 12 años de 'California Gurls' y el comienzo de la era Teenage Dream de Katy Perry|last=Caridad|first=Mario|url=https://los40.com/los40/2022/05/24/musica/1653406899_452203.html|access-date=August 15, 2025|language=es|date=May 25, 2022|publisher=[[Los 40]]}}</ref>

==Music video==
===Background and concept===
[[File:TeenageDream video.jpg|thumb|left|210px|Perry during a beach party in the music video for "Teenage Dream"]]
The music video for "Teenage Dream" was filmed in [[Santa Barbara, California]], in July 2010, and was directed by [[Yoann Lemoine]].<ref name=mtvmv /> Perry planned the concept along with the songwriters during the sessions.<ref name=mckee /> Pictures from the shoot feature Perry kneeling in the sand looking at a man (played by Josh Kloss)<ref>{{cite magazine|url =https://people.com/celebrity/meet-josh-kloss-the-hot-guy-in-katy-perrys-latest-video/|title =Meet Josh Kloss: The Hot guy in Katy Perry's new video|magazine =[[People (magazine)|People]]|date=August 23, 2010|access-date =May 15, 2022}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://variety.com/2019/music/news/katy-perry-sexual-misconduct-josh-kloss-1203300452/ |title = 'Teenage Dream' Co-Star Accuses Katy Perry of Sexual Misconduct|work=Variety|first=Nate|last=Nickolai| date=August 13, 2019 }}</ref> wearing speedos, while others show her kissing the man in a hotel pool. She commented about the filming on her [[Twitter]], saying, "That's a wrap for Teenage Dream! So gorgeous [.....] In my hometown. I got to cast all my friends in the new music vid for Teenage Dream = amazing insanity."<ref name=mtvmv>{{cite web|url=http://www.mtv.com/news/1643826/katy-perry-talks-gorgeous-video-for-teenage-dream/|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150225052711/http://www.mtv.com/news/1643826/katy-perry-talks-gorgeous-video-for-teenage-dream/|url-status=dead|archive-date=February 25, 2015|title=Katy Perry Talks 'Gorgeous' Video For 'Teenage Dream'|last=Vena|first=Jocelyn|date=June 16, 2010|publisher=MTV News|access-date=May 15, 2022}}</ref> The video features images of Perry in a car with a boyfriend, then, Perry is shown in a party with her friends, and the closing scene shows Perry kissing her boyfriend under the water. Perry was interviewed online via [[YouTube]], where she talked about the music video. She said: "It is gonna be completely different from 'California Gurls.' With 'Teenage Dream' you'll see a very raw, almost vulnerable [side] ..... I had to wear so many less layers of [[Cosmetics|makeup]]. I had to make out with a boy which was very traumatizing, I was kind of mean to him. I would be the one to call cut because I was like, 'oh I can't do this!' I feel so horrible. But I know it's a job. [Russell and I] understand what our work is."<ref name=fmqb>{{cite magazine|url=http://www.fmqb.com/article.asp?id=1893526|title=Quick Hits: Kanye West, Katy Perry, American Idol judges, Michael Jackson, Smashing Pumpkins, John Lennon, Interpol|date=July 28, 2010|magazine=[[FMQB]]|publisher=Kal Rudman|access-date=July 28, 2010|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100802103530/http://www.fmqb.com/Article.asp?id=1893526|archive-date=August 2, 2010}}</ref>

==''Glee'' version==
The [[Beelzebubs]], a male [[a cappella]] group from [[Tufts University]], covered "Teenage Dream" and gained prominence when it was used on the American musical television series ''[[Glee (TV series)|Glee]]'' in the episode "[[Never Been Kissed (Glee)|Never Been Kissed]]".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.bostonherald.com/2010/11/10/we-hear-beelzebubs-brian-white-ideapaint-more/|title=We Hear: Beelzebubs, Brian White, IdeaPaint & more...|author=Inside Track|date=November 10, 2010|work=[[Boston Herald]]|access-date=May 15, 2022}}</ref> The Beelzebubs provided the arrangement and back-up vocals (recorded during September in [[New York City]]) for the performance, and actors playing students from the fictional [[The Dalton Academy Warblers|Dalton Academy Warblers]] lip-synced to their vocals.<ref name="Tufts">{{cite web|url=https://tuftsdaily.com/news/2010/11/09/bubs-to-be-heard-but-not-seen-on-tonights-glee/ |title=Bubs to be heard but not seen on tonight's 'Glee' |last1=Goldberg |first1=Rebecca |first2=Martha |last2=Shanahan |date=November 9, 2010 |work=[[The Tufts Daily]] |access-date=May 15, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101112065512/http://www.tuftsdaily.com/news/bubs-to-be-heard-but-not-seen-on-tonight-s-glee-1.2398811 |archive-date=November 12, 2010 }}</ref> The lead vocals were provided by [[Darren Criss]], playing [[Blaine Anderson]] in the episode.<ref name="Tufts" /> Following the airing of the episode in the US on November 9, 2010, the track was released as a single credited to the [[Glee discography|''Glee'' cast]]; the recording is also featured on ''[[Glee: The Music, Volume&nbsp;4]]''.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://music.apple.com/us/album/teenage-dream-glee-cast-version/401338437|title=Teenage Dream (Glee Cast Version)|work=[[Apple Music]]|access-date=May 15, 2022|archive-date=May 15, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220515215358/https://music.apple.com/us/album/teenage-dream-glee-cast-version/401338437|url-status=dead}}</ref> The cover sold 55,000 copies through the [[iTunes Store]] on the first day, breaking the group's first-day sales record previously held by "[[Empire State of Mind]]".<ref>{{Cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/glees-teenage-dream-targeting-big-chart-bow-next-week-1197763/|title='Glee's' 'Teenage Dream' Targeting Big Chart Bow Next Week|date=November 11, 2010|first=Keith|last=Caulfield|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|access-date=May 15, 2022}}</ref> Selling 214,000 copies over the week, the song debuted at the eighth position on the [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]], surpassing "[[Don't Stop Believin']]" for the highest first-week sales of any ''Glee'' song, and first top ten by Glee Cast on the chart since "Don't Stop Believin'".<ref>{{Cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/a-dream-come-true-glee-cast-and-susan-boyle-top-charts-1197519/|title=A 'Dream' Come True: 'Glee' Cast and Susan Boyle Top Charts|date=November 17, 2010|first=Keith|last=Caulfield|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|access-date=May 15, 2022|archive-date=November 20, 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101120213613/http://www.billboard.biz/bbbiz/content_display/industry/e3i156046bdea3250ce85794b3b5746aded}}</ref><ref>{{cite magazine|url=http://www.billboard.com/charts/2010-11-27/hot-100?order=gainer|title=Hot 100: Week of November 27, 2010 (Biggest Jump)|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|access-date=November 18, 2010}}</ref> It remains the third best-selling recording in the show's history, having sold 652,000 copies in the United States.<ref name=glee_sales>{{cite magazine |url=https://www.billboard.com/pro/glee-cast-10-best-selling-downloads/ |title='Glee' Cast's 10 Best-Selling Downloads |magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]] |date=March 20, 2015 |access-date=May 15, 2022}}</ref> The song also charted at number ten in Canada, twenty-four in Australia, and eighteen in Ireland.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=http://www.billboard.com/charts/2010-11-27/canadian-hot-100?order=gainer|title=Canadian Hot 100: Week of November 27, 2010 (Biggest Jump)|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|access-date=November 18, 2010}}</ref><ref name="ire"/><ref>{{cite web|url=http://australian-charts.com/showitem.asp?interpret=Glee+Cast&titel=Teenage+Dream&cat=s|title=Glee Cast – Teenage Dream (song)|publisher=Australian Charts|access-date=November 21, 2010}}</ref> The cover was included in a list of no particular order of the 210 best songs of 2010, published by the ''[[New York Post]]''.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://nypost.com/2010/12/28/the-top-210-songs-from-2010/|title=The top 210 songs from 2010|date=December 28, 2010|first1=Dan|last1=Aliquante|last2=Amoroso |first2=Christina |last3=Barracato |first3=Joe |last4=Heller |first4=Billy |last5=Heller |first5=Charlie |last6=Huhn |first6=Mary|work=[[New York Post]]|access-date=May 15, 2022}}</ref>

The song appears in three Glee albums: ''[[Glee: The Music, Volume 4]]'', ''[[Glee: The Music Presents the Warblers]]'' and the live album ''[[Glee: The 3D Concert Movie]]''. Criss covered the song again on ''Glee''{{'}}s  season four episode, "[[The Break-Up (Glee)|The Break-Up]]", on October 4, 2012, with a slower, piano-only version. Unlike the original version, which was lip-synced, Criss sang this version live on set to capture the emotion of the performance.<ref>{{cite web|first=MJ|last=Santelli|title=Glee Season 4 – The Break Up – Sneak Peek Feat "Teenage Dream" (VIDEO)|date=September 28, 2012|url=http://www.mjsbigblog.com/glee-season-4-the-break-up-sneak-peek-feat-teenage-dream-video.htm|publisher=MJs Big Blog|access-date=October 2, 2012}}</ref>
