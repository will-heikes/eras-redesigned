It later ranked as the best-performing single of the year.<ref name="yearinpop">{{cite web|last1=Caulfield|first1=Keith|title=The Year in Pop 2014: One Direction, 'Frozen,' & Pharrell Dominate|url=http://www.billboard.com/articles/events/year-in-music-2014/6386043/the-year-in-pop-2014-one-direction-frozen-pharrell|website=Billboard.com|access-date=2014-12-10|date=2014-12-09}}</ref>]]
The [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]] is a chart that ranks the best-performing [[single (music)|singles]] of the United States. Its data, published by ''[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]'' magazine and compiled by [[Nielsen SoundScan]], is based collectively on each single's weekly [[CD single|physical]] and [[Digital distribution|digital]] sales, as well as [[Heavy rotation|airplay]] and [[streaming media|streaming]].

During 2014, nine singles reached number one on the Hot 100; a tenth single, "[[The Monster (song)|The Monster]]" by [[Eminem]] featuring [[Rihanna]], began its run at number one in December 2013.<ref>{{Cite magazine|url=http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/5820081/eminem-rihanna-rule-hot-100-with-the-monster|magazine=Billboard|title=Eminem, Rihanna Rule Hot 100 With 'The Monster'|first=Gary|last=Trust|date=2013-12-11|access-date=2015-01-18}}</ref> Of those ten number-one singles, four were collaborations. In total, thirteen acts topped the chart as either lead or featured artists, with six—[[Juicy J]], [[John Legend]], [[Iggy Azalea]], [[Charli XCX]], [[Magic!]] and [[Meghan Trainor]]—achieving their first Hot 100 number-one single. [[Pharrell Williams]]' "[[Happy (Pharrell Williams song)|Happy]]" was the longest-running number-one of the year, leading the chart for ten weeks; it subsequently topped the [[Billboard Year-End Hot 100 singles of 2014|''Billboard'' Year-End Hot 100]].<ref name="yearinpop" />

In May, Legend's "[[All of Me (John Legend song)|All of Me]]" reached number one in its thirtieth week on the Hot 100, earning the distinction of the third-longest ascent to number one after [[Los del Río]]'s "[[Macarena (song)|Macarena]]" and [[Lonestar]]'s "[[Amazed]]".<ref name="allofme1">{{Cite magazine|url=http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/6077635/hot-100-john-legend-all-of-me-ariana-grande-iggy-azalea|magazine=Billboard|title=John Legend's 'All Of Me' Tops Hot 100, Ariana Grande Debuts At No. 3|first=Gary|last=Trust|date=2014-05-07|access-date=2014-05-07}}</ref> The song also became the third number-one to feature only piano and vocals, following [[Adele]]'s "[[Someone like You (Adele song)|Someone like You]]" and [[Bruno Mars]]' "[[When I Was Your Man]]".<ref name="allofme1" /> Azalea became the fourth solo female rapper to top the chart when "[[Fancy (Iggy Azalea song)|Fancy]]" reached number one in June, a feat previously achieved only by [[Lauryn Hill]], [[Lil' Kim]] and [[Shawnna]].<ref name="fancy1">{{Cite magazine|url=http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/6099390/iggy-azalea-tops-hot-100-fancy-matches-beatles|magazine=Billboard|title=Iggy Azalea Tops Hot 100 With 'Fancy,' Matches Beatles' Historic Mark|first=Gary|last=Trust|date=2014-05-28|access-date=2014-05-28}}</ref> In its sixth of seven weeks on top, "Fancy" became the longest-reigning single by a female rapper on the chart.<ref name="fancy6">{{Cite magazine|url=http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/6143261/hot-100-iggy-azalea-fancy-magic-rude-shawn-mendes|magazine=Billboard|title=Iggy Azalea Claims Longest Hot 100 Reign For A Female Rapper With 'Fancy'|first=Gary|last=Trust|date=2014-07-02|access-date=2014-07-02}}</ref>

[[Taylor Swift]]'s "[[Blank Space]]" reached number one in November, ending the run of Swift's own "[[Shake It Off]]".<ref name="blankspace1">{{Cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/pro/taylor-swift-blank-space-hot-100/|magazine=Billboard|title=Taylor Swift Makes Hot 100 History With 'Blank Space'|first=Gary|last=Trust|date=2014-11-19|access-date=2014-11-19}}</ref> In doing so, Swift became the first female artist in the history of the Hot 100, and tenth act overall, to replace herself at the top of the chart.<ref name="blankspace1" /> With "Shake It Off" and "Blank Space", Swift was also the only artist to notch multiple number-ones in 2014.<ref name="blankspace1" />

2014 was also the first year in 18 years to have all of its number-one singles that topped the chart that year enter the decade-end top 100 in the 2010s, after all of the number-one songs from [[List of Billboard Hot 100 number-one singles of 1996|1996]] made the decade-end top 100 of the 1990s. (The Monster did not make the decade-end top 100, but started its peak position in 2013.)<ref>{{cite book|author=Geoff Mayfield|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=9w0EAAAAMBAJ&pg=RA1-PA4|title=1999 The Year in Music Totally '90s: Diary of a Decade - The listing of Top Pop Albums of the '90s & Hot 100 Singles of the '90s|publisher=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|date=December 25, 1999|access-date=October 15, 2010}}</ref><ref>{{Cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/charts/decade-end/hot-100|title=Hot 100 Songs - Decade-End|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]] }}</ref>
