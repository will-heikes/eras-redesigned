[[File:Robin Thicke 2012 cropped.jpg|thumb|right|170px|[[Robin Thicke]] ''(pictured)'' earned his first Hot 100 number-one single with "[[Blurred Lines]]", which stayed at the top position for twelve straight weeks.]]
The [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]] is a chart that ranks the best-performing [[single (music)|singles]] of the United States. Its data, published by ''[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]'' magazine and compiled by [[Nielsen SoundScan]], is based collectively on each single's weekly [[CD single|physical]] and [[Digital distribution|digital]] sales, as well as [[Heavy rotation|airplay]] and [[streaming media|streaming]]. In 2013, a total of 11 singles claimed the top spot in 52 issues of the magazine. One of which, "[[Locked Out of Heaven]]" by singer [[Bruno Mars]] started its peak position in late 2012.<ref name="locked">{{Cite magazine|url=http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/1481307/bruno-mars-has-top-hot-100-spot-locked-up-again|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|title=Bruno Mars Has Top Hot 100 Spot 'Locked' Up Again |first=Gary|last=Trust|date=2012-12-12|access-date=2012-12-19|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130213044518/http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/1481307/bruno-mars-has-top-hot-100-spot-locked-up-again|archive-date=2013-02-13}}</ref>

Throughout 2013, eight acts achieved their first US number-one single, either as a lead artist or a featured guest: [[Macklemore & Ryan Lewis]],{{efn|Macklemore & Ryan Lewis were credited as a duo, not two individual acts.<ref name="hold"/>}} [[Wanz]], [[Baauer]], [[Ray Dalton]], [[Robin Thicke]], [[Miley Cyrus]] and [[Lorde]]. [[Nate Ruess]], already having hit number one with [[Fun (band)|Fun]], earns his first number one song as a solo act. Five collaboration singles topped the chart. Macklemore & Ryan Lewis' debut single "[[Thrift Shop]]" featuring Wanz became the best-performing single of 2013, peaking atop the chart for six weeks,<ref name="numberones">{{cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/pro/2013-year-end-hot-100-by-the-numbers/|title=2013 Year-End Hot 100 By The Numbers|magazine=Billboard|access-date=2013-12-25|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131219115456/http://www.billboard.com/articles/columns/chart-beat/5827382/2013-year-end-hot-100-by-the-numbers|archive-date=2013-12-19}}</ref> also topping the [[Billboard Year-End Hot 100 singles of 2013|''Billboard'' Year-End Hot 100]].<ref name="topping">{{cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/pro/macklemore-ryan-lewis-bruno-mars-highlight-year-end-hot-100/|title=Macklemore & Ryan Lewis, Bruno Mars Highlight Year-End Hot 100 Chart|first1=Gary|last1=Trust|first2=Keith|last2=Caulfield|magazine=Billboard|date=2013-12-13|access-date=2013-12-25|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131219115455/http://www.billboard.com/articles/columns/chart-beat/5827379/macklemore-ryan-lewis-bruno-mars-highlight-year-end-hot-100|archive-date=2013-12-19}}</ref> The duo's second hit "[[Can't Hold Us]]" also reached the chart's top spot, making the duo the first act to score number-one singles on the Hot 100 with their first two charting songs since [[Lady Gaga]] with her hits "[[Just Dance (song)|Just Dance]]" and "[[Poker Face (Lady Gaga song)|Poker Face]]" in 2009.<ref name="hold"/> Baauer scored his first number-one hit with "[[Harlem Shake (song)|Harlem Shake]]".<ref name="shake"/> Originally, it was released commercially in June 2012, yet it did not sell significantly until February due to viral videos on [[YouTube]] and later created a [[Harlem Shake (meme)|meme with the same name]].<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.rollingstone.com/music/news/how-baauer-took-harlem-shake-to-number-one-20130222|title=How Baauer Took 'Harlem Shake' to Number One|magazine=[[Rolling Stone]]|first=Steve|last=Knopper|date=2013-02-22|access-date=2013-12-26|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130224123301/http://www.rollingstone.com/music/news/how-baauer-took-harlem-shake-to-number-one-20130222|archive-date=2013-02-24}}</ref>

Macklemore & Ryan Lewis were the only act to have more than one number one song, with two.

"[[When I Was Your Man]]" was Bruno Mars' fifth single to top the chart.<ref name="man"/> With the achievement, Mars became [[List of Billboard Hot 100 chart achievements and milestones|the fastest male artist]] to gain five number-one singles since [[Elvis Presley]].<ref name="man"/> It was also the second hit only featuring piano and vocals to peak atop the Hot 100 chart since [[Adele]]'s "[[Someone like You (Adele song)|Someone like You]]" (2011).<ref name="man"/> Singer Lorde's song "[[Royals (Lorde song)|Royals]]" made her the youngest solo artist to achieve a number-one single in the US since [[Tiffany Darwish|Tiffany]]'s "[[I Think We're Alone Now]]" (1988).<ref name="lorde2">{{cite magazine|url=http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/5748180/lordes-royals-scores-global-samsung-commercial-watch|title=Lorde's 'Royals' Scores Global Samsung Commercial: Watch|first=Andrew|last=Hampp|magazine=Billboard|date=2013-10-09|access-date=2013-12-25|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131009145110/http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/5748180/lordes-royals-scores-global-samsung-commercial-watch|archive-date=2013-10-09}}</ref><ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://entertainment.time.com/2013/10/04/lorde-is-youngest-performer-to-top-billboard-charts-in-26-years/|title=Lorde is Youngest Performer to Top Billboard Charts in 26 Years|first=Tim|last=Newcomb|date=2013-10-04|magazine=[[Time (magazine)|Time]]|access-date=2013-12-27|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131004164716/http://entertainment.time.com/2013/10/04/lorde-is-youngest-performer-to-top-billboard-charts-in-26-years/|archive-date=2013-10-04}}</ref> "[[The Monster (song)|The Monster]]" by [[Eminem]] featuring [[Rihanna]] became the former's fifth number-one single in the country, tying him with [[Sean Combs|P. Diddy]] and [[Ludacris]] as the rappers with the most number-one songs on the chart.<ref name="monster"/> Meanwhile, it also made Rihanna the artist with the third-most US number-one singles (13), alongside [[Michael Jackson]].<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.digitalspy.co.uk/music/news/a538306/eminem-rihanna-unveil-the-monster-music-video-teaser--watch.html|title=Eminem, Rihanna unveil 'The Monster' music video teaser|first=Justin|last=Harp|date=2013-12-13|publisher=[[Digital Spy]]|access-date=2013-12-27}}</ref>

"[[Blurred Lines]]" by Robin Thicke featuring [[T.I.]] and [[Pharrell Williams|Pharrell]] was the longest-running single of the year at the top spot, peaking at number one for twelve consecutive weeks.<ref name="numberones"/> With the combined chart run throughout June, July and August, "Blurred Lines" became ''Billboard''{{'}}s Song of the Summer 2013.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/5687036/robin-thickes-blurred-lines-is-billboards-song-of-the-summer|title=Robin Thicke's 'Blurred Lines' Is Billboard's Song of the Summer|first=Gary|last=Trust|magazine=Billboard|date=2013-09-05|access-date=2013-12-26|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130906215102/http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/5687036/robin-thickes-blurred-lines-is-billboards-song-of-the-summer|archive-date=2013-09-06}}</ref> Lorde's "Royals" became the second longest-running number-one single, claiming the top spot for a total of nine weeks.<ref name="numberones"/> "Locked Out of Heaven" by Bruno Mars and "Thrift Shop" by Macklemore & Ryan Lewis featuring Wanz both spent six weeks atop the Hot 100.<ref name="numberones"/>{{efn|"Locked Out of Heaven" peaked at number one on the Hot 100 for two weeks in 2012,<ref name="locked"/> and four weeks in 2013.<ref name="locked2"/>}}
