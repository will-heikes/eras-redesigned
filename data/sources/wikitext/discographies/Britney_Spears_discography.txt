American singer [[Britney Spears]] has released nine studio [[album]]s, eight [[compilation album]]s, nine [[box set]]s, three [[extended play]]s (EPs), 50 [[Single (music)|singles]] (including two as a featured artist), 12 [[Promotional recording|promotional singles]], two [[charity single]]s, and has made three [[Guest vocalist|guest appearances]]. In 1997, Spears signed a recording contract with American record label [[Jive Records]] in order to launch her career.<ref>{{cite magazine | author = Taylor, Chuck | url = https://books.google.com/books?id=JQoEAAAAMBAJ&pg=PA80 | magazine = [[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]] | title = Air Waves: Jive's Britney Spears Sets Top 40 Abuzz With Rhythm-Leaning 'Baby One More Time' | volume = 110 | issue = 43 | date = October 24, 1998 | access-date = March 11, 2011 | issn = 0006-2510 | archive-date = June 17, 2021 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20210617065142/https://books.google.com/books?id=JQoEAAAAMBAJ&pg=PA80 | url-status = live }}</ref>

Spears made her chart debut in November 1998 with "[[...Baby One More Time]]", which was followed by the release of her debut studio album, ''[[...Baby One More Time (album)|...Baby One More Time]]'' (1999). The record opened the [[Canadian Albums Chart]] and the US [[Billboard 200|''Billboard'' 200]] at number one, being later certified fourteen times platinum by the [[Recording Industry Association of America]] (RIAA).<ref name="xcellent">{{cite magazine|url=http://www.billboard.com/articles/chartbeat/485947/ask-billboard-spears-lovatos-x-cellent-sales-updated|title=Ask Billboard: Spears, Lovato's 'X'-cellent Sales|last=Trust|first=Gary|date=May 27, 2012|access-date=May 27, 2012|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|archive-date=March 4, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160304060442/http://www.billboard.com/articles/chartbeat/485947/ask-billboard-spears-lovatos-x-cellent-sales-updated|url-status=live}}</ref> The singer's second studio album, ''[[Oops!... I Did It Again (album)|Oops!... I Did It Again]]'', was made available for consumption on May 16, 2000, and became the fastest-selling record ever by a female act in America, bringing first-week sales of 1,319,193 units and certified diamond in United States.<ref>{{cite magazine | title=Britney Bound For Sales Record | magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]] | date=May 18, 2000 | access-date=May 28, 2012 | url=http://www.billboard.com/#/news/britney-bound-for-sales-record-875779.story | archive-date=October 13, 2011 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111013225745/http://www.billboard.com/#/news/britney-bound-for-sales-record-875779.story | url-status=live }}</ref> It spawned four singles—"[[Oops!... I Did It Again (song)|Oops!... I Did It Again]]", "[[Lucky (Britney Spears song)|Lucky]]", "[[Stronger (Britney Spears song)|Stronger]]", and "[[Don't Let Me Be the Last to Know]]". In November 2001, the singer's [[Britney (album)|third album]] spawned worldwide hit "[[I'm a Slave 4 U]]", which had been pointed out by [[music journalism|music critics]] for being a musical departure from her previous material.<ref>{{cite magazine | first=Todd | last=Marten | title=Teen Queen Britney Knocks King of Pop from No. 1 | magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]] | date=November 15, 2001 | access-date=June 25, 2009 | url=http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/77732/teen-queen-britney-knocks-king-of-pop-from-no-1 | archive-date=May 18, 2013 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130518164854/http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/77732/teen-queen-britney-knocks-king-of-pop-from-no-1 | url-status=live }}</ref> Spears released her fourth studio album, ''[[In the Zone]]'', in November 2003, which featured "[[Me Against the Music]]"—a collaboration with [[Madonna]] that reached the top position of the [[European Hot 100 Singles]]—and "[[Toxic (song)|Toxic]]", which earned Spears her first [[Grammy]] in the category of [[Grammy Award for Best Dance Recording|Best Dance Recording]], and gained her credibility among critics.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.npr.org/templates/story/story.php?storyId=120385043|title=The Decade In Music: Britney Spears' 'Toxic' (2004) : NPR|year=2009|access-date=September 11, 2010|last=Schriefer|first=Amy|publisher=[[NPR]]. NPR|archive-date=August 22, 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100822173555/http://www.npr.org/templates/story/story.php?storyId=120385043|url-status=live}}</ref> The singer's first compilation album, ''[[Greatest Hits: My Prerogative]]'', was released the following year.

Following experiencing personal struggles through 2007,<ref>{{cite news|url=http://edition.cnn.com/2007/SHOWBIZ/Music/10/01/spears.federline/index.html|title=Spears will lose custody of children|date=October 2, 2007|access-date=June 11, 2010|publisher=[[CNN]]|archive-date=September 1, 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100901195707/http://edition.cnn.com/2007/SHOWBIZ/Music/10/01/spears.federline/index.html|url-status=live}}</ref> Spears's fifth studio album, ''[[Blackout (Britney Spears album)|Blackout]]'', was released in October of the same year. Unlike all of the singer's previous records, ''Blackout'' failed to be heavily promoted through magazine interviews, talk-show appearances, or televised performances—besides a performance at the [[2007 MTV Video Music Awards]]—and was not accompanied by a supporting tour either.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2007/10/29/arts/music/29spea.html?_r=1&ref=music&oref=slogin|title='Miss Bad Media Karma' Sings, Too|last=Sanneh|first=Kelefa|date=October 29, 2007|access-date=August 8, 2011|work=[[The New York Times]]|archive-date=April 2, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150402184334/http://www.nytimes.com/2007/10/29/arts/music/29spea.html?_r=1&ref=music&oref=slogin|url-status=live}}</ref> With the release of her sixth studio album ''[[Circus (Britney Spears album)|Circus]]'', Spears became the only act in the [[Nielsen SoundScan]] era—from 1991 to present,—to have four records debuting with 500,000 or more copies sold in the United States.<ref>{{cite magazine | first=Katie | last=Hasty | title=Britney's 'Circus' Debuts Atop Album Chart | magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]] | url=http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/266126/britneys-circus-debuts-atop-album-chart | date=December 10, 2008 | access-date=June 25, 2009 | archive-date=May 18, 2013 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130518190850/http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/266126/britneys-circus-debuts-atop-album-chart | url-status=live }}</ref> Supported by the releases of commercially successful international hits like "[[Womanizer (song)|Womanizer]]" and "[[Circus (Britney Spears song)|Circus]]", it managed to sale four million copies globally.<ref name="CircusWW2012">{{cite web|title=Britney Spears vendió un millón de copias de "Blackout"|url=http://www.voanoticias.com/content/britney-spears-blackout/1145339.html|publisher=[[Voice of America]]|access-date=April 20, 2013|date=May 30, 2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120531224758/http://www.voanoticias.com/content/britney-spears-blackout/1145339.html|archive-date=May 31, 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref> The singer's third compilation album, ''[[The Singles Collection (Britney Spears album)|The Singles Collection]]'', featured her third number-one single in America, "[[3 (Britney Spears song)|3]]". In 2011, she released the song "[[Hold It Against Me]]", which made Spears the second artist in the ''Billboard'' Hot 100's 52-year history to debut at number one with two or more songs, just behind American recording artist [[Mariah Carey]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://new.music.yahoo.com/blogs/chart_watch/73797/week-ending-jan-16-2011-songs-britney-tops-taylor/ |title=Week Ending Jan. 16, 2011: Songs: Britney Tops Taylor |last=Grein |first=Paul |publisher=[[Yahoo! Music]] |date=January 19, 2011 |access-date=January 23, 2011 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110124195857/http://new.music.yahoo.com/blogs/chart_watch/73797/week-ending-jan-16-2011-songs-britney-tops-taylor |archive-date=January 24, 2011 }}</ref> The track was included on her seventh studio album, ''[[Femme Fatale (Britney Spears album)|Femme Fatale]]'', which debuted at number one in that country. Also her first album ever to yield three top ten hits in the US, including commercially successful singles like "[[Till the World Ends]]" and "[[I Wanna Go (Britney Spears song)|I Wanna Go]]".<ref>{{cite magazine|url=http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/472190/britney-spears-snares-sixth-no-1-on-billboard-200-with-femme-fatale|title=Britney Spears Snares Sixth No. 1 on Billboard 200 with 'Femme Fatale'|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|date=April 6, 2011|access-date=April 6, 2011|archive-date=May 26, 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110526040752/http://www.billboard.com/|url-status=live}}</ref> Spears's eighth studio album, ''[[Britney Jean]]'', was released in 2013. It marks Spears's first major activity under [[RCA Records]] since the dissolution of her long-time record label, Jive Records, in 2011. Receiving mixed reviews from music critics, it experienced minor commercial success, and thus became the lowest-selling record of her career. Spears began working on her ninth studio album in 2014; she also renewed her record deal with RCA Records. ''[[Glory (Britney Spears album)|Glory]]'' was released in 2016 and received positive reviews from music critics. The album debuted at number three on US ''Billboard'' 200 with 111,000 [[album-equivalent units]], including 88,000 copies sold, and spawned the singles "[[Make Me (Britney Spears song)|Make Me]]" and "[[Slumber Party (song)|Slumber Party]]", which peaked at 17 and 86 on the Hot 100, and topped the [[Dance Club Songs]] in the United States.

Spears has sold over 150 million records worldwide,<ref>{{Cite web |last=Gregory |first=Elizabeth |date=October 19, 2023|title=The Woman in Me: Britney Spears' best songs of all time, ranked, from Piece Of Me to Toxic |url= https://www.standard.co.uk/culture/music/britney-spears-best-songs-ranked-list-b1114018.html |access-date=October 28, 2023|website=[[Evening Standard]] }}</ref> including 70 million records in United States (36.9 million digital singles and 33.6 million digital albums), making her one of the [[List of best-selling music artists|best-selling music artists of all time]].<ref name=bb2019/><ref name="2011musicreport">{{cite press release|url=http://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20120105005547/en/Nielsen-Company-Billboard%E2%80%99s-2011-Music-Industry-Report |title=The Nielsen Company & Billboard's 2011 Music Industry Report |publisher=[[Business Wire]] |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120108044835/http://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20120105005547/en/Nielsen-Company-Billboard%E2%80%99s-2011-Music-Industry-Report |archive-date=January 8, 2012 |url-status=dead }}</ref> ''Billboard'' ranked her the eighth overall Artist of the Decade, and also recognized her as the best-selling female album artist of the 21st century's first decade, as well as the fifth overall.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/266420/artists-of-the-decade|title=Artists of the Decade|year=2010|access-date=May 3, 2010|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|archive-date=November 12, 2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121112192911/http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/266420/artists-of-the-decade|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://new.music.yahoo.com/blogs/chart_watch/34074/chart-watch-extra-the-top-20-album-sellers-of-the-2000s|title=Chart Watch Extra: The Top 20 Album Sellers Of The 2000s|date=May 29, 2009|access-date=May 3, 2010|last=Grein|first=Paul|publisher=[[Yahoo! Music]]|archive-date=January 15, 2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120115034358/http://music.yahoo.com/blogs/chart-watch/chart-watch-extra-the-top-20-album-sellers-of-the-2000s.html|url-status=live}}</ref> Additionally, the Recording Industry Association of America recognized Spears as the ninth [[List of best-selling music artists in the United States|best-selling female artist in the United States]], with 38.5 million [[RIAA certification|certified]] albums.<ref>{{cite web|title=Top Selling Artists|url=https://www.riaa.com/goldandplatinum.php?content_selector=top-selling-artists|publisher=[[Recording Industry Association of America]]|access-date=July 6, 2012|archive-date=December 9, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131209120422/http://www.riaa.com/goldandplatinum.php?content_selector=top-selling-artists|url-status=live}}</ref> Spears serves as one of the few artists in history to have a number-one single and studio album in each of the three decades of their career—1990s, 2000s, and 2010s.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/pro/ask-billboard-britney-spears-3-decades-of-no-1s/|title=Britney Spears' '3' Decades of No. 1s|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|date=February 11, 2011|access-date=October 6, 2011|archive-date=June 28, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140628203710/http://www.billboard.com/articles/columns/chart-beat/473073/ask-billboard-britney-spears-3-decades-of-no-1s|url-status=live}}</ref> As of 2020, Spears has reportedly drawn 25.9 billion in cumulative radio airplay audience and 3.7 billion on-demand U.S. audio and video streams combined.<ref name=billboard2020/>
