American singer-songwriter [[Lana Del Rey]] has released nine [[studio album]]s, four [[extended play]]s (EPs), 54 [[single (music)|singles]] (including 11 as a featured artist), 17 [[promotional single]]s, and two [[box set]]s. She has sold over 77 million records and has garnered over 58 billion streams worldwide.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2026-04-16 |title=IO Interactive and Amazon MGM Studios reveal Lana Del Rey and David Arnold as title song artists from 007 First Light |url=https://www.universalmusic.ca/2026/04/16/io-interactive-and-amazon-mgm-studios-reveal-lana-del-rey-and-david-arnold-as-title-song-artists-for-007-first-light/ |access-date=2026-07-03 |website= |publisher=[[Universal Music Group]], [[IO Interactive]] and [[Amazon Studios]] |language=en |quote=}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |last=Curran |first=Shaun |date=June 24, 2025 |title=Lana Del Rey Was As Enchanting As She Was Odd at Cardiff's Principality Stadium |url=https://inews.co.uk/culture/music/lana-del-rey-live-cardiff-review-enchanting-odd-3767207 |access-date=January 13, 2026 |newspaper=[[The i Paper]]}}</ref> In the United States, Del Rey has earned 55 million [[Music recording certification|certified]] units across albums and singles from the [[Recording Industry Association of America]] (RIAA).<ref>{{Cite web|title=Searchable Database — RIAA|url=https://www.riaa.com/gold-platinum/?tab_active=awards_by_artist#search_section|access-date=January 13, 2025|publisher=[[Recording Industry Association of America]]|archive-date=December 31, 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251231084558/https://www.riaa.com/gold-platinum/?tab_active=awards_by_artist#search_section|url-status=live}}</ref> With over 25.9 million certified units from the [[British Phonographic Industry]] (BPI), she is one of the [[List of best-selling female music artists in the United Kingdom|best-selling female music artists in the United Kingdom]].<ref>{{cite certification|region=United Kingdom|artist=Lana Del Rey|access-date=July 1, 2026}}</ref>

Del Rey signed a record deal with 5 Points Records in 2007.<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Way |first=Mish |date=September 30, 2015 |title=How Lizzy Grant Became Lana Del Rey |url=https://www.complex.com/music/a/myszkaway/how-lizzy-grant-became-lana-del-rey |access-date=January 13, 2026 |magazine=[[Complex (magazine)|Complex]] |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251003020342/https://www.complex.com/music/a/myszkaway/how-lizzy-grant-became-lana-del-rey |archive-date=October 3, 2025 |url-status=live }}</ref> Through 5 Points, she released her debut EP, ''[[Kill Kill]]'' (2008), under the name Lizzy Grant, and her debut studio album, ''[[Lana Del Ray (album)|Lana Del Ray]]'' (2010). ''Lana Del Ray'' has been pulled from music stores because 5 Points was unable to fund it.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.mtv.com/news/2695337/lana-del-rey-first-album-5-points-records-interview/|title=Why Lana Del Rey's First Album Disappeared|publisher=[[MTV News]]|first=Mike|last=Ayers|date=January 30, 2012|access-date=February 6, 2021|archive-date=March 31, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210331230706/http://www.mtv.com/news/2695337/lana-del-rey-first-album-5-points-records-interview/}}</ref> In 2011, Del Rey [[self-released]] her debut single, "[[Video Games (Lana Del Rey song)|Video Games]]", which reached the top ten and received certifications in many European countries, including double platinum certifications in Germany (where it reached number one) and Switzerland. "Video Games" was certified triple platinum in the US.

In 2012, Del Rey signed a joint record deal with [[Interscope Records|Interscope]] and [[Polydor Records]] and released her second studio album, ''[[Born to Die]]'', which contained "Video Games".<ref>{{cite news|last=Savage|first=Mark|title=Love, the law, and Lana Del Rey|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/entertainment-arts-16729651|publisher=[[BBC News]]|access-date=April 15, 2014|date=January 27, 2012|archive-date=February 1, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200201202721/https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/entertainment-arts-16729651|url-status=live}}</ref> ''Born to Die'' peaked at number two on the US [[Billboard 200|''Billboard'' 200]] and reached number one in Australia and many European countries. By 2014, the album had sold one million copies in the US and seven million copies worldwide.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.vogue.com/article/lana-del-rey-second-album-ultraviolence|title=Lana Del Rey Releases Her Second Album, Ultraviolence|date=June 18, 2014|magazine=[[Vogue (magazine)|Vogue]]|access-date=January 9, 2019|archive-date=October 21, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181021190726/https://www.vogue.com/article/lana-del-rey-second-album-ultraviolence|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/pro/lana-del-reys-born-to-die-crosses-1-million-copies-sold/|title=Lana Del Rey's 'Born To Die' Crosses 1 Million Copies Sold|date=March 26, 2014|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|access-date=January 9, 2019|archive-date=August 28, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190828100434/https://www.billboard.com/articles/columns/chart-beat/6022008/lana-del-reys-born-to-die-crosses-1-million-copies-sold|url-status=live}}</ref> It spawned five other singles: "[[Born to Die (Lana Del Rey song)|Born to Die]]", "[[Blue Jeans (Lana Del Rey song)|Blue Jeans]]", "[[Summertime Sadness]]", "[[National Anthem (Lana Del Rey song)|National Anthem]]", and "[[Dark Paradise]]". "Summertime Sadness" reached the top ten in various countries worldwide and was supported by a [[remix]] by [[Cedric Gervais]], which peaked at number six in the US, becoming Del Rey's first top-ten single.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/lana-del-rey-sam-smith-jennifer-lopez-linkin-park-deadmau5-album-poll-6121562/|title=Lana Del Rey, Sam Smith, Jennifer Lopez, Linkin Park, deadmau5: Which New Album Are You Buying?|first=Jason|last=Lipshutz|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|date=June 17, 2014|access-date=December 13, 2014|archive-date=September 24, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150924194058/http://www.billboard.com/articles/columns/pop-shop/6121562/lana-del-rey-sam-smith-jennifer-lopez-linkin-park-deadmau5-album-poll|url-status=live}}</ref> In Australia and the US, "Born to Die", "Blue Jeans", and "Summertime Sadness" were certified multi-platinum, with "National Anthem" and "Dark Paradise" receiving platinum certifications. In 2025, ''Born to Die'' became the [[Billboard 200#Most weeks on the chart|longest-charting album by a female artist in ''Billboard'' 200 history]].<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Boisvert |first=Lauren |date=December 18, 2025 |title=Lana Del Rey Sets Record as ''Born To Die'' Continues Long-Held Chart Residency |url=https://www.vice.com/en/article/lana-del-rey-sets-record-as-born-to-die-continues-long-held-chart-residency/ |magazine=[[Vice (magazine)|Vice]] |access-date=July 18, 2026 |archive-date=July 6, 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260706035830/https://www.vice.com/en/article/lana-del-rey-sets-record-as-born-to-die-continues-long-held-chart-residency/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

Del Rey released an EP, ''[[Paradise (Lana Del Rey EP)|Paradise]]'', and a [[reissue]] of ''Born to Die'', ''[[Born to Die: The Paradise Edition]]'', in late 2012. She recorded and released the singles "[[Young and Beautiful (Lana Del Rey song)|Young and Beautiful]]" and "[[Once Upon a Dream (Lana Del Rey song)|Once Upon a Dream]]" for the soundtracks of ''[[The Great Gatsby: Music from Baz Luhrmann's Film|The Great Gatsby]]'' (2013) and ''[[Maleficent (film)|Maleficent]]'' (2014), respectively. "Young and Beautiful" was certified multi-platinum in Australia, New Zealand, the UK, and the US. Del Rey's third studio album, ''[[Ultraviolence (album)|Ultraviolence]]'' (2014), was her first US ''Billboard'' 200 number one.<ref>{{cite magazine|last1=Caulfield|first1=Keith|title=Lana Del Rey Lands First No. 1 Album On Billboard 200|url=https://www.billboard.com/pro/lana-del-rey-lands-first-no-1-album-on-billboard-200/|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|access-date=June 25, 2014|date=June 25, 2014|archive-date=September 25, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160925204105/http://www.billboard.com/articles/columns/chart-beat/6133901/lana-del-rey-lands-first-no-1-album-on-billboard-200|url-status=live}}</ref> It peaked atop the charts in Australia, Canada, New Zealand, and the UK. ''Ultraviolence'' spawned the singles "[[West Coast (Lana Del Rey song)|West Coast]]" (which was certified multi-platinum in Australia, New Zealand, and the US), "[[Shades of Cool]]", "[[Ultraviolence (song)|Ultraviolence]]", and "[[Brooklyn Baby]]". Del Rey's fourth studio album, ''[[Honeymoon (Lana Del Rey album)|Honeymoon]]'' (2015), reached number one in Australia and Ireland and spawned the singles "[[High by the Beach]]" and "[[Music to Watch Boys To]]".

Del Rey's fifth studio album, ''[[Lust for Life (Lana Del Rey album)|Lust for Life]]'' (2017), was her second number-one album in the US,<ref>{{cite magazine |last1=Caulfield |first1=Keith |title=Lana Del Rey Debuts at No. 1 on Billboard 200 Albums Chart, Tyler, The Creator and Meek Mill Bow at Nos. 2 & 3 |url=https://www.billboard.com/pro/lana-del-rey-lust-for-life-no-1-billboard-200-albums/ |magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]] |access-date=August 2, 2017 |archive-date=August 6, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170806123342/http://www.billboard.com/articles/columns/chart-beat/7882489/lana-del-rey-lust-for-life-no-1-billboard-200-albums |url-status=live }}</ref> and peaked atop the charts in Australia, Canada, and the UK. Two of its singles, "[[Love (Lana Del Rey song)|Love]]" and "[[Lust for Life (Lana Del Rey song)|Lust for Life]]" (featuring [[the Weeknd]]), were certified platinum in Australia and the US. Del Rey's sixth studio album, ''[[Norman Fucking Rockwell!]]'' (2019), reached number one in Switzerland and the UK. One of its singles, "[[Doin' Time (Lana Del Rey song)|Doin' Time]]", was certified platinum in Australia, New Zealand, the UK, and the US. Del Rey's collaboration with [[Ariana Grande]] and [[Miley Cyrus]], "[[Don't Call Me Angel]]" (2019) from the ''[[Charlie's Angels: Original Motion Picture Soundtrack|Charlie's Angels]]'' soundtrack, reached the top five in Australia, Ireland, Switzerland, and the UK. In 2020, she released a [[spoken word album]] for her debut poetry book, ''[[Violet Bent Backwards over the Grass]]''.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://thequietus.com/articles/28875-lana-del-rey-violet-bent-backwards-over-the-grass-review|title=Violet Bent Backwards over the Grass|magazine=[[The Quietus]]|first=Nick|last=Roseblade|date=September 4, 2020|access-date=September 4, 2020|archive-date=April 21, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210421184231/https://thequietus.com/articles/28875-lana-del-rey-violet-bent-backwards-over-the-grass-review|url-status=live}}</ref> The next year, she released the albums ''[[Chemtrails over the Country Club]]'' and ''[[Blue Banisters]]''. The former reached number one in Switzerland and the UK, and [[Chemtrails over the Country Club (song)|its title track]] received gold certifications in multiple countries worldwide.

Del Rey featured on [[Taylor Swift]]'s single "[[Snow on the Beach]]" (2022), which reached the top five in Canada, Ireland, New Zealand, the UK, and the US. Her ninth studio album, ''[[Did You Know That There's a Tunnel Under Ocean Blvd]]'' (2023), peaked at number one in Australia, Ireland, New Zealand, and the UK. It spawned four singles: [[Did You Know That There's a Tunnel Under Ocean Blvd (song)|the title track]], "[[A&W (song)|A&W]]", "[[The Grants]]", and "[[Candy Necklace]]" (featuring [[Jon Batiste]]). She also released the single "[[Say Yes to Heaven]]" in 2023, which reached the top 20 on the [[Billboard Global 200|''Billboard'' Global 200]] and was certified double platinum in Australia.
