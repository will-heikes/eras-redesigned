The American [[Rock music|rock]] band [[Fall Out Boy]] has released eight [[studio album]]s, two  [[live album]]s, two [[compilation albums]], one remix album, one mixtape, nine [[extended play]]s, 49 (seven as a featured artist) [[Singles (music)|singles]], and 55 [[music video]]s. The band was formed in [[Wilmette, Illinois]] by friends [[Joe Trohman]] and [[Pete Wentz]], who had played in local Chicago hardcore punk and heavy metal bands; [[Patrick Stump]] was soon recruited as the band's lead singer.<ref>{{cite magazine | url=https://www.rollingstone.com/news/story/7546662/fall_out_boys_sugar_rush | title=Fall Out Boy's Sugar Rush | publisher=[[Jann Wenner|Wenner Media]] | magazine=[[Rolling Stone]] | date=August 12, 2005 | access-date=March 1, 2013 | author=Gitlin, Lauren | url-status=dead | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20091224142303/http://www.rollingstone.com/news/story/7546662/fall_out_boys_sugar_rush | archive-date=December 24, 2009}}</ref> They debuted with the [[Split album|split EP]] ''[[Project Rocket / Fall Out Boy]]'' (2002) and the [[mini-LP]] ''[[Fall Out Boy's Evening Out with Your Girlfriend]]'' (2003), both released on [[Uprising Records]].<ref name="Evening"/> After the release of the latter, drummer [[Andy Hurley]] joined Fall Out Boy and Stump picked up guitar, forming the band's current lineup.<ref>{{cite web | url=http://www.jbtvonline.com/archives/Fall-Out-Boy/all-years/Arlington-Heights-Knights-of-Columbus | title=Fall Out Boy – Arlington Heights Knights Of Columbus | work=[[JBTV]] | access-date=December 12, 2009 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100606234015/http://www.jbtvonline.com/archives/Fall-Out-Boy/all-years/Arlington-Heights-Knights-of-Columbus | archive-date=June 6, 2010 | url-status=dead }}</ref> After signing to indie-label [[Fueled by Ramen]], Fall Out Boy released their first full-length studio album, ''[[Take This to Your Grave]]'', in May 2003. Following the album's release, the band signed with major label [[Island Records]]. Their second studio album ''[[From Under the Cork Tree]]'' was released in May 2005 to great commercial success, peaking at number nine on the United States [[Billboard 200|''Billboard'' 200]] and being certified five-times platinum by the [[Recording Industry Association of America]] (RIAA).<ref name="US-albums" /><ref name="RIAA" /> The album's popularity was aided by the success of its first two singles, "[[Sugar, We're Goin Down]]" and "[[Dance, Dance (Fall Out Boy song)|Dance, Dance]]", which both became top ten hits on the US [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]] and eventually sold over two million downloads each.<ref name="US-singles" /><ref name="Yahoo 07-10-2013">{{cite news |url=http://music.yahoo.com/blogs/chart-watch/week-ending-july-7-2013-songs-actress-top-213747613.html |title=Week Ending July 7, 2013. Songs: The Actress in the Top 10 |author=Paul Grein |work=Chart Watch |publisher=Yahoo |date=July 10, 2013 }}</ref>

Fall Out Boy's third studio album ''[[Infinity on High]]'' was released in February 2007, debuting at number one on the ''Billboard'' 200 with 260,000 first week sales and being certified triple platinum by the RIAA.<ref name="US-albums"/><ref name="RIAA"/> "[[This Ain't a Scene, It's an Arms Race]]", the album's lead single, peaked at number two on the ''Billboard'' Hot 100 and also hit the top ten in countries such as Canada, New Zealand and the United Kingdom.<ref name="US-singles"/><ref name="CAN-singles"/><ref name="NZ"/><ref name="UK-singles"/> The second single commissioned, "[[Thnks fr th Mmrs]]", peaked at number eleven on the Hot 100 and sold 3 million downloads.<ref name="US-singles"/><ref name="Yahoo 07-10-2013"/> ''Infinity on High'' also produced the singles "[["The Take Over, the Breaks Over"]]" and "[[I'm Like a Lawyer with the Way I'm Always Trying to Get You Off (Me & You)]]". The band released their fourth studio album ''[[Folie à Deux (album)|Folie à Deux]]'' in December 2008; it debuted at number eight on the ''Billboard'' 200 with 149,000 first week sales and was certified Platinum by the RIAA.<ref name="US-albums"/><ref name="RIAA"/> Four singles were released from ''Folie à Deux'' – the most successful of these was the platinum-certified "[[I Don't Care (Fall Out Boy song)|I Don't Care]]", which peaked at number 21 on the Hot 100.<ref name="US-singles"/>

Fall Out Boy released the compilation album ''[[Believers Never Die – Greatest Hits]]'' in November 2009. Following the album's release, the band announced that they would be going on an indefinite hiatus.<ref>{{cite magazine | url=http://music-mix.ew.com/2009/11/03/pete-wentz-band-hero-tattoo-bronx-ashlee-simpson/ | title=Pete Wentz: A Music Mix Q&A on 'Band Hero,' lost-bet tattoos and why Fall Out Boy isn't breaking up | magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]] | date=November 3, 2009 | access-date=March 1, 2013 | author=Lynch, Joseph Brannigan}}</ref> The group announced the end of their hiatus four years later, releasing the single "[[My Songs Know What You Did in the Dark (Light Em Up)]]"; it peaked at number thirteen on the ''Billboard'' Hot 100 and went nine-times platinum.<ref name="US-singles"/> Their fifth studio album ''[[Save Rock and Roll]]'' was released on April 16, 2013. It debuted at number one on the ''Billboard'' 200 with 154,000 first week sales.<ref name="US-albums" /> ''[[PAX AM Days]]'', a punk and hardcore-influenced EP, followed later in the year on October 15. "[[Centuries (song)|Centuries]]" was released as the sixth album's lead single in September 2014, peaking on the Hot 100 at number 10 and being Diamond. Sixth album ''[[American Beauty/American Psycho]]'', released in January 2015, became the band's third ''Billboard'' 200 number one, with 192,000 first week sales. On January 19, 2018, their seventh studio album, ''[[Mania (Fall Out Boy album)|Mania]]'', was released and became their third ''Billboard 200'' number one in a row and their fourth overall.<ref name="US-albums"/> Their eighth studio album ''[[So Much (for) Stardust]]'' was released on March 24, 2023.
