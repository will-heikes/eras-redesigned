French [[electronic music]] duo [[Daft Punk]] released four [[studio album]]s, two [[live album]]s, one [[compilation album]], one [[soundtrack album]], five [[remix album]]s, two [[video album]]s, twenty-two [[Single (music)|single]]s and twenty-four [[music video]]s. Group members [[Thomas Bangalter]] and [[Guy-Manuel de Homem-Christo]] met in 1987 while studying at the [[Lycée Carnot]] secondary school.<ref name="Bio">{{cite web | url=http://sites.radiofrance.fr/franceinter/em/electronlibre/index.php?id=60730 | title=Daft Punk | publisher=[[Radio France Internationale]] | date=3 December 2007 | access-date=9 August 2013 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090729041050/http://sites.radiofrance.fr/franceinter/em/electronlibre/index.php?id=60730 | archive-date=29 July 2009 | language=fr}}</ref> They subsequently recorded several demo tracks together, forming Daft Punk in 1993.<ref name="Bio"/> Their debut single "The New Wave" was released the following year on the [[Soma Quality Recordings]] label.<ref name="Bio"/> Daft Punk first found commercial success with the release of their second single "[[Da Funk]]", which peaked at number seven in France and topped the United States ''[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]'' [[Hot Dance Club Songs|Hot Dance Club Play]] chart.<ref name="FRA"/><ref name="US-Dance-singles">Peak chart positions for singles on the Hot Dance Club Songs chart in the United States:
* All except "Lose Yourself to Dance": {{cite magazine | url=https://www.billboard.com/artist/daft-punk/chart-history/dsi/ | title=Daft Punk – Chart History: Dance Club Songs | magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]] | publisher=[[Prometheus Global Media]] | access-date=24 September 2012 | archive-date=17 November 2021 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211117173333/https://www.billboard.com/artist/daft-punk/chart-history/dsi/ | url-status=live }}
* "Lose Yourself to Dance": {{cite magazine | url=http://www.billboard.com/charts/2013-11-02/dance-club-play-songs | title=Dance Club Songs: November 2, 2013 | magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]] | publisher=[[Prometheus Global Media]] | access-date=31 January 2014 | archive-date=2014-10-13 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141013121506/http://www.billboard.com/charts/2013-11-02/dance-club-play-songs | url-status=live }}</ref>

After signing to [[Virgin Records]], the duo released their debut studio album ''[[Homework (Daft Punk album)|Homework]]'' in January 1997.<ref name="AmazonHomework"/> The album peaked at number three in France, earning a platinum certification from the [[SNEP|Syndicat National de l'Édition Phonographique]] (SNEP).<ref name="FRA"/><ref name="Infodisc"/> The success of ''Homework'' also brought worldwide attention to [[Progressive electronic dance music|progressive house]] music,<ref>{{cite web | url=http://pitchfork.com/features/staff-lists/5923-top-100-albums-of-the-1990s/4/ | title=Staff Lists: Top 100 Albums of the 1990s | publisher=[[Pitchfork Media]] | date=17 November 2003 | access-date=9 August 2013 | author=Carr, Erick | archive-date=24 March 2016 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160324114606/https://pitchfork.com/features/lists-and-guides/5923-top-100-albums-of-the-1990s/?page=4 | url-status=dead }}</ref> and it has since been viewed as a landmark album of the genre.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Mullins |first1=Ellie |title=Daft Punk iconic album 'Homework' turns 23 years old |url=https://weraveyou.com/2020/01/daft-punk-iconic-album-homework-turns-23-years-old/ |website=We Rave You |access-date=29 March 2020 |date=18 January 2020 |archive-date=29 March 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200329235115/https://weraveyou.com/2020/01/daft-punk-iconic-album-homework-turns-23-years-old/ |url-status=live }}</ref> The ''Homework'' single "[[Around the World (Daft Punk song)|Around the World]]" became a top five hit in several European countries and gave the duo their first entry on the US [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]], where it peaked at number 61.<ref name="FRA"/><ref name="US-singles"/> The album also produced the singles "[[Burnin' (instrumental)|Burnin']]" and "[[Revolution 909]]".

Daft Punk released their second studio album ''[[Discovery (Daft Punk album)|Discovery]]'' in February 2001. The album peaked at number two in France and attained a triple platinum certification from the SNEP.<ref name="FRA"/><ref name="SNEP"/> ''Discovery'' also performed well internationally, becoming a top ten chart hit in countries such as Australia, Canada and the United Kingdom.<ref name="AUS-albums"/><ref name="CAN-albums"/><ref name="UK-albums"/> "[[One More Time (Daft Punk song)|One More Time]]", the album's first single, became a number-one hit in France and on the ''Billboard'' dance chart.<ref name="FRA"/><ref name="US-Dance-singles"/> The single also reached the top ten on the charts of Australia, Germany and the United Kingdom.<ref name="AUS-singles"/><ref name="GER"/><ref name="UK-singles"/> An additional six singles were released from ''Discovery'': "[[Aerodynamic (instrumental)|Aerodynamic]]", "[[Digital Love (Daft Punk song)|Digital Love]]", "[[Harder, Better, Faster, Stronger]]", "[[Face to Face (Daft Punk song)|Face to Face]]" and "[[Something About Us (Daft Punk song)|Something About Us]]". Daft Punk also oversaw the release of ''[[Interstella 5555: The 5tory of the 5ecret 5tar 5ystem]]'', a 2003 film featuring tracks from ''Discovery'' as its soundtrack.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/films/2003/10/13/interstella_5555_the_5tory_of_the_5ecret_5tar_5system_2003_review.shtml | title=Interstella 5555: The 5tory of the 5ecret 5tar 5system (2003) | publisher=[[BBC]] | date=13 October 2003 | access-date=9 August 2013 | author=Russell, Jamie | archive-date=19 April 2005 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20050419000633/http://www.bbc.co.uk/films/2003/10/13/interstella_5555_the_5tory_of_the_5ecret_5tar_5system_2003_review.shtml | url-status=live }}</ref> ''[[Human After All]]'', the duo's third studio album, was released in March 2005 to mixed reviews.<ref>{{cite web | url=http://www.metacritic.com/music/human-after-all/daft-punk | title=Human After All – Daft Punk | work=[[Metacritic]] | publisher=[[CBS Interactive]] | access-date=9 August 2013 | archive-date=30 April 2013 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130430072540/http://www.metacritic.com/music/human-after-all/daft-punk | url-status=live }}</ref> Nonetheless, the album topped the ''Billboard'' [[Dance/Electronic Albums]] chart and peaked at number three in France.<ref name="FRA"/><ref name="US-Dance-albums"/> ''Human After All'' featured the singles "[[Robot Rock (song)|Robot Rock]]", "[[Technologic]]", "[[Human After All (song)|Human After All]]" and "[[The Prime Time of Your Life]]".

Following their [[Alive 2006/2007]] tour, Daft Punk composed the score for the 2010 film ''[[Tron: Legacy]]'' and released an accompanying [[Tron: Legacy (soundtrack)|soundtrack album]]. The album produced the single "[[Derezzed]]" and became the duo's first top five album on the [[Billboard 200|''Billboard'' 200]].<ref name="FRA"/><ref name="US-albums"/> It also received a platinum and gold certifications from the [[Recording Industry Association of America]] (RIAA) and SNEP, respectively.<ref name="Infodisc"/><ref name="RIAA"/> ''[[Random Access Memories]]'', Daft Punk's first studio album in eight years, was released in May 2013 and topped several charts worldwide.<ref>{{cite web | url=http://lescharts.com/showitem.asp?interpret=Daft+Punk&titel=Random+Access+Memories&cat=a | title=Daft Punk – Random Access Memories | work=lescharts.com | publisher=Hung Medien | access-date=9 August 2013 | archive-date=13 June 2013 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130613203851/https://lescharts.com/showitem.asp?interpret=Daft+Punk&titel=Random+Access+Memories&cat=a | url-status=live }}</ref> In France, the album was Daft Punk's first release to debut at number one,<ref name="FRA"/> a position it retained for three consecutive weeks after its release.<ref>{{cite web |last=Decant |first=Charles |url=http://www.chartsinfrance.net/actualite/news-86419.html |title=Top Albums : Jenifer s'incline face à Daft Punk mais devance Johnny Hallyday |language=fr |publisher=Charts in France |date=14 June 2013 |access-date=19 April 2020 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130618031453/http://www.chartsinfrance.net/actualite/news-86419.html |archive-date=18 June 2013 }}</ref> "[[Get Lucky (Daft Punk song)|Get Lucky]]", the first single from ''Random Access Memories'', also experienced chart success in several countries.<ref>{{cite web | url=http://lescharts.com/showitem.asp?interpret=Daft+Punk+feat.+Pharrell+Williams&titel=Get+Lucky&cat=s | title=Daft Punk feat.
