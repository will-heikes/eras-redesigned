"'''This Is How We Do'''"  is a song by American singer [[Katy Perry]] from her fourth studio album, ''[[Prism (Katy Perry album)|Prism]]'' (2013). Perry co-wrote the track with its producers [[Max Martin]] and [[Klas Åhlund]] and recorded it in [[Stockholm, Sweden]]. The song was released as the album's fifth and final single along with its music video on July 31, 2014. "This Is How We Do" is a [[dance-pop]] song influenced by [[hip hop music|hip hop]], having "synth squiggles" and "melodic dots" as its main instrumentation. Lyrically, it has Perry sing-talking about her hangout routine with her friends. The song's official remix featuring American rapper [[Riff Raff (rapper)|Riff Raff]] was released on August 25, 2014.

Commercially, the song reached the top 10 in Canada and Israel while peaking within the top 20 in Australia, Austria, the Czech Republic, Lebanon, the Netherlands, New Zealand, and Slovakia as well as the top 30 in Finland, Scotland, Sweden, the United States, and Venezuela. Joel Kefali directed the song's accompanying music video, which was released on July 31, 2014. It features the singer enjoying summer and having fun with her friends. The song was performed on Perry's [[Prismatic World Tour]], and appeared on the Prismatic World Tour concert film [[The Prismatic World Tour Live|''The Prismatic World Tour Live'']].

== Composition ==

"This Is How We Do" is a [[dance-pop]] song,<ref>{{cite magazine |first= Jason |last= Lipshutz |title= 15 Recent Pop Songs That Weren't Released as Singles (But Should Have Been) |date= April 24, 2015 |magazine= [[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]] |url= https://www.billboard.com/music/pop/15-awesome-pop-non-singles-lady-gaga-justin-bieber-miley-cyrus-6545946/ |access-date= June 15, 2015 |archive-date= May 31, 2015 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20150531034155/http://www.billboard.com/articles/columns/pop-shop/6545946/15-awesome-pop-non-singles-lady-gaga-justin-bieber-miley-cyrus |url-status= live }}</ref> and was written by Perry with producers [[Max Martin]] and [[Klas Åhlund]], who were also responsible for [[programming (music)|programming]] of the song, while Perry also provided background vocals. It is set in the time signature of [[common time]] and has a moderate [[Hip hop music|hip hop]] tempo of 96 [[beats per minute]]. The song is written in the key of [[A minor]], and Perry's vocals span from the low note of A<sub>3</sub> to the high note of C<sub>6</sub>.<ref name="sheet">{{cite web|title=Digital sheet music – Katy Perry – This Is How We Do|date = February 24, 2014|url=http://www.musicnotes.com/sheetmusic/mtdFPE.asp?ppn=MN0130460|access-date=July 24, 2014|publisher=Musicnotes.com. [[Alfred Publishing]]}}</ref> The song has been described as a "wobbling [[dance music|dance]] track",<ref name=albumpreview/> with hip-hop underpinnings<ref name=usatoday/> and "faux-urban west coast pose".<ref name=observer/> Randall Roberts of ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' remarked that the song is "replete with synth squiggles and melodic dots—slowed and chopped."<ref name=latimes>{{cite news|last1=Roberts|first1=Randall|title=Review: Hits pack Katy Perry's 'Prism'|url=http://www.latimes.com/entertainment/music/posts/la-et-ms-katy-perry-prism-review-20131022-story.html|access-date=July 25, 2014|work=[[Los Angeles Times]]|date=October 22, 2013|archive-date=July 20, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140720122115/http://www.latimes.com/entertainment/music/posts/la-et-ms-katy-perry-prism-review-20131022-story.html|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Kitty Empire]] of ''[[The Observer]]'' claimed that the song is "a sequel of sorts to both '[[California Gurls]]', and '[[Last Friday Night (T.G.I.F.)]]', Perry's previous party-hearty mega-hits."<ref name=observer>{{cite news|author-link=Kitty Empire|last=Empire|first=Kitty|title=Katy Perry: Prism – review|url=https://www.theguardian.com/music/2013/oct/20/katy-perry-prism-review|access-date=July 24, 2014|work=[[The Observer]]|date=October 20, 2013|archive-date=September 14, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140914110851/http://www.theguardian.com/music/2013/oct/20/katy-perry-prism-review|url-status=live}}</ref>

Lyrically, "This Is How We Do" finds Perry sing-talking about her hangout routine with her friends,<ref name=popmatters/> also encouraging her fans to spend money they don't have just so they can have a good time, as noted by [[Stephen Thomas Erlewine]] of [[AllMusic]].<ref name="allmusic"/> Empire claimed that on the song, Perry and her friends "are on the prowl for tacos and "hotties", "sucking really bad at [[Mariah Carey]]-oke".<ref name=observer/> While also claiming that the song recalls her own 'Last Friday Night (T.G.I.F.)', Ben Ratliff of ''[[The New York Times]]'' claimed that its lyrics are "more modest, adult, and middle-class idea of fun—tacos, karaoke, and "gettin' our nails did, all Japanese-y."<ref name=nytimes>{{cite news|last1=Ratliff|first1=Ben|title=This Bubble Gum, Too, Shall Pop|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2013/10/20/arts/music/this-bubble-gum-too-shall-pop.html?pagewanted=2|newspaper=[[The New York Times]]|date=October 18, 2013|access-date=February 28, 2017|archive-date=February 2, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170202010955/http://www.nytimes.com/2013/10/20/arts/music/this-bubble-gum-too-shall-pop.html?pagewanted=2|url-status=live}}</ref> The song also has a repetitive bridge where Perry repeats, "This is no big deal", an outro where Perry asks to "bring the beat back",<ref name=billboardtrackbytrack>{{cite magazine|last1=Lipshut|first1=Jason|title=Katy Perry's 'PRISM': Track-By-Track Review|url=http://www.billboard.com/articles/review/5763162/katy-perrys-prism-track-by-track-review|access-date=July 25, 2014|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|date=October 21, 2013|archive-date=October 23, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161023174051/http://www.billboard.com/articles/review/5763162/katy-perrys-prism-track-by-track-review|url-status=live}}</ref> while Perry also gives a shout-out to her diehards: "This one goes out to the ladies at breakfast ... in last night's dress. Uh-huh. I see you," she says.<ref>{{cite news|last1=Reed|first1=James|title=Katy Perry shows many colors on 'Prism'|url=https://www.bostonglobe.com/arts/music/2013/10/20/perry-shows-many-colors-prism/fIMiSxHkP3V3r4yN5Z8r4H/story.html|access-date=July 25, 2014|newspaper=[[The Boston Globe]]|date=October 21, 2013|archive-date=January 25, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140125095113/http://www.bostonglobe.com/arts/music/2013/10/20/perry-shows-many-colors-prism/fIMiSxHkP3V3r4yN5Z8r4H/story.html|url-status=live}}</ref>

== Chart performance ==
[[File:Katy Perry - The Prismatic 24.jpg|thumb|upright|Perry performing "This Is How We Do" in Glasgow]]
"This Is How We Do" first charted on South Korea's [[Gaon Chart|Gaon Download Chart]] and the [[Canadian Hot 100]] when its parent album was released, debuting at number 73, with sales of 2,263 copies, and number 88, respectively.<ref name="can"/><ref name=sksales/> When it was released as a single, the song charted moderately throughout the world. In Canada, where the song had previously charted, the song achieved more success, reaching number nine, becoming the fourth top-10 single from the album, and her 16th overall.<ref name="can"/> In the United States, after the video's release, the song debuted at number 88 on the [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]] chart of August 16, 2014. The song eventually peaked at number 24, becoming the sixth song from ''Prism'' to reach the top 40.<ref>{{Cite magazine|url=http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/6207100/hot-100-magic-rude-bang-bang-meghan-trainor|magazine=Billboard|title=MAGIC! Maintains No. 1 Perch on Hot 100; 'Bang Bang,' Meghan Trainor Soar Into Top 10|first=Gary|last=Trust|date=August 6, 2014|access-date=August 6, 2014|archive-date=November 21, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151121211406/http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/6207100/hot-100-magic-rude-bang-bang-meghan-trainor|url-status=live}}</ref> On the [[Pop Songs]] chart, it debuted at number 37<ref>{{cite magazine|last1=Trust|first1=Gary|title=Chart Highlights: Sam Smith Settles In For A No. 1 'Stay' On Adult Contemporary|url=https://www.billboard.com/pro/chart-highlights-sam-smith-settles-in-for-a-no-1-stay-on-adult/|access-date=August 12, 2014|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|date=August 11, 2014|archive-date=August 15, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140815022034/http://www.billboard.com/articles/columns/chart-beat/6214363/chart-highlights-sam-smith-settles-in-for-a-no-1-stay-on-adult|url-status=live}}</ref> and peaked at number 15; her second of her career to miss the top-10 and first in five years.<ref name="pop"/> On the other hand, on the [[Hot Dance Club Songs]] chart, the song peaked at number-one, further extending her record of most consecutive songs to top the "Dance Club" chart to 15.<ref>{{cite magazine|last1=Trust|first1=Gary|title=Chart Highlights: Tove Lo's 'Habits' Hits No. 1 'High' on Pop Songs|url=https://www.billboard.com/pro/chart-highlights-tove-los-habits-hits-no-1-high-on-pop-songs/|access-date=November 3, 2014|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|date=November 3, 2014|archive-date=May 13, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160513072117/http://www.billboard.com/articles/columns/chart-beat/6304431/chart-highlights-tove-los-habits-hits-no-1-high-on-pop-songs|url-status=live}}</ref> The song has sold more than 2,000,000 copies in the United States; consequently it was certified double Platinum by the [[Recording Industry Association of America]] (RIAA).<ref name=RIAA/>
 
In New Zealand, the song debuted at number 20, reaching its peak position the following week, number 13, where it remained for a further week, becoming her highest charting single since "[[Dark Horse (Katy Perry song)|Dark Horse]]".<ref name=nz/> In Australia, it debuted at number 27 and peaked at number 18, three weeks later, becoming her 15th top-20 single.<ref name="aus"/> In Austria and Netherlands, while peaking at numbers 14 and 20, respectively, the song was also the third highest charting single after "Roar" and "Dark Horse".<ref name=aust/><ref name=dutch/> In France, however, "This Is How We Do" and "[[Birthday (Katy Perry song)|Birthday]]", the album's last two singles, were the only two tracks of the album to miss the top-40.<ref name="fra"/> In the United Kingdom, the song peaked at number 33 on the [[UK Singles Chart]].<ref name="ukkaty">{{cite web|title=Katy Perry &#124; Official Charts|url=http://www.officialcharts.com/artist/_/katy%20perry/|website=[[The Official Charts Company]]|access-date=October 13, 2014|archive-date=July 9, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140709051059/http://www.officialcharts.com/artist/_/katy%20perry/|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Music video==

=== Production and synopsis ===
On July 24, 2014, a lyric video for the song was uploaded to YouTube.<ref>{{cite web|title=Katy Perry Does the Ultimate #tbt in Lyric Video for 'This Is How We Do': Watch|url=http://ksfm.cbslocal.com/2014/07/24/katy-perry-does-the-ultimate-tbt-in-lyric-video-for-this-is-how-we-do-watch/|publisher=CBS-Local|access-date=August 11, 2014|date=July 24, 2014|archive-date=October 23, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141023211454/http://ksfm.cbslocal.com/2014/07/24/katy-perry-does-the-ultimate-tbt-in-lyric-video-for-this-is-how-we-do-watch/|url-status=live}}</ref> The official music video for the song, directed by [[New Zealand]] filmmaker Joel Kefali and produced during a three-day studio shoot in [[Los Angeles]], was released on July 31, 2014.<ref name="music-video-director-3">{{cite web|url=http://www.stuff.co.nz/entertainment/music/10342078/Katy-Perry-eats-out-of-Kiwi-directors-hands|title=Katy Perry eats out of Kiwi director's hands|last=Duff|first=Michelle|publisher=[[Stuff.co.nz]]|date=August 4, 2014|access-date=August 12, 2014|archive-date=August 9, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140809090541/http://www.stuff.co.nz/entertainment/music/10342078/Katy-Perry-eats-out-of-Kiwi-directors-hands|url-status=live}}</ref> According to the director, he was trying to "fully interpret the pop nature of Katy Perry, because [he] hadn't done any straight down the line pop videos before. [His] idea was to make a pop video but to keep some edge to it, and make it feel like it was [[pop art]]... We did a studio-based shoot where we used lots of animation and props, and then each line of the song had its own little vignette. I wanted to do something that was close to the work I'd already done. I like mixed media, jumping between live action and animation, and I focus more on design."<ref name="music-video-director-3"/>

The music video opens with a visual of an old man staring at a painting, which turns out to be Perry.<ref>{{cite news|title=Katy Perry Drops Trippy 'This Is How We Do' Music Video: Watch|url=http://www.ryanseacrest.com/2014/07/31/katy-perry-drops-trippy-this-is-how-we-do-music-video-watch/|access-date=August 12, 2014|work=[[Ryan Seacrest]]|date=July 31, 2014|archive-date=August 13, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140813124900/http://www.ryanseacrest.com/2014/07/31/katy-perry-drops-trippy-this-is-how-we-do-music-video-watch/|url-status=live}}</ref> The video features Perry, accompanied by model Aglae Kounkou and dancer Leah Adler, in front of various backdrops, including a kitchen, a [[ping pong]] table, a bathtub, a [[three-dimensional|3D]] version of [[Piet Mondrian]]'s ''[[Composition with Red Blue and Yellow]]'', and an ice cream truck. The video also includes a myriad of male dancers in grey suits and red [[bowtie]]s, who also move furniture and props around. A scene during the bridge of the song shows various young adults in midair while food flies around them, and also features Perry on a [[Skype]] call with someone named "Jessica Thot". The video also features various animated cartoon versions of [[ice cream]], [[pizza]], and [[watermelon]], which are all shown dancing at the end of the video.
